Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a straightforward DLA product solicitation for a single NSN item (Interface Unit, Auto, CAGE 1PXG6, part 1771-IAD/D) — a physical hardware buy, not a services or IT contract. The compliance-over-outcomes sin is fundamentally a services-contract pathology: it manifests when recurring reporting and process surveillance crowd out performance metrics and user-outcome measures. In a product acquisition of this type, the accountability structure is appropriately outcome-oriented by design: the item either conforms to its technical specification and packaging requirements or it does not. The solicitation's QA section (Section E) is brief and conventional — contractor self-inspection, government reservation of inspection rights, and a 365-day records retention requirement. There are no weekly status reports, no prescribed report formats, no recurring meetings, no plan-adherence tracking, and no CPMP or project schedule against which deviations must be reported. The supply chain traceability documentation requirement (C03) and the surplus property representation (C04) are pre-award and delivery-time documentation obligations that serve authentication and anti-counterfeit purposes — legitimate outcome-supporting requirements, not compliance theater. Product Verification Testing (E05) is conditionally invoked and, when applied, tests whether the physical item performs as specified — a direct outcome measure. The evaluation criterion is Lowest Price Technically Acceptable (LPTA), which is pass/fail against technical conformance — again outcome-focused. There is nothing in this solicitation resembling the overlapping reporting cadences, prescribed status-report sections, or process-surveillance mechanisms that characterize a severity-2 or severity-3 finding. Severity 1 is appropriate.

It supports traceability and post-delivery audit rather than substituting for outcome evaluation. No reporting cadence, prescribed format, or recurring status meeting is attached to it.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation exhibits moderate insider rewards characteristics driven primarily by the combination of (1) a single approved design CAGE code pointing to a specific distributor rather than a manufacturer, (2) an authorized-distributor-only award restriction, and (3) upfront traceability documentation requirements within an 8-day response window. Together, these requirements create a supply channel barrier that is controlled not by the government but by a private commercial intermediary (Schaedler Yesco). A vendor that can source and deliver the identical item but is not currently an authorized Schaedler Yesco distributor cannot compete — regardless of their capability. This is not the most severe form of insider rewards. The solicitation does not require years of prior federal contracting experience, specific dollar thresholds, or multiple prior contracts. The requirements are grounded in legitimate concerns — supply chain integrity, counterfeit avoidance, and product traceability for military hardware — which are reasonable in the defense logistics context. DLA procurement notes C03 and L04 are standard DLA instruments with genuine quality rationales. However, the practical effect of the authorized-distributor restriction, combined with an 8-day window that makes it impossible for a new entrant to obtain distributor authorization in time, tilts the playing field toward whoever already holds that distributor relationship. This is a moderate insider rewards concern — not a closed competition, but a structurally tilted one. Severity 2 is appropriate.
Evidence Quotes
Consideration for award of contract shall be given only to authorized distributors of the original manufacturer's item represented in this solicitation. If you desire to be considered as a potential source for award of this contract, proof of being an authorized distributor shall be provided on company letterhead signed by a responsible company official and sent with your offer to the Procurement Contracting Officer.
This requirement restricts award to vendors who are already authorized distributors of the OEM (Schaedler Yesco Distribution, CAGE 1PXG6). A new entrant who is not yet an authorized distributor — even one fully capable of sourcing and delivering the item — cannot compete unless they first obtain distributor authorization, which is controlled entirely by the OEM. This effectively gives the OEM and its existing authorized channel exclusive access to the award.
Any source interested in supplying this item who is not the Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) must provide complete traceability information tracing material back to the OEM when offer is submitted. Failure to provide this information may render your offer as non-responsive.
While supply chain traceability is a legitimate anti-counterfeiting measure, requiring it to be provided at time of offer submission — rather than at delivery — creates a practical barrier for distributors who do not already have an established supply relationship with this specific OEM. Vendors without a pre-existing channel relationship with Schaedler Yesco (CAGE 1PXG6) cannot obtain this documentation in time for the 8-day response window, functionally limiting competition to those already in the supply chain.
Approved Design Cage Code: Schaedler Yesco Distribution (CAGE 1PXG6)
The solicitation designates a single approved design CAGE code — a distributor, not a manufacturer. This is notable: the approved source is itself a distributor rather than the OEM. This means competition is anchored to a single commercial intermediary's authorization structure, concentrating supply channel control and limiting who can realistically respond.
Offers of alternate product will not be evaluated for the contract action if: (1) The solicitation is automated; (2) It does not meet the dollar threshold for savings, after an evaluation factor of $600 is applied for coordination with each ESA; or (3) When the time proposed for award does not permit evaluation and delay of award would adversely affect the Government.
The evaluation framework for alternate products contains multiple exit ramps that allow the government to decline evaluation of non-incumbent supply sources. Combined with the 8-day response window and the upfront traceability requirement, this reinforces a structural preference for the established supply channel over new entrants who might offer equivalent product through alternative sources.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a simple one-unit spare parts buy for a single line item (NSN 7025-01-469-6648, an Allen-Bradley 1771-IAD/D Interface Unit) procured by DLA Maritime Mechanicsburg. The nature of this procurement fundamentally limits the applicability of requirement sprawl as a sin: there is no statement of work, no service delivery, no staffing structure, and no workflow being prescribed. The solicitation defines WHAT the government needs to receive — a specific part number from an approved source, packaged to military standards, traceable through the supply chain — rather than HOW a vendor should organize its internal operations to produce or deliver that item. The prescriptive elements present — MIL-STD-2073 packaging, MIL-STD-130 marking, supply chain traceability documentation, IUID requirements under DFARS 252.211-7003, and the surplus property representation form — are all legitimate constraints driven by DoD logistics system requirements, counterfeit part prevention, and statutory/regulatory obligations. None of them foreclose vendor innovation on the core delivery question (where to source the part, how to manufacture it if they are the OEM, how to price it competitively). A distributor or the OEM alike can respond to this solicitation without being constrained to a particular internal process. The solicitation also explicitly uses LPTA evaluation criteria and leaves open the possibility of alternate product offers (via DLATERML04), which further confirms the government is focused on outcomes (receiving a conforming part at the lowest price) rather than mandating a particular production or delivery methodology. Overall, this is a competently scoped parts procurement solicitation. The length and density of the document reflects DLA's standard boilerplate clause library — not organizational dysfunction or sprawl. Severity is 1.
Evidence Quotes
3.2 Marking - This item shall be physically identified in accordance with <MIL-STD-130, REV N, 16 NOV 2012>.
This is a military marking standard reference, which is a legitimate technical interoperability and identification constraint, not prescriptive process sprawl. It defines a required standard outcome (proper identification marking) rather than dictating how a manufacturer organizes its production process.

The detailed packaging requirements (MIL-STD-2073, packing levels, palletization, DLR labels, etc.) are prescriptive in the sense that they specify exact standards. However, for a military spare parts procurement, these are legitimate safety, logistics, and interoperability constraints driven by the military distribution system's operational requirements — not process sprawl in the acquisition sense. The government has genuine reasons (fleet readiness, shelf life, counterfeit prevention) for each of these standards.
Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation exhibits moderate rule pool characteristics, primarily driven by copy-forward template accumulation rather than any single egregious inclusion of irrelevant or contradictory regulations. The core solicitation is for a single unit of a commercial PLC interface module (Allen-Bradley 1771-IAD/D) — a commodity electronics purchase from an identified approved source. Against that simple scope, the document runs 32 pages and incorporates: a multi-page packaging standard block (WSSTERMDZ03) referencing a dozen distinct standards; a three-page IUID clause with unpopulated threshold tables; a four-page FASCSA clause reproduced in triplicate with all agency-selection fields blank; a two-page Government surplus property checklist of uncertain applicability; and a document-ordering reference section that exists despite the explicit statement that no technical data is available. None of these inclusions are individually inexplicable for DLA maritime procurement templates, and the FAR/DFARS clauses cited in Section I are standard and appropriate. However, several clauses contain unfilled placeholder fields ('<>') that indicate the template was not fully tailored to this specific action. The most notable issues are: (1) the FASCSA clause reproduced with all three versions simultaneously, leaving vendors unable to determine which applies; (2) the IUID clause with blank CLIN tables, leaving the $5,000 threshold applicability unresolved; and (3) the document-ordering infrastructure block carried forward despite acknowledged absence of technical data. This is not a severe rule pool — there is no contradictory regulation, no absurdly irrelevant policy, and the total regulatory citation count is moderate rather than extreme. The regulations cited are at least generically applicable to DLA materiel procurement. But the pattern of incomplete template population and unreviewd boilerplate carryover elevates this above minimal. Severity 2 is appropriate: the compliance burden is elevated relative to scope, some provisions appear to be unconsidered carryovers, but the list is not impenetrable and experienced defense suppliers will recognize the template pattern.

This is an uncurated copy-forward insertion of a lengthy regulatory block without completing the clause's own instructions, which defeats its purpose and adds interpretive burden without adding clarity.

The Government surplus property procurement note occupies nearly two full pages and includes a comprehensive 9-section questionnaire about material provenance. The note itself acknowledges it 'only applies to offers of Government surplus material,' making its inclusion either prophylactic boilerplate or an uncurated carryover. Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a DLA supply solicitation for a single unit of a discrete, part-numbered hardware item (Interface Unit, Auto, NSN 7025-01-469-6648, part number 1771-IAD/D). The contract type is firm-fixed-price, as evidenced by the unit price/amount structure, the LPTA evaluation method, FAR 52.246-2 Inspection of Supplies--Fixed-Price clause inclusion, and the FOB Origin delivery clause. A firm-fixed-price contract is entirely appropriate here. The requirement is maximally well-defined: a specific NSN, a specific approved CAGE code and part number, a specific quantity (1 EA), a specific delivery window (30 days), and a specific ship-to address. There is no ambiguity in scope, no variable labor content, and no research or services component. The government is simply buying a known hardware item from a known approved source. FFP is the textbook correct contract type for this acquisition. There is no square peg concern.
