Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a software subscription renewal (SailPoint IdentityIQ) procured via LPTA methodology. It is a commercial product acquisition, not a services contract with ongoing performance obligations or project execution. The contract structure is straightforward: vendor delivers licensed software subscriptions and support for defined periods; FDIC accepts or rejects delivery within 15 business days. The compliance-over-outcomes sin is most damaging in services contracts where elaborate reporting regimes substitute for genuine outcome accountability — where progress reports, weekly status calls, and plan-adherence tracking crowd out any measurement of whether the work actually achieves its purpose. That pattern is entirely absent here. The solicitation contains no reporting cadences, no prescribed report formats, no meeting frequency requirements, no project management plan tracking, and no QA surveillance plans. Section C (Description/Specifications/Work Statement) has no substantive content beyond a single reference clause (7.3.2-34, Duty to Deliver or Perform). There is no SOW or PWS imposing performance monitoring obligations. The absence of performance metrics is not a finding here — this is a COTS subscription renewal. The 'outcome' is binary: is the software licensed and operational for the subscription period? The invoice-upon-delivery structure and 15-day acceptance window are appropriate for that purpose. Requiring SLAs or user satisfaction metrics for a software license renewal would be category errors. The only clauses that touch on quality assurance (7.6.4-01 Inspection and Acceptance) are standard boilerplate appropriate to a commercial purchase. They do not impose compliance theater. Overall, this solicitation shows no meaningful evidence of the compliance-over-outcomes sin. Severity is 1.

This is a standard inspection clause (7.6.4-01) for a commercial software subscription. This is a binary acceptance/rejection mechanism tied to delivery of the licensed software subscription. It is outcome-adjacent — it asks whether the product was actually delivered — not a process-compliance check. For a software subscription renewal, this is a reasonable acceptance standard.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a software subscription renewal — by its nature, a transaction that strongly favors the incumbent. The structure is not egregious by the standards of the worst insider-rewards solicitations: it does not impose dollar thresholds for prior contracts, does not require years of federal-specific experience, and does not award evaluation points for incumbency proxies. The evaluation methodology is LPTA, and the technical acceptability criteria are primarily about authorization status (reseller certification) and completeness of the price schedule rather than demonstrated past performance. However, the solicitation does exhibit moderate insider-rewards characteristics through a combination of factors. First, the mandatory SailPoint authorized reseller/partner requirement is a legitimate screen for a brand-specific renewal, but it limits competition to vendors already in SailPoint's channel ecosystem. Second — and more problematic — the mandatory HPE Aruba partnership requirement appears disconnected from the actual deliverable (SailPoint IdentityIQ software). Its presence as a mandatory disqualifying criterion for a SailPoint-only procurement is unexplained and stacks a second vendor-specific authorization barrier. Third, the 4-day response window after amendments, combined with 12 exact part-number line items across three periods, structurally advantages whoever already has pricing relationships in place. A vendor new to this account would need to rapidly obtain SailPoint partner authorization, HPE Aruba partner status, and precise SailPoint SKU pricing while simultaneously assembling SCRM documentation — all in under a week. The cumulative effect tilts the field toward the incumbent or an incumbent-adjacent reseller without rising to the level of a categorical exclusion based on prior contract history or dollar thresholds. Severity 2 is appropriate: the playing field is tilted but not formally closed, and competition among authorized SailPoint channel partners is theoretically possible.
Evidence Quotes
Offeror must provide proof of being an Authorized Reseller/Partner of SailPoint or be the Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM). Proof of status must be provided with the Offeror's Quote. The CO may contact the manufacturer to confirm the Offeror's reseller status.
This requirement limits competition to vendors who have already established a formal reseller/partner relationship with SailPoint. While this is defensible for a brand-specific software renewal, it means any vendor without an existing SailPoint partnership is categorically excluded from competing, regardless of their general IT procurement capability. The pool of eligible vendors is not the full market of government IT resellers — it is only the subset that SailPoint has already approved as partners.
Proof of partnership with HPE Aruba (REQUIRED)
The solicitation description lists 'Proof of partnership with HPE Aruba (REQUIRED)' as a mandatory submission component. This is notable because the underlying requirement is for SailPoint IdentityIQ software, not HPE Aruba products. Requiring HPE Aruba partnership status as a mandatory qualification for a SailPoint software renewal stacks a second vendor-specific authorization requirement on top of the SailPoint requirement, further narrowing the eligible vendor pool. A vendor with a SailPoint authorization but without HPE Aruba partnership status would be disqualified from a solicitation where HPE Aruba is not part of the described deliverables.
Offeror must provide ALL items with matching Part Numbers in Section B - Price Schedule.
The price schedule specifies exact SailPoint part numbers (e.g., SPT-IIQ RNWL, IIQ-Base, IIQ-CM IU, IIQ-PAM-G3, PS-ES 40HR BLK, TERM-IIQ-SDIM-SVM-IU) with precise quantities for 12 line items per period. A vendor must be able to price all items at these exact part numbers to be technically acceptable. This presupposes an established pricing relationship with SailPoint sufficient to obtain quotes at these specific SKUs — something an authorized partner would have readily, but a new entrant would struggle to obtain within the 4-day response window.
Response Window: 4 days
The final response window after two deadline extensions is 4 calendar days (posted April 24, response due April 28). Combined with the mandatory SailPoint partnership proof, HPE Aruba partnership proof, SCRM information, and 12-line-item pricing across three periods, this extremely compressed timeline functionally advantages vendors who already have pricing relationships and documentation in place — i.e., the incumbent or incumbent-adjacent vendors. A qualified newcomer that would need to establish partner relationships, obtain pricing authorization, and compile all required materials would be severely disadvantaged.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a commercial software subscription renewal solicitation, not a services contract with a statement of work. The fundamental structure of the solicitation is a price schedule for named SailPoint software products and maintenance with specific commercial part numbers. There is no SOW or PWS dictating how contractors must perform services, no staffing structure requirements, no step-by-step process mandates, and no workflow prescriptions. Section C (Description/Specifications/Work Statement) contains no substantive content beyond a duty-to-deliver clause, which is appropriate for a commercial product purchase. The prescriptive elements that do exist — specific part numbers, quantities, and vendor authorization requirements — are inherent to the nature of the acquisition: renewing an existing deployed software platform. Requiring the same software (with matching part numbers) for a renewal is a legitimate technical constraint, not sprawl, because the FDIC cannot substitute an alternative identity governance platform mid-deployment without a separate acquisition decision. The 40-hour expert services block is a minor concern but reflects a commercial catalog item, not a bespoke process mandate. The administrative and compliance clauses (SCRM, insurance, invoicing) are standard and protective, not prescriptive about how core work is performed. Overall, this solicitation scores severity 1: it is competently scoped for its procurement type, with requirements that define what must be purchased rather than how a vendor must internally operate.

Offeror must provide ALL items with matching Part Numbers in Section B - Price Schedule. Offeror must provide proof of being an Authorized Reseller/Partner of SailPoint or be the Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM).
The solicitation specifies exact SailPoint part numbers and requires proof of authorized reseller status. While this nominally constrains the solution to a single vendor's product, this is a software subscription renewal for an existing deployed platform — the FDIC already uses SailPoint IdentityIQ. Mandating the same software for a renewal is a legitimate technical interoperability constraint (continuing to operate the existing system), not solution-space sprawl in the traditional sense. It does not prescribe how the vendor must deliver the software or support it.

This prescribes a quantity of effort (40 hours) without articulating what the hours should accomplish, which could be considered a minor form of process prescription. However, given that this is a packaged commercial item with a specific SailPoint part number (PS-ES 40HR BLK), it reflects a commercial catalog purchase rather than a bespoke process mandate — a meaningful distinction from sprawl in a services contract.
Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a software subscription renewal for a single commercially available product (SailPoint IdentityIQ) from a single vendor ecosystem. The compliance apparatus applied to it is drawn substantially from a standard FDIC contract template that was not curated for this acquisition type. The most telling indicators are: (1) bodily injury and automobile liability insurance requirements that have no logical connection to an electronic software subscription; (2) a multi-page Trade Agreements Act clause defining country-of-origin rules for physical manufactured goods, with a certification requirement, applied to a software license; (3) employment reporting and affirmative action clauses that are boilerplate carryovers with no practical bearing on a subscription renewal; and (4) an essentially blank Statement of Work section contrasted with approximately 40 compliance clauses. However, this does not rise to severity 3 because: the regulations cited are at least drawn from legitimate federal procurement law (they are FDIC's standard APGM clauses, not an ad hoc dump); the list is approximately 40 clauses, not 88; many clauses (SAM registration, SCRM, supply chain reporting, information security, EULA governance, disputes) are genuinely relevant to a software IT acquisition; and the solicitation provides full text for the most operationally significant clauses rather than citing everything by reference only. The problem is moderate over-application of a standard template to a narrow commercial transaction, not a catastrophic rule pool. Severity 2 is the appropriate finding.

Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is for a straightforward commercial software subscription renewal of SailPoint IdentityIQ and associated modules. The requirements are fully defined: named products, specific part numbers, fixed quantities (e.g., 20,000 IIQ-CM IU identities, 12,000 SSIQ-FS IU), and a known performance period. There is no ambiguity about scope, no unpredictable labor component, and no technical uncertainty. The FDIC correctly selected firm-fixed-price, which is the standard and appropriate contract type for commercial software license and maintenance renewals. FFP places price risk on the vendor, incentivizes competitive pricing, and matches the delivery model (subscription certificates/keys delivered at contract start). There is no square peg here — the pricing structure aligns well with the nature of the requirement.
