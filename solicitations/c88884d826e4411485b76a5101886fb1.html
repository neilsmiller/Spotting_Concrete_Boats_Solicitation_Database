Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Insufficient Data 0 quotes ▾

⚠ Insufficient content to evaluate this sin — the solicitation lacked a substantive Statement of Work or attachments.

Reasoning
This is a pre-solicitation notice (presolicitation) for a small quantity purchase (2 EA) of a defense aviation part (NSN 7R-5895-017210636-P8, Amplifier-Filter AS). The posting contains no evaluation criteria, no experience requirements, no past performance scoring rubrics, no personnel qualifications, and no SOW/PWS attachments. There is simply no content from which to assess insider rewards in the traditional sense of biased experience or evaluation criteria. That said, the solicitation does invoke FAR 6.302-1 (other than full and open competition — only one responsible source) and explicitly states that only previously Government-approved sources have been solicited, citing engineering source approval requirements, proprietary data rights, and flight-critical part status. This is a structural restriction on competition, but it is categorically distinct from the 'Insider Rewards' sin as defined. Source approval restrictions for flight-critical parts with proprietary technical data are a recognized legal mechanism under FAR and DFARS, not a solicitation-drafting pathology that rewards incumbency through manipulated experience thresholds or evaluation criteria. The restriction is disclosed transparently, the basis (data rights, flight criticality, design control activity approval) is stated, and the notice explicitly invites non-approved sources to submit qualification requests alongside their proposals. This is the opposite of hidden incumbency bias — it is an openly declared, legally grounded limitation. Because the document lacks the evaluation criteria, experience requirements, and scoring rubrics needed to assess Sin #4, sufficient_content is set to false and severity is set to 1.
