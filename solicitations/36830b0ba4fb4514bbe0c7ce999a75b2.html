Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 3 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The solicitation uses Cost-Plus-Fixed-Fee (CPFF), a cost-reimbursement contract type, for professional support services in support of highly complex naval programs — Refueling and Complex Overhaul (RCOH) and Inactivation (INACT) of aircraft carriers, plus Integrated Logistics Support (ILS). These are among the most technically demanding and schedule-sensitive programs in the Navy's portfolio, with inherent uncertainty in scope, labor mix, and workload driven by the operational status of the vessels and evolving program requirements. CPFF is well-suited here: the government is purchasing expert professional labor in a domain where the quantity and nature of effort cannot be precisely predicted in advance, and where the incumbent has been performing under a similar structure. The NAICS code 541330 (Engineering Services) and the nature of RCOH/INACT support — which involves detailed engineering analysis, logistics planning, and program management advisory work tied to unpredictable fleet events — further support the appropriateness of cost-reimbursement pricing over fixed-price. There is no indication in the solicitation of a well-defined deliverable set or a stable, predictable scope that would argue for FFP. Nor is the work purely time-driven labor-hour type work that might favor T&M. CPFF is the standard and appropriate vehicle for this class of professional engineering support services on complex naval programs. The SeaPort-NxG MAC contract vehicle, which is the ordering mechanism, is also designed for cost-type task orders of this nature. No significant mismatch between contract type and requirements is apparent from the available information.
