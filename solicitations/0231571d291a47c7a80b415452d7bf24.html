Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The solicitation explicitly identifies the contract type as Cost Plus Fixed Fee (CPFF) for what is described as routine maintenance and updates to an existing proprietary software system. CPFF is a cost-reimbursement vehicle appropriate under FAR when 'uncertainties involved in contract performance do not permit costs to be estimated with sufficient accuracy to use any type of fixed-price contract.' That rationale is difficult to apply here: the government explicitly states it is not pursuing new software development, only maintenance of an existing, well-understood system by the incumbent vendor who built and owns it. Maintenance work on a known system by a known vendor with a structured annual option period is precisely the kind of engagement where labor categories and approximate hours can be estimated — making T&M the more natural choice. T&M would provide cost visibility through fixed hourly rates while accommodating variability in the precise mix of maintenance tasks from year to year. CPFF, by contrast, reimburses actual costs with a fixed fee, giving the vendor less cost-efficiency incentive than T&M and adding administrative burden without commensurate benefit for a straightforward maintenance engagement. That said, the severity is assessed as moderate (2) rather than severe (3) for several reasons: the solicitation provides minimal scope detail, so one cannot fully rule out technical uncertainty in the maintenance tasks; the software involves complex radioepidemiology models where even maintenance might encounter unpredictable technical challenges; and the government may have institutional reasons for CPFF related to budget flexibility. The mismatch is real and worth flagging, but it is not egregious given the limited information provided.
Evidence Quotes
The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention intends to award a sole source cost plus fixed fee contract to Oak Ridge Center for Risk Analysis (ORCRA) to provide updates and maintenance to their existing proprietary internet-based software program called NIOSH-Interactive RadioEpidemiological Program (NIOSH-IREP).
The contract type is explicitly stated as Cost Plus Fixed Fee (CPFF). This is a cost-reimbursement vehicle, which is typically appropriate for large, complex, technically uncertain programs — not routine software maintenance and updates.
At this time, the Government is not interested in new software development and only intends on purchasing maintenance services for NIOSH-IREP.
The government explicitly states it is purchasing maintenance services only — not new development. Maintenance and updates to an existing, well-understood proprietary system represent relatively predictable, recurring labor. This description aligns more closely with T&M or even a fixed-price contract than cost-reimbursement.
The anticipated period of performance is August 28, 2026 through August 27, 2027, and will include four one-year option periods.
A multi-year maintenance contract with defined annual option periods suggests a stable, ongoing engagement. The recurring nature of maintenance work — with a known system and a known incumbent — supports the predictability of labor scope, further undermining the rationale for cost-reimbursement pricing.
This contract action is for services for which the Government intends to solicit and negotiate with only one source under the authority of FAR 6.103-1 and 41 U.S.C. 3304(a)(1).
The sole-source justification is based on ORCRA's proprietary ownership of the software. While this explains why there is no competition, it does not explain why CPFF was chosen over T&M. Since ORCRA has deep familiarity with NIOSH-IREP, the labor categories, hours, and costs for maintenance should be reasonably estimable — making T&M a more appropriate vehicle for labor-intensive maintenance work with somewhat variable but broadly knowable scope.
