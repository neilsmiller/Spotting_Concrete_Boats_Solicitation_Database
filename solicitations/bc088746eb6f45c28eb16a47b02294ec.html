Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a textbook appropriate use of firm-fixed-price contracting. The requirement is a discrete, well-scoped construction task: removal and replacement of an asphalt shingle roof on a known building with a known footprint (~4,000 square feet). The materials are standard (asphalt shingles, flashing, guttering), the work is performed by an established trade (NAICS 238160, Roofing Contractors), and the labor and material costs for such work are highly predictable in the market. Davis-Bacon wage rates are provided, further anchoring the labor cost baseline. There is no indication of hidden complexity, unstable requirements, or uncertain scope that would make FFP risky or inappropriate. FFP places performance risk on the contractor — the correct allocation for a routine construction replacement — and incentivizes efficient execution. No alternative contract type (T&M or cost-reimbursement) would be preferable here; in fact, either would be clearly inferior for a fixed-scope roofing replacement. The contract type is well-matched to the requirements. Severity is 1.
