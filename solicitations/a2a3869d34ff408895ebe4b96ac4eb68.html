Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is for a 50 MB commercial Intra-Europe telecommunications circuit — a commodity telecom service with a well-understood, stable scope. The pricing structure of Monthly Recurring Charges (MRC) and Non-Recurring Charges (NRC) is effectively a fixed-price arrangement, which is the correct contract type for this kind of requirement. The scope is precisely defined (bandwidth, geography), the service is commercially available, costs can be estimated with high accuracy, and the market is competitive among registered DITCO Basic Agreement holders. There is no ambiguity in the deliverable — the vendor provides a circuit at a specified capacity between European locations. A T&M or cost-reimbursement structure would be entirely inappropriate here and would expose the government to unnecessary cost risk. The LPTA evaluation method further reinforces the appropriateness of fixed pricing, as it presupposes that technically acceptable offers can be compared purely on price — which is only sensible when the deliverable is well-defined and the price is predictable. No square peg concern is present. The contract pricing scheme is well-matched to the requirement.
Evidence Quotes
All quoted prices shall be identified by Monthly Recurring Charge (MRC), Non-Recurring Charge (NRC), and any tiered pricing for additional service months, as applicable.
The pricing structure — MRC and NRC — is the standard commercial telecommunications billing framework. This is a well-defined, commercially available service (50 MB Intra-Europe circuit) with predictable, recurring costs. A fixed-price structure (effectively FFP with monthly recurring charges) is entirely appropriate here.
This is a combined synopsis/solicitation for commercial products or commercial services prepared in accordance with FAR Part 12.
FAR Part 12 acquisitions are designed for commercial items and services. Telecommunications circuits are commodity-like commercial services with established market prices. Fixed pricing (MRC/NRC) is the industry-standard contract structure for such services and aligns well with FAR Part 12 intent.
