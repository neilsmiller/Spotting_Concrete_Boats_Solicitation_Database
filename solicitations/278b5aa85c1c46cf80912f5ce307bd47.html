Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Insufficient Data 0 quotes ▾

⚠ Insufficient content to evaluate this sin — the solicitation lacked a substantive Statement of Work or attachments.

Reasoning
This solicitation (SPRMM126QKD19) is a simplified acquisition for a commercial/military hardware item — an Encoder-Decoder communications component — issued by DLA Maritime Mechanicsburg. The document is a standard DLA supply solicitation using boilerplate FAR/DFARS clauses and DLA procurement notes. It contains no Statement of Work, Performance Work Statement, or Quality Assurance Surveillance Plan beyond the standard inspection clause (FAR 52.246-2, Inspection of Supplies — Fixed-Price). There are no reporting requirements, meeting cadences, deliverable documentation mandates, or performance metrics of any kind — because this is a product purchase, not a services contract. The quality assurance provisions are limited to standard supply inspection language (Section 4 of the item description), which assigns inspection responsibility to the contractor and reserves government inspection rights. This is entirely appropriate for a supply procurement. There is simply insufficient content to assess Compliance Over Outcomes as defined — that sin applies to service contracts with QA surveillance plans, reporting structures, and performance accountability frameworks, none of which are present here.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a straightforward DLA supply solicitation for a hardware item (ENCODER-DECODER,COM) using LPTA evaluation. It contains no experience requirements with specific dollar thresholds, no federal-specific experience mandates, no lookback windows, no institutional knowledge requirements, and no evaluation criteria that bonus incumbents. The authorized distributor requirement (Section 6.2) could narrow the vendor pool but is a legitimate supply chain integrity measure standard to DLA hardware procurements — it is applied equally to all offerors and does not privilege the prior contract holder. The LPTA evaluation methodology further reduces insider rewards risk by making price the tiebreaker among technically acceptable offerors. A qualified vendor who has never held this specific contract could realistically compete as long as they hold authorized distributor status from the OEM. Severity is minimal.

It is not an incumbency-favoring criterion in the insider rewards sense, as any legitimate authorized distributor (including new entrants) can compete.

The use of LPTA actually mitigates insider rewards risk. Under LPTA, once a vendor meets the technical threshold, price determines award. This structure does not bonus incumbents or reward prior contract history with additional evaluation points.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a DLA supply solicitation for a specific NSN hardware item — an encoder-decoder communications component for naval vessels. The solicitation is structured as a standard DLA purchase order template with a short item description (SQMS-style) rather than a complex services contract with a Statement of Work. The nature of the procurement fundamentally limits the relevance of requirement sprawl analysis: the government is buying a defined part number to a known specification, not procuring a service or capability. In this context, prescribing WHAT the item must be is the entire point of the solicitation. The requirements that do appear — mercury-free construction, MIL-STD-130 marking, MIL-STD-2073 packaging, CAGE/reference number conformance, and the configuration change control codes — are all standard DLA supply chain requirements applicable to military hardware. None of them dictate HOW the contractor manufactures the item; they define material constraints, compliance standards, and administrative procedures consistent with defense supply chain management. The mercury testing procedure is the most granular process specification, but it is a legitimate safety standard for submarine equipment, not an artifact of incumbent capture or consensus-driven sprawl. There is no SOW, PWS, or performance work statement present. The solicitation is appropriately scoped for a commercial/catalog-type supply item. Requirement sprawl — in the sense of over-prescribing how services are delivered or how complex work is organized — is not a material issue here. Severity is 1.

Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation does not exhibit the rule pool pattern in any meaningful way. The regulatory citations are extremely limited and tightly scoped to the actual work: MIL-STD-130 for item identification/marking (directly required for DoD item unique identification compliance) and MIL-STD-2073 for preservation and packaging (standard for physical hardware procurement). The FAR/DFARS clauses incorporated at the top — IUID valuation, inspection of supplies, WAWF payment instructions, security prohibitions, representations and certifications, and priority rating — are all standard, expected clauses for a DLA fixed-price supply procurement. None appear irrelevant or contradictory. The Applicable Documents section is notably sparse rather than overwhelming, which is the opposite of a rule pool. The mercury contamination requirement, while detailed, is a legitimate and well-explained safety requirement for submarine/surface ship applications with clear technical rationale provided in the text itself. The solicitation provides more context for its requirements than most comparable DLA supply solicitations. This is a low-complexity, small-quantity hardware buy with an appropriately focused compliance landscape. Severity 1 is warranted.
