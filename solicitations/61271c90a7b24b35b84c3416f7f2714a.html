Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation exhibits moderate requirement sprawl. The core problem — design, build, equip, staff, and manage a trade show booth — is broadly stated in outcome terms, and much of the solicitation appropriately defines what must be delivered (booth infrastructure, staffing, AV, photography) without dictating every implementation detail. However, several sections, particularly the AV and promotional materials section, shift from defining outcomes to prescribing specific methods, formats, quantities, and equipment. The video production requirements specify exact format counts and orientations; the digital display workflow describes a step-by-step production process; and equipment specifications name exact microphone types and screen sizes. These are most consistent with requirements written around an incumbent's prior execution — the 'how' of the last event has become the required 'how' for the next vendor. A professional event production company cannot propose innovations in content strategy, audio reinforcement, or display technology because the solution has already been partially designed for them. That said, the sprawl is not pervasive — large portions of the requirement (booth design, kitchen equipment, staffing structure, utilities) are described at a functional level that leaves meaningful room for vendor judgment. The severity is therefore 2 (moderate): constraining in specific areas, but not so dominant that the solicitation reads as an operating manual.
Evidence Quotes
The contractor shall produce four (4) vertical-format videos for social media and two (2) long-form horizontal videos suitable for website use.
This prescribes specific deliverable formats and quantities rather than defining the outcome — e.g., 'produce social media and web video content sufficient to support ATO's digital communications goals.' A vendor cannot propose alternative formats (square video, reels, shorts) that might perform better on specific platforms, even if they would better serve the government's communications objectives.
The contractor shall produce and format the digital materials to be displayed on the booth's television screens. ATO will provide the content (including images, videos, text, and logos), and the contractor will be responsible for editing, organizing, and assembling the materials into visually engaging video loops or presentations suitable for continuous display during the event. All materials must be submitted to ATO for review and approval prior to use.
This prescribes a specific production workflow — ATO provides raw content, contractor assembles into loops — rather than defining the outcome (engaging digital displays that showcase U.S. agricultural products). A vendor with a different content strategy (e.g., real-time digital signage, interactive displays) cannot propose it. The step-by-step process ('editing, organizing, and assembling') reads like an incumbent's workflow transposed into requirements.
Provide two (2) wireless handheld microphones and two (2) headset microphones, primarily for use during cooking demonstrations. Provide one megaphone for use during daily activities and announcements.
This specifies exact equipment types and quantities rather than an outcome like 'provide audio reinforcement sufficient to support cooking demonstrations and general announcements throughout the booth.' A vendor cannot propose a better-suited audio solution — e.g., a small PA system that obviates the need for a separate megaphone — even if it would be more effective or cost-efficient.
One 43-inch screen to be placed above the cooking station.
Added via amendment, this specifies the exact screen size and placement rather than defining the display need (e.g., 'a screen visible to cooking demonstration attendees above the cooking station'). A vendor cannot propose a different size that better fits the booth layout or achieves better visibility.

Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 3 quotes ▾
Reasoning
FFP is the correct contract type for this requirement. The deliverables are concrete and enumerable: booth design and construction, specified AV equipment (e.g., two wireless handheld microphones, one 43-inch screen above the cooking station), photography and videography for 20 defined hours, four vertical-format social media videos, two long-form horizontal videos, staffing, and daily operational support. The event has a fixed venue, a fixed duration, and a well-understood market (event production services in Mexico City, NAICS 561920). Experienced vendors in this space routinely price such engagements as lump-sum fixed-price contracts because the cost drivers — labor, equipment rental, materials, logistics — are predictable at time of bidding. There are no significant scope uncertainties that would warrant T&M (which is reserved for work where quantity of labor cannot be predicted) or cost-reimbursement (reserved for complex, technically uncertain programs). The FAR's own standard — FFP 'shall be used when the risk involved is minimal or can be predicted with an acceptable degree of certainty' — is satisfied here. No mismatch between contract type and requirements is present.

This is exactly the type of requirement for which FFP is appropriate.
