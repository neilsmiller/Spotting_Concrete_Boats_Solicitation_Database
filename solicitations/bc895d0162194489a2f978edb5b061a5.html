Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Severe 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation represents the most severe form of insider rewards — a sole source determination rather than a competitive acquisition with biased evaluation criteria. The government has not written experience requirements that tilt the playing field toward the incumbent; it has dispensed with competition altogether. The stated basis for sole source is that the incumbent's proprietary software, institutional knowledge, and specialized expertise make any other vendor incapable of performing the work without a complete system redesign. The root cause is textbook vendor capture. Over a twenty-year period, NGA has allowed critical system knowledge and source code to reside entirely with Compusult rather than the government. The government does not own the source code, does not possess the institutional knowledge, and cannot articulate requirements in vendor-neutral terms because the system is built on the incumbent's proprietary platform. The solicitation itself confirms this: competing would require 'a complete redesign of the IGW System,' and any other award would cause 'substantial duplication of cost and unacceptable delays.' This is a severity 3 finding — the most severe level — because the insider rewards dynamic has fully matured. There are no evaluation criteria to analyze for bias because there is no competition. The government has formally declared that only one vendor can perform the work, and the structural reason for that declaration is twenty years of accumulated dependency. A qualified vendor who has never held this contract cannot realistically compete; that is not a risk but a certainty, acknowledged by the government in its own justification. The solicitation does include the required boilerplate that 'all responsible sources may submit a capability statement,' but this is a pro forma notice requirement under FAR 5.101, not a genuine competitive opening. The government's own language makes clear that any such submission would face an insuperable barrier: no vendor other than Compusult holds the proprietary platform on which IGW runs.

Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The use of FFP for this contract is defensible but not clearly optimal, warranting a moderate severity rating. On one hand, FFP is reasonable for the hosting and routine maintenance components of this contract, where the scope is relatively stable and predictable: the system has been running for 20 years, the incumbent knows the workload well, and historical data should allow for reasonable cost estimation. The FAR supports FFP when risk can be estimated with acceptable certainty, and a mature, long-running operational system has a track record that supports estimation. On the other hand, the inclusion of 'enhancement' work in scope introduces meaningful uncertainty: feature enhancements by definition respond to evolving government requirements, and the extent of that work cannot be fully defined at contract award. T&M or a hybrid structure (FFP for hosting/maintenance, T&M for enhancements) would better match the variable nature of enhancement work and give the government better cost control levers. The absence of competition compounds the concern: FFP's discipline depends partly on competitive bidding to set an honest price, but here the price is negotiated solely with the incumbent who controls proprietary software the government cannot walk away from. This is not a severe square peg — the predictable components of the work (hosting, routine maintenance) do fit FFP, and the long incumbent relationship provides cost history — but there is meaningful risk that the enhancement work will generate scope creep or modifications that undermine the intended price certainty of FFP. A severity of 2 is appropriate: the choice is defensible given the mature system, but a contracting expert would reasonably recommend a hybrid pricing approach to better match the variable enhancement workload.
Evidence Quotes
IGW4 will be established as firm fixed price contract with a seven-month base period and four twelve-month option periods.
The solicitation explicitly states this will be a firm-fixed-price contract. FFP places cost risk on the vendor, which is appropriate only when the scope is well-defined and costs can be estimated with reasonable certainty.
The contract scope is to provide the necessary skilled technical personnel, materials, facilities, security, and services required to accomplish the hosting, maintenance, and enhancement of the IGW applications on an unclassified domain, as well as the secure access services that support the IGW.
The scope includes 'enhancement' of the IGW applications alongside hosting and maintenance. Enhancement work — adding new features, responding to evolving user needs — is inherently variable in scope and difficult to estimate upfront, which is a characteristic that weighs against FFP and toward T&M or a hybrid structure.
IGW4 is a follow-on contract for highly specialized services that spans twenty years. Compusult has been fulfilling all primary functions and components of the IGW system using their proprietary Web Enterprise Suite (WES) software and secure hosting facilities.
The 20-year incumbent relationship means NGA has substantial historical cost data from prior contract periods, which could support a reasonably accurate cost estimate and makes FFP more defensible than it would be for a new system. However, the sole-source nature also means there is no competitive pressure to keep the fixed price honest, which is one of the key protections FFP relies on.
The government has restricted rights to the baseline software used in IGW and does not possess the baseline source code.
Because the government lacks source code and the vendor controls the proprietary WES platform, future enhancement work may generate unpredictable labor requirements as the government identifies new functional needs. This dependency on the vendor's proprietary system makes the volume and complexity of enhancement work difficult to pre-define, creating tension with a FFP structure.
An award to any other source would result in substantial duplication of cost and unacceptable delays.
The sole-source justification removes competitive pricing pressure. In a competitive FFP environment, vendor bids discipline the price. Here, with no competition and the government lacking source code, the fixed price is essentially negotiated bilaterally with the incumbent, reducing one of the primary advantages of FFP — market-driven cost discipline.
