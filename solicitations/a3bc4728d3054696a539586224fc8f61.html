Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a pre-solicitation synopsis for a multi-award IDIQ, and the contract type framework it describes is well-matched to the nature of the work. The solicitation explicitly reserves both FFP and CPFF (plus hybrids) for use at the task order level, rather than locking all work into a single pricing structure. This is the correct design for a vehicle safety research IDIQ: some task orders — such as discrete component tests or structured evaluation studies — will have sufficiently defined scopes to support FFP. Others — such as multi-year ADS research, AI/ML application research, or naturalistic driving studies — will involve genuine technical uncertainty where CPFF is the appropriate vehicle. The IDIQ wrapper itself does not carry a pricing type mismatch; it defers pricing decisions to the task order level, which is standard and appropriate practice. No T&M is mentioned, which is notable but not concerning given that the work described emphasizes research outputs and defined testing activities rather than purely labor-hour-based maintenance or support tasks. The pre-solicitation's brevity limits full analysis — the actual solicitation and any attached PWS/SOW will be the real test — but based on available content there is no evident square peg. Severity is 1.

Task orders issued under the IDIQ may utilize a variety of contract types, including: Firm-Fixed-Price (FFP), Cost-Plus-Fixed-Fee (CPFF), Hybrid task orders combining FFP and CPFF elements, as appropriate
The solicitation explicitly preserves flexibility in pricing structure, allowing FFP for well-defined tasks and CPFF for more uncertain, research-intensive efforts. This is a reasonable approach for a multi-award IDIQ covering a wide range of work types.
Work under this contract may include: Planning, conducting, and documenting vehicle safety research studies; Testing and evaluation of ADAS and ADS technologies; Simulation and modeling of vehicle systems and driver behavior; Vehicle component testing and functional safety assessments; On-road testing and naturalistic driving studies; Safety impact assessments and data analysis; Cybersecurity research and evaluation; Application of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) to vehicle safety
The scope spans both well-defined testing and evaluation tasks (which could support FFP task orders) and open-ended research activities (which are better suited to CPFF). By preserving both pricing types at the task order level, the IDIQ structure maps to this heterogeneous work mix.
