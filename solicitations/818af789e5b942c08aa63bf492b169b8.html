Insider Rewards

Minimal 0 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is for 1 Gbps NIPRNET commercial internet service at two specific locations (Moffett FAF and Alameda, CA) under NAICS 517121 (Wired Telecommunications Carriers). It is a small business set-aside using Lowest Price Technically Acceptable (LPTA) evaluation methodology. The solicitation contains no evidence of insider rewards. Several characteristics of this procurement actually work against incumbency bias: 1. LPTA source selection: The evaluation methodology rewards the lowest price among technically acceptable offers. This inherently levels the playing field — a new entrant who can deliver the required service at a lower price wins, regardless of prior contract history. 2. Commercial telecommunications service: 1 Gbps NIPRNET connectivity is a standardized commercial telecommunications product. There is no specialized institutional knowledge requirement, no agency-specific system familiarity needed, and no complex transition involving incumbent staff or tribal knowledge. Any telecom provider with infrastructure in the Moffett/Alameda area can technically compete. 3. No experience thresholds stated: The solicitation references no minimum dollar thresholds for prior contracts, no required number of prior federal contracts, no lookback windows, and no federal-specific experience mandates. The description contains no personnel qualification requirements at all. 4. Small business set-aside: The 1,500-employee size standard for NAICS 517121 is the standard threshold and does not function as an insider filter — it opens competition to a broad class of smaller telecom providers. 5. No past performance scoring details: While the solicitation references evaluation criteria details in an attachment that is not provided, the LPTA framework described does not suggest past performance is being used as a discriminating factor beyond a threshold acceptable/unacceptable determination, which is standard practice. The nature of the requirement — commodity bandwidth service at specific locations — means the primary differentiator is whether a vendor has physical network infrastructure capable of serving those locations, which is a genuine technical capability requirement rather than an incumbency proxy. A qualified vendor who has never held this contract but has fiber infrastructure in the area could realistically compete and win on price. No stacking of insider-favoring criteria is apparent. Severity is 1.
