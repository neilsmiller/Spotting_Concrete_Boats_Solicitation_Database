Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Minimal 3 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a straightforward depot-level repair solicitation for a small quantity (8 units) of a military display server. The QA structure is minimal and appropriate for the contract type. There is no evidence of the characteristic patterns of Compliance Over Outcomes: no overlapping reporting cadences, no prescribed weekly/monthly/quarterly report formats, no mandated meeting frequencies generating redundant documentation, and no waterfall-style plan-adherence tracking. The primary accountability mechanism is the RTAT requirement — 81 calendar days — backed by an automatic price reduction for late delivery. This is a clean, outcome-oriented performance standard. The inspection standard references the original manufacturer's specifications and drawings, which is the correct technical acceptance criterion for a repair contract: did the item return to its designed operational state? The CAV RP reporting requirement (Section F) is a standard Navy inventory tracking system — it records asset receipt and delivery transactions, which serves a legitimate supply chain visibility function rather than generating compliance paperwork about process adherence. The solicitation lacks the hallmarks of this sin: there are no weekly status reports, no prescribed report sections, no plan deviation tracking, and no QA surveillance plans that check whether the contractor followed approved procedures rather than evaluating whether the repaired item actually works. Severity 1 is appropriate — the accountability structure is lean and outcome-oriented for this type of contract.

Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation presents a moderate-to-significant insider rewards pattern, rooted in a specific structural dynamic common to depot-level repair contracts for proprietary military electronics. The government's own notice acknowledges the core problem: it does not own the technical data package for the Display Server, the data cannot be economically acquired, and reverse engineering is not viable. As a result, the repair can only be performed by the entity holding the proprietary repair manual (CAGE 53711, apparent reference to Mercury Systems) or vendors explicitly authorized by that entity. The 'authorized repair source' requirement then operates as a formal gatekeeping mechanism that translates this data-rights constraint into a contractual exclusion criterion. The solicitation is issued under FAR 6.302-1 (only one responsible source), which is the legally correct vehicle for a genuine sole-source situation. This mitigates the severity somewhat — the government is not pretending to compete while actually wiring the outcome. It is being transparent about the structural constraint. However, the underlying conditions that produced this constraint — failure to retain data rights at original procurement, dependence on OEM-controlled technical documentation, inability to independently qualify new repair sources — are themselves the product of earlier procurement decisions that embedded an insider rewards structure. There are no stacked dollar thresholds, federal-specific experience mandates, or evaluation point bonuses for prior contract history in the traditional sense, because the solicitation doesn't even reach the evaluation stage in a competitive way. The past performance evaluation criteria (WSSTERMMZ01) are standard NAVSUP language and would apply neutrally if there were multiple qualified offerors — but there aren't. The 'authorized repair source' requirement and the data rights situation together function as the de facto insider filter. Severity is rated 2 rather than 3 because: (1) the government is transparent about the sole-source basis rather than disguising it as open competition; (2) the FAR 6.302-1 justification reflects a genuine (if self-created) technical constraint rather than an artificial one; and (3) the solicitation does include a market research notice inviting interested parties to identify capability, preserving at least a procedural pathway for a challenger. A severity-3 rating would be more appropriate for a solicitation that presents itself as competitive while embedding requirements that ensure the incumbent wins.
Evidence Quotes
The ultimate awardee of the proposed contract action must be an authorized repair source. Award of the proposed contract action will not be delayed while an unauthorized source seeks to obtain authorization.
This requirement explicitly restricts award to vendors already holding authorized repair source status for this specific item. A vendor with the capability to repair this display server — including the required technical skills, tools, and facilities — would be excluded if they have not previously been formally authorized. The authorization process itself is a barrier that advantages the incumbent or any previously authorized vendor, while closing out capable newcomers.

The repair work is tied to a specific CAGE code (53711) and reference document (TI14-S401). This means the technical repair procedures are sourced from a specific, identified vendor. A new entrant who does not already possess or have access to these proprietary repair documents — documents held by a specific company — cannot realistically perform the repair to specification. This creates a structural advantage for the entity holding CAGE 53711 (likely Mercury Systems, Inc., whose name appears on the continuation sheets) and any previously authorized subcontractors.
The Government does not own the data or the rights to the data needed to purchase / contract repair of this part from additional sources. It has been determined to be uneconomical to buy the data or rights to the data. It is uneconomical to reverse engineer the part.
The government explicitly acknowledges it cannot open competition to additional sources because the technical data package is controlled by the OEM/authorized source. This admission reveals that competition is structurally foreclosed by data rights constraints — only vendors who already possess the proprietary data can perform the repair. This is the root cause of the incumbency lock-in and explains why the solicitation is being issued as a FAR 6.302-1 sole-source.
The proposed contract action is for supplies or services for which the Government intends to solicit and negotiate with only one source under the authority of FAR 6.302-1.
The solicitation is structured as a single-source acquisition under the 'only one responsible source' authority. While this is a legal justification, it is the direct result of a proprietary data and authorization ecosystem that ensures no competitive entry point exists. The J&A basis (OEM data rights) is itself an insider rewards structure — the market has been defined so narrowly that only one source qualifies, functionally transforming incumbency into the only selection criterion.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a standard NAVSUP WSS repair RFQ for a specific NSN item (Display Server) and does not exhibit meaningful requirement sprawl. The technical requirements section (Section C) is notably sparse: it identifies the item by CAGE/reference number, defers entirely to the contractor's own repair/overhaul practices and the original manufacturer's specifications, and sets no step-by-step process mandates. The bulk of the document's pages are consumed by standard FAR/DFARS clauses (IUID marking, packaging to MIL-STD-2073, CAV RP reporting, WAWF payment instructions, property management), which are regulatory and administrative compliance requirements — not prescriptive constraints on how the repair itself should be accomplished. The packaging section (Section D) is detailed but references military packaging standards that apply uniformly across DoD and constitute legitimate supply chain constraints, not sprawl. The RTAT requirement (81 days, with price reduction for delay) and the FFP/NTE pricing structure define outcomes and accountability mechanisms rather than dictating repair methods. The solicitation appropriately focuses on WHAT must be delivered (a serviceable Display Server, meeting OEM specifications, packaged to MIL-STD-2073, within 81 days) rather than HOW the contractor must repair it. A vendor has full latitude to apply their own repair processes, tooling, and staffing. Severity 1 is appropriate — this is a competently scoped repair solicitation with no meaningful requirement sprawl.
Evidence Quotes
All repair work shall be performed in accordance with the contractors repair/overhaul standard practices, manuals and directives including but not limited to drawings, technical orders, manufacturing operations, tooling instructions, approved repair standards and any other contractor or government approved documents developed to provide technical repair procedures.
This language explicitly defers to the contractor's own repair practices and manuals, which is the opposite of prescriptive sprawl. It defines what technical authority governs the work (the manufacturer/contractor's own documentation) rather than dictating how the repair must be physically performed.
The Contractor shall perform all inspection and testing requirements as specified in the original manufacturer's specifications and drawings.
This references external authoritative standards (OEM specs) as the quality benchmark — a legitimate outcome/compliance constraint — rather than inventing bespoke government-defined inspection procedures that would prejudge the contractor's quality methods.
Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation does not exhibit the rule pool sin in any meaningful way. The 'Applicable Documents' section cites a single OEM technical instruction (TI14-S401 from CAGE 53711). The regulatory citations in Section I (contract clauses) are standard FAR and DFARS clauses incorporated by reference — a normal, expected element of federal contracting that does not constitute a rule pool. The packaging section (WSSTERMDZ03) does cite multiple MIL-STDs (MIL-STD-2073, MIL-STD-129, MIL-STD-147, etc.), but these are all directly and contextually relevant to military packaging and shipping requirements, each addressing a distinct aspect (preservation method, marking, palletization, wood packaging material), and many are cross-referenced with specific applicability explanations. The IUID clause (252.211-7003) is lengthy but is a mandatory, standard DFARS clause with clear relevance to asset tracking for a depot-level reparable item. The cybersecurity clause (252.204-7012) is similarly a standard DFARS mandate. None of the cited regulations appear irrelevant or contradictory to the scope of work (repair of a display server shelf assembly). The solicitation's total regulatory footprint is proportionate to a straightforward sole-source repair contract for a military electronics item. A vendor experienced in NAVSUP repair work would find the compliance landscape entirely familiar and parseable. Severity 1 is appropriate.

Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a standard NAVSUP WSS depot repair solicitation for a discrete, serialized hardware item — a Display Server — using an NTE/FFP pricing structure. FFP-NTE is the standard and well-accepted contract type for this class of work in the Navy's repair supply chain. The repair scope is bounded: the contractor works from an established TDP, follows known repair procedures, and delivers a specific item in 'A' condition. The quantity is small (8 units). The NTE structure appropriately allocates cost risk to the contractor while protecting the government from unlimited liability. The solicitation also includes a BR/BER (Beyond Repair/Beyond Economic Repair) clause that allows a negotiated reduced price if an asset is found unrepayable, which is a standard mitigation for the main uncertainty in depot repair (whether an induction is actually repairable). This is exactly the scenario where FFP/NTE is the right tool: well-defined technical requirements, established repair baseline, known contractor, limited quantity. There is no mismatch between contract type and requirements. Severity 1.
Evidence Quotes

The solicitation explicitly mandates a firm-fixed-price structure for a depot-level repair contract. This is the appropriate pricing mechanism here: the repair work is performed against a known technical data package (TDP VER 005), by an authorized source following established repair procedures, on a well-characterized item (NSN 7H-7025-016717368, Display Server). The scope — teardown, evaluate, repair and/or modify — is standard for this class of depot repair solicitation.
