Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a draft PWS pre-solicitation notice for a large MAC IDIQ covering network services for AFRICOM, CENTCOM, and EUCOM — three combatant commands spanning multiple continents with highly variable operational demands. The notice does not explicitly state the pricing type that will be used at the task order level, which is where the square peg risk would materialize. Based on available context, the IDIQ vehicle itself is structurally appropriate: the government cannot predict in advance exactly how much network capacity, how many circuits, or what mix of services will be needed across three combatant commands over a multi-year period. An IDIQ allows flexibility to place task orders as requirements emerge. The moderate concern is that network services contracts of this type tend to encompass a heterogeneous mix of requirements: (1) recurring telecommunications services with predictable pricing (e.g., leased circuits at fixed monthly rates), which are well-suited to FFP; and (2) variable labor-intensive services such as network engineering support, installation, configuration, and surge capacity, which are better suited to T&M or labor-hour arrangements. If the final solicitation adopts a single pricing approach across all service categories — particularly if it forces FFP on unpredictable support labor or allows T&M for predictable recurring services — that would represent a meaningful square peg. The NAICS code (517111 – Wired Telecommunications Carriers) suggests the primary framing is as a telecommunications services buy, where FFP per unit (per circuit, per Mbps) is standard commercial practice and well-aligned. However, the GNS predecessor contracts are known to include managed services and IT support components that are less predictable, creating a risk that the pricing structure may not be differentiated appropriately by service type. Given that the actual pricing structure is not yet specified in this draft release, and that IDIQ vehicles inherently allow for mixed pricing at the task order level, this is rated Severity 2 — Moderate. The structural choice of MAC IDIQ is defensible and commonly used for this type of requirement. The real square peg risk depends on how pricing is structured at the CLIN and task order level in the final solicitation, which has not been disclosed. The government's decision to release a draft PWS for industry feedback is a positive mitigant, as it provides an opportunity to surface pricing structure concerns before the formal solicitation is released.
