Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Insufficient Data 0 quotes ▾

⚠ Insufficient content to evaluate this sin — the solicitation lacked a substantive Statement of Work or attachments.

Reasoning
This solicitation does not contain sufficient content to meaningfully assess the Compliance Over Outcomes sin. The document is a 9-page commercial products/services RFQ (SF 1449) for Gigamon software license renewals and hardware support — essentially a product order form. The solicitation contains only a schedule of supplies/services listing specific Gigamon part numbers with quantities and periods of performance, administrative information (contracting officer, COR contacts), and standard FAR commercial clauses by reference. There is no Statement of Work, Performance Work Statement, Quality Assurance Surveillance Plan, reporting requirements, deliverables list, meeting cadence requirements, or any performance standards. The acquisition is for COTS software licenses and manufacturer support contracts — a transaction-based purchase rather than a services contract where QA structures would typically appear. Because there are no QA mechanisms, reporting requirements, or accountability structures of any kind visible in this solicitation, there is nothing to evaluate for compliance-vs-outcomes orientation. The absence of such structures is expected and appropriate for a commercial product license renewal of this nature.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Minimal 0 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a brand-name, sole-source-equivalent Request for Quotation for renewal of Gigamon proprietary software licenses and support services — specific Gigamon part numbers for existing hardware already installed at PBGC. The solicitation explicitly states 'This is a brand name RFQ' and specifies exact Gigamon part numbers (e.g., GSS-RNL-ELT-PSS, GVS-HC1P-SW-TM, TAP-HC1-G10040-SW-TM, GFM-FM000-SW-TM, GVS-TAX20-SW-TM) tied to hardware already deployed. Critically, this is a product/subscription procurement, not a services contract requiring personnel experience, past performance evaluation against incumbent-favoring criteria, or knowledge of agency-specific systems. The solicitation contains no experience requirements, no past performance evaluation criteria, no key personnel qualifications, and no institutional knowledge requirements of any kind. There is nothing in the document that would constitute an 'insider rewards' pattern as defined — no dollar thresholds for prior contracts, no federal-specific experience mandates, no evaluation scoring rubrics at all. The brand-name restriction does effectively limit competition to authorized Gigamon resellers, and the renewal nature of the procurement (with part numbers tied to existing installed hardware) means the universe of competitive vendors is constrained by the proprietary nature of the product rather than by artificially constructed experience requirements. This is a product refresh/renewal, not a services recompete, and the constraint on competition flows from the brand-name justification rather than from insider-rewards mechanisms targeting a specific incumbent contractor. The appropriate analytical lens for this solicitation would be brand-name justification adequacy, not insider rewards in the experience-requirement sense. No evidence of insider rewards patterns was identified. Severity is 1 — minimal.

Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Minimal 0 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a brand-name RFQ for commercial Gigamon software license renewals and hardware support services — a straightforward commercial product/service acquisition competed under FAR Subpart 16.505 using the NASA SEWP V GWAC vehicle. The 9 pages available consist almost entirely of a Standard Form 1449 and line-item schedules listing specific Gigamon part numbers, quantities, and periods of performance. There is no 'Applicable Documents' section, no 'Referenced Standards' section, no compliance framework list, and no regulatory citations beyond the standard SF 1449 references to FAR 52.212-1, 52.212-4, 52.212-3, and 52.212-5 — all of which are entirely appropriate and expected for a commercial items acquisition. The solicitation incorporates FAR clauses by reference in the standard manner (block 7a/7b of the SF 1449), which is normal federal contracting practice and explicitly excluded from the rule pools definition. The document contains no evidence of bulk regulatory citation, no 'Applicable Documents' dumps, no agency-specific policy overlays, and no irrelevant or contradictory regulatory inclusions. The compliance landscape for a vendor responding to this RFQ is straightforward: standard FAR commercial items clauses apply. This solicitation scores a 1 on the rule pools severity scale. Note: the solicitation notice indicates 26 total pages but only 9 pages were available for review; additional pages may contain clauses or attachments with more regulatory content. However, based on the available text, there is no indication of a rule pools problem, and the nature of the acquisition (commercial software license renewals via an existing GWAC vehicle) makes an extensive regulatory compliance section structurally unlikely.
