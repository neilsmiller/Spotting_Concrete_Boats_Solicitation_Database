Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation uses FFP for a maintenance and repair requirement that has significant inherent scope uncertainty, which is a moderate square peg. The most egregious mismatch is CLIN 0003 (parts), where the solicitation explicitly states 'if parts are needed' and 'Government will determine the price' — language that is fundamentally at odds with a true FFP structure. You cannot have a firm-fixed-price contract where pricing for a CLIN is determined after award by the government. This CLIN functions more like a cost-reimbursable or T&M element grafted onto an FFP vehicle, which creates contract administration complexity and ambiguity about how modifications will be handled. The repair and return CLIN (0002) and remote support CLIN (0001) also have unpredictable workloads by nature — equipment failure frequency and support demand are stochastic. T&M with a ceiling price, or a hybrid structure with FFP for the standing support retainer and T&M for variable repair labor, would better reflect actual risk allocation. However, this is not severity 3 because there are mitigating factors: (1) this is a relatively small, single-vendor commercial procurement for a specific OEM product (Tellabs GPON), which limits the contractor pool and may make pricing more predictable; (2) the government's approval-gate on parts (CLIN 0003) does provide some cost control even if it is structurally awkward under FFP; (3) for a one-year base period with two option years, a vendor with knowledge of the installed base at Camp Blaz may be able to provide a reasonable fixed-price estimate for the support and repair labor components. The mismatch is real and creates contract administration risk, particularly around how parts costs will be incorporated, but it is not a textbook severe square peg — it is a common and somewhat defensible approach to small maintenance contracts that the government frequently uses.
Evidence Quotes
The Government anticipates award of a firm-fixed-Price Purchase Order type contract to the company whose quote with the lowest price that meets requirements.
The solicitation explicitly states FFP as the contract type. This is the primary data point for the square peg analysis.
0002 | Equipment repair and return service — The contractor shall restore all failed network components to a serviceable operating condition. | 1 | Job
Equipment repair and return is inherently demand-driven and unpredictable. The quantity of repairs needed over a 12-month period cannot be known in advance — it depends on how many components fail, which is a stochastic function of equipment age, usage, and environmental conditions at Camp Blaz in Guam. Pricing this as a single FFP 'Job' obscures the actual scope uncertainty.
0003 | Approval parts — If parts are needed, the contractor must provide a list of parts required and cost estimate to the Government for approval. Parts that have been replaced will become the property of the government. (Government will determine the price)
The parts CLIN explicitly acknowledges indeterminate scope: 'if parts are needed' and 'Government will determine the price.' This is structurally incompatible with FFP, which requires a fixed price agreed at award. The government appears to be treating CLIN 0003 as an open-ended, on-demand cost element, which is more consistent with a T&M or cost-reimbursement approach than with a true FFP structure.
0001 | Remote technical assistance support — The contractor shall provide remote technical support includes assistance to Camp Blaz personnel via telephone and/or MS Teams in troubleshooting and resolving technical issues affecting the GPON network. | 1 | Job
Remote technical support is labor-intensive and demand-driven. The volume of calls, hours of troubleshooting, and complexity of issues affecting the GPON network are unpredictable in advance. Pricing this as a single FFP 'Job' means either the vendor will pad the price to cover worst-case scenarios, or will underperform if actual demand exceeds estimates — both outcomes the government should want to avoid.
