Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?

⚠ Insufficient content to evaluate this sin — the solicitation lacked a substantive Statement of Work or attachments.

Reasoning
This posting is a Sources Sought Notice and an early-stage Request for Quote with minimal substantive requirements. The document is primarily a standard SF-1449 form with boilerplate FAR/DFARS clauses and a very thin evaluation framework. The solicitation description (from the notice text) asks potential sources to submit a capabilities statement describing 'experience providing CATV and internet services within the past three years, unique capabilities and resources, and the Company's size status' — but this is market research language for a Sources Sought, not binding evaluation criteria for a competitive award. The actual solicitation (W91QVP26QA006) contains an evaluation addendum referencing only two factors: Technical Approach (one-page narrative) and Price, with no specific dollar thresholds, no federal-experience mandates, no personnel qualification requirements, no past performance scoring rubric, and no minimum contract history requirements. There is simply not enough substantive requirement or evaluation language to assess whether insider rewards are present. The one-page technical narrative requirement is capability-focused and does not impose any of the hallmarks of insider rewards (specific dollar thresholds, federal-specific experience mandates, narrow lookback windows, or institutional knowledge requirements). A meaningful Insider Rewards analysis requires a SOW/PWS with personnel qualifications, detailed past performance requirements, or a structured evaluation rubric — none of which are present here in sufficient detail.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?

Reasoning
This document is a Sources Sought Notice combined with what appears to be an early-stage solicitation shell (SF 1449) for CATV and internet services at Schofield Barracks, Hawaii. The substantive technical content is extremely thin — the requirement is described in a single paragraph stating the contractor shall provide 'cable television and internet services at approximately thirteen locations' and be 'responsible for providing all personnel, equipment, tools, materials, vehicles and supervision necessary to install, maintain, repair, etc equipment pertinent to providing cable television (CATV) programming and internet services.' The single line item (0001) specifies '100M and Managed Network Wi-Fi MR-28, Digital Cable w/ Receivers' with quantity 1 Job. The solicitation contains no bullet lists of prescribed processes, no staffing structures, no step-by-step methodologies — because there is essentially no detailed requirements section at all. The MR-28 reference (a specific Cisco Meraki access point model) is the closest thing to a prescriptive technical specification, but it appears in a line item description rather than a requirements section, and the document is too sparse to determine whether this reflects genuine sprawl or simply a product specification for a commercial off-the-shelf procurement. Without an attached SOW or PWS containing actual performance requirements, there is insufficient content to meaningfully assess requirement sprawl. The document is better characterized as a minimally populated solicitation shell than a substantive requirements document.

Severity 1 is appropriate.

Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The solicitation uses a Firm Fixed Price contract for the provision of commercial cable television and internet services at Schofield Barracks. This is a textbook FFP use case: the requirements are well-defined (100M internet, managed Wi-Fi, digital cable with receivers at roughly thirteen buildings), the services are commercially available, market pricing is transparent and competitive, and the scope is stable over the one-year performance period. There is no technical uncertainty that would warrant T&M, and no long-term R&D or undefined labor mix that would call for cost-reimbursement. The government knows what it is buying — a standard commercial telecom package — and FFP gives the vendor the incentive to deliver efficiently. The contract type is well-matched to the requirement.
