Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The solicitation specifies a Firm-Fixed Price Requirements Contract for Contract Logistics Support (CLS) of the TADS hardware units and CMDP software for the F-15 fleet. The requirement is primarily for repair, maintenance, firmware/software upgrades, and associated logistics support delivered by the OEM. FFP is a reasonable — arguably well-suited — contract type for this context. The government has projected quantities (20 minor repairs, 10 major repairs per year) that suggest a predictable workload, and CLS contracts with OEMs for fielded systems are a classic use case for FFP task orders under a requirements contract. The OEM's long familiarity with the hardware and software means cost estimation is feasible. The scope of individual deliverables (a repaired unit, a software upgrade package) is concrete and measurable, which supports fixed-price pricing. While repair work can occasionally reveal hidden complexity that drives scope changes, the use of a Requirements Contract with per-unit pricing is a standard and defensible way to handle this — the government is essentially buying individual repair or upgrade events at pre-negotiated prices, not contracting for an open-ended labor pool. A T&M structure would be less appropriate here because the government does have enough knowledge of the work type and volume to set prices in advance, and T&M would remove the vendor's incentive to repair units efficiently. There is no significant mismatch between the contract type and the requirements as described. Severity is assessed as 1 — minimal concern.
