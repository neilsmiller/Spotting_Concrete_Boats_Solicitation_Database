The past performance questionnaire rating categories (Quality of Service, Timeliness of Performance, Customer Satisfaction, Ability to Manage Cost) provide a glimpse of the evaluation framework the FAA uses for prior efforts, but these are backward-looking assessments of historical performance, not the forward-looking QA and reporting requirements imposed on future contractors. The solicitation summary mentions 'product-based services' including asset lifecycle management, deployment, and maintenance — scope that would typically generate QA and reporting obligations — but those specific obligations are contained in the SOW and contract terms that were not included in the provided documents. Without those sections, no reliable assessment of whether the accountability structure measures compliance versus outcomes is possible.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation shows moderate incumbency-favoring characteristics, primarily concentrated in the past performance evaluation structure. The combination of OEM-specific experience requirements (named OEMs from L.11.2.2.1(a)(i)), a 12-month order volume metric tied to those specific OEMs, and dollar-threshold relevance criteria creates a past performance scoring environment that advantages vendors who have recently held large IT reseller contracts covering the same OEM portfolio. These requirements are not trivially satisfied by a capable but non-incumbent IT reseller. However, several factors prevent this from reaching severity 3. First, the multi-award structure with up to eight IDIQ awards — including six reserved for small businesses — meaningfully expands competition and reduces the winner-take-all dynamic that makes incumbency bias most damaging. Second, the LPTA evaluation approach means price disciplines the field: an incumbent cannot simply coast on institutional knowledge; they must also be competitive on price. Third, the NAICS code (541519, IT Value Added Resellers) is a broadly populated industry segment with many qualified vendors, meaning the OEM relationship requirements, while specific, are not exotic — many established IT resellers maintain authorized reseller status with the major OEMs likely listed in L.11.2.2.1(a)(i). The available document excerpts do not include the full Section L evaluation criteria or the specific OEM list, which limits confidence in this assessment. If the named OEM list is narrow and the dollar thresholds in M.3.2 are high, the actual severity could be closer to 3. Based on available evidence, severity 2 is the appropriate finding: the playing field is tilted toward vendors with recent, large-scale, OEM-aligned federal IT reseller experience, but the multi-award structure and LPTA approach preserve meaningful competitive opportunity for qualified non-incumbents.
Evidence Quotes
Total value of orders issued within 12 months of proposal as it directly pertains to the OEM (reference Sec L.11.2.2.1): _____
The past performance form requires offerors to report order volume specifically tied to named OEMs within a 12-month window. This metric rewards incumbents who already have established, high-volume OEM reseller relationships under an existing similar contract. A new entrant with genuine OEM partnerships but without a recent high-volume federal contract history would appear weak on this dimension, even if they are a fully authorized reseller with deep OEM relationships.
Discuss how work performed on this program is relevant to the requirements of this SIR, specifically addressing the SOW Sections C06-16, and discussing the relevant OEM coverage and relationships for those OEMs listed in SIR Section L.11.2.2.1(a)(i)
The past performance narrative requirement explicitly demands that offerors address OEM coverage for a specific enumerated list of OEMs (referenced in L.11.2.2.1(a)(i)). This narrows the relevance criterion beyond generic IT reseller experience — vendors must demonstrate prior contract performance that directly maps to the same named OEMs as the SAVES program. An otherwise capable IT reseller whose prior contracts covered different OEM portfolios would be disadvantaged even if their underlying reseller capability is equivalent.
Contract Annual Awarded Value (including all orders, reference Sec M.3.2)
The past performance form requires reporting contract annual awarded value, and Section M.3.2 is referenced as a relevance threshold. While the full evaluation criteria are not reproduced in the available text, this structure suggests annual dollar thresholds are used to assess past performance relevance. Combined with the OEM-specificity requirements, this creates a stacked screen: past contracts must be large enough AND cover the right OEMs — a combination that favors the incumbent SAVES holder and a small number of similarly positioned large IT resellers.
The FAA intends to competitively award approximately eight (8) multiple-award Indefinite Delivery Indefinite Quantity (IDIQ) contracts under this SIR... Up to six (6) awards to Offerors reserved for Small Businesses under the Small Business Pool, and up to two (2) awards to Offerors Unrestricted in Size under the Unrestricted Pool.
The multi-award, two-pool structure is a genuine mitigating factor. With up to six small business awards, the solicitation broadens competition considerably and reduces the severity of incumbency bias. A qualified small business IT reseller with reasonable past performance and OEM relationships has a realistic path to award even without being the prior SAVES incumbent. This substantially limits the exclusionary effect of the more specific experience requirements.

Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 3 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation establishes a multiple-award IDIQ for the strategic sourcing of commercial IT hardware, software, and directly related product-based services (e.g., asset recovery, warehousing, deployment). The available documents — primarily administrative amendments extending deadlines, past performance forms, and a Q&A template — do not include the full SIR text with explicit CLIN-level pricing types. However, sufficient context exists to make a reasonable assessment. The predominant requirement is for commercial products (COTS hardware and software) sold at catalog or market prices. IDIQ contracts with fixed-price task/delivery orders are the standard, well-accepted, and FAR-endorsed vehicle for this type of strategic sourcing. The LPTA evaluation approach further confirms that pricing is expected to be firm and comparable across offerors, consistent with a fixed-price structure at the order level. The inclusion of some product-based services (asset recovery, warehousing, deployment) introduces modest variability, but these are ancillary to the core product procurement and are described as performed 'as requested by the Contracting Officer on individual delivery orders,' suggesting they will be scoped and priced at the order level. There is no indication of a T&M or cost-reimbursement structure being applied to work that is commercially predictable, nor of a fixed-price structure being imposed on genuinely uncertain or open-ended development work. The structure is appropriate for the requirements. No square peg concern is warranted.
Evidence Quotes
The FAA intends to competitively award approximately eight (8) multiple-award Indefinite Delivery Indefinite Quantity (IDIQ) contracts under this SIR.
The vehicle is an IDIQ, which is not itself a pricing type but a contract structure. IDIQ contracts are a standard and appropriate vehicle for strategic sourcing of commercial IT hardware and software, where the government cannot predict exact quantities or timing of orders in advance.
The Government will make awards using a Lowest Price Technically Acceptable (LPTA) approach with a two-pool evaluation process.
LPTA is an evaluation methodology, not a contract pricing type. However, its use in conjunction with an IDIQ for commercial products (IT hardware, software, peripherals) is consistent with a fixed-price approach at the order level, which is appropriate for commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) product procurement where prices are catalog-based and well-defined.
This SIR is for Commercial Products (Hardware (HW) and Software (SW))... products and services which include, but are not limited to, software, network infrastructure components (servers, switches, storage arrays, and network devices), personal computing devices (workstations, laptops, tablets, thin clients, notebooks, and similar items)...
The predominant scope is commercial products with established market prices. Fixed-price ordering under an IDIQ is the textbook-appropriate pricing structure for this type of acquisition. There is no mismatch between the pricing approach and the nature of the goods being procured.
