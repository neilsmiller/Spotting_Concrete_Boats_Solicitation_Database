Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The solicitation designates a Firm-Fixed-Price (FFP) contract type through the Contract Administration Plan. VAMOSC (Visibility and Management of Operating and Support Costs) work — which appears to be a financial management and systems governance support effort based on the NAICS code (541330, Engineering Services) and the internal Q&A references to governance LCATs, SAP/S4 HANA considerations, and financial systems — is inherently labor-intensive professional services work with variable workloads and evolving requirements. FFP is most appropriate when requirements are well-defined, scope is stable, and costs can be estimated with confidence. Here, several signals cut against that: (1) the government's own internal comments acknowledge uncertainty about the labor mix and role structure; (2) there is no predecessor contract providing cost history; (3) LCAT and scope elements appear to have been carried over from other task orders without deliberate tailoring; and (4) the program office expressed uncertainty about fundamental aspects of the requirement (e.g., whether key personnel are needed, what the PM role looks like). A labor-hour or T&M contract would better accommodate this type of advisory and financial systems support work where the quantity of effort is difficult to predict. That said, the severity is assessed as moderate (2) rather than severe (3) because: FFP on SeaPort task orders for professional services is common and sometimes workable when LCATs and rates are well-structured; the IDIQ vehicle provides some flexibility in how individual task orders are scoped; and the solicitation does establish a CDRL and COR oversight structure that provides some performance accountability. The mismatch is real but not unambiguous enough to call it a clear error that most experts would unanimously condemn — it is a defensible but questionable choice given the evident uncertainty in scope definition.
