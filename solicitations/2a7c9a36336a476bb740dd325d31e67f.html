Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The solicitation describes ongoing engineering support services — maintenance, troubleshooting, installation, training, and programmatic support — across more than ten ship classes. This is a prototypical labor-hour or T&M requirement: the work is continuous and event-driven, the quantity of labor needed at any given time is inherently unpredictable, and the government is purchasing expert capacity rather than defined deliverables. The description does not explicitly state a contract type, and no attachments were included. However, the nature of the requirement (broad engineering and maintenance support, 'as required' tasking across a complex fleet) strongly implies that a T&M or labor-hour vehicle would be the appropriate and expected vehicle. There is no indication from the description that the government is attempting to use a fixed-price structure for inherently uncertain work, nor is there any suggestion of a cost-reimbursement approach for a task that doesn't warrant it. The description is consistent with standard NSWC engineering support contracts, which are routinely awarded as IDIQ vehicles with T&M or labor-hour task orders. Without explicit contract type language or attached SOW/PWS detail, there is not enough evidence to flag a pricing mismatch. The apparent fit between the requirement type and the implied contract structure warrants a severity 1 — the contract type appears appropriate for the requirements as described.
Evidence Quotes
The contractor shall provide technical, logistical, programmatic, training, field engineering, cybersecurity, and administrative services required for development, fielding, and maintenance of NSWCPD LCS, LPD, LSM, LHD, LSD, DDG, CVN, LHA, FFG, DDG1000 and CG Ship Control System projects.
The scope of work encompasses a broad, multi-disciplinary set of services — technical, logistical, programmatic, training, cybersecurity, and administrative — across a large and varied fleet of ship classes. This type of labor-intensive, ongoing engineering support work, where the quantity and mix of labor required at any given time cannot be predicted with precision, is a classic use case for T&M or labor-hour contracting.
These services shall include support for land and shipboard based hardware and testing, program management support, shipboard installation, testing, and troubleshooting support (including at-sea troubleshooting and testing), waterfront technical, logistical and training support, and administrative support, as required to maintain and upgrade designated shipboard systems for the U.S. Navy.
The phrase 'as required' signals that demand is event-driven and variable rather than fixed and predictable. At-sea troubleshooting and testing in particular is inherently unpredictable in scope and duration. These characteristics are consistent with T&M or labor-hour contract structures, which are well-suited to maintenance, repair, and field engineering support where the extent of work cannot be estimated in advance.
