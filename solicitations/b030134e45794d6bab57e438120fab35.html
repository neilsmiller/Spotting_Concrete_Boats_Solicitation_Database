Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation presents a moderate square peg concern. The FFP Requirements contract structure is being applied to a mix of work types: (1) hardware procurement and provisioning, where FFP with unit prices is entirely appropriate and standard; and (2) ongoing engineering support, maintenance, configuration management, and technical data updates, where the scope and labor intensity are inherently unpredictable — a context that typically warrants T&M or labor-hour pricing. The FAR guidance is clear that FFP should be used when risk can be estimated with acceptable certainty, but complex C4I system sustainment — especially for specialized military platforms like the MMSC with proprietary data links (Link-11, Link-16) — involves variable, hard-to-predict support demands. A T&M component for labor-intensive support services would better align incentives and protect the government from overpaying for underdelivered effort, or from needing costly modifications when unanticipated engineering challenges arise. That said, the severity is moderated to 2 rather than 3 for several reasons: (a) the Requirements contract structure does allow for indefinite quantities, which provides some flexibility; (b) this appears to be a follow-on contract (N6523618D1002), meaning the government and contractor have historical performance data that could inform more accurate fixed pricing; (c) the parts/equipment provisioning component of the work is genuinely well-suited to FFP unit pricing; and (d) the solicitation does not provide enough detail about how the services CLINs are structured to definitively confirm the mismatch. The absence of a SOW or PWS limits full assessment. A mixed contract type — FFP for deliverable hardware and T&M for labor-intensive support services — would have been a stronger fit for this requirement.
Evidence Quotes
NIWC Atlantic intends to award, on a sole source basis, a Firm Fixed Price, Requirements contract to Ultra Advance Tactical Systems Intelligence and Communications (Ultra)
The solicitation explicitly states this will be a Firm Fixed Price (FFP) Requirements contract. Under FAR, FFP is appropriate when risk can be predicted with acceptable certainty. However, this is a sustainment and maintenance contract for complex military C4I systems, which typically involves unpredictable repair and support demands — a context where T&M or labor-hour contracts are more commonly appropriate.
comprehensive support, maintenance, procurement and provisioning of equipment necessary to ensure the continued operational effectiveness of the Multi-Data Link Management Systems (MDLMS), Air Defense System Integrator, and associated peripheral devices
The scope includes open-ended 'support and maintenance' of complex defense electronics systems. Maintenance and support work is inherently variable in labor demand — the number of failures, repair hours, and engineering interventions cannot be predicted with the precision FFP requires. This variability is a classic indicator that T&M or a hybrid approach would better allocate risk.
continuous updates to the technical data package, and the provisioning of expert engineering and technical support
'Continuous updates' and 'expert engineering and technical support' are effort-driven activities without a defined deliverable quantity. FFP is ill-suited for open-ended technical support because the government cannot easily scope the exact level of effort in advance, making fixed pricing difficult to set accurately and increasing the risk of either overpaying or forcing costly modifications.
ensuring a readily available supply of ancillary equipment, replacement parts, and test equipment to maintain peak system performance
This is a 'Requirements contract,' meaning the government will order parts and supplies as needed against an indefinite quantity. The FFP pricing for a Requirements contract is workable for the parts/equipment component (unit prices can be established), but combined with unpredictable labor-intensive support services, the overall structure creates tension — the support services component would normally warrant T&M pricing.
Authority for other than full and open competition is 10 USC 3204(a)(1) and FAR 6.302-1, Only One Responsible Source
The sole-source justification means there is no competitive pressure to keep the vendor honest on pricing. Under a FFP structure with a single incumbent who has deep proprietary knowledge, the government has limited ability to verify that the fixed prices accurately reflect actual costs. This amplifies the risk of overpayment that is normally mitigated by competition in FFP contracts.
