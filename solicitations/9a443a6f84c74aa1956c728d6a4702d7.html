Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?

Reasoning
This solicitation is for a commercial telecommunications circuit — a 100 Mbps Ethernet service between two Washington, DC locations — procured under NAICS 517121 (Wired Telecommunications Carriers). The pricing structure of monthly recurring charges (MRC) and non-recurring charges (NRC) is consistent with a firm-fixed-price commercial services contract, which is standard practice for bandwidth circuit acquisitions through DITCO. The contract type is well-matched to the requirements. A defined-bandwidth Ethernet circuit is a commodity telecommunications service with a well-understood commercial market, predictable costs, and stable scope. The government knows exactly what it wants (100 Mbps), the vendor knows how to price it, and competition among small business carriers (consistent with the small business set-aside and LPTA evaluation method) will keep pricing honest. There is no labor uncertainty, no evolving scope, and no technical ambiguity that would argue for T&M or cost-reimbursement pricing. The LPTA evaluation approach further reinforces that this is a routine commercial purchase where price is the primary differentiator among technically capable vendors. This is a textbook case where fixed-price commercial services pricing is appropriate. No square peg concern exists here.
