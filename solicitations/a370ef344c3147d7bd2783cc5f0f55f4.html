Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The solicitation describes an IDIQ for contract closeout and administrative support services under AbilityOne mandatory source rules. The underlying work — scanning documents, closing out contracts, destroying records, deobligating ULOs/UDOs — is largely routine and transactional. These are services where unit-level pricing (e.g., per contract closed, per document scanned) or FFP task orders would be appropriate, because the nature of the work is well understood even if total volume varies. An IDIQ structure handles volume variability appropriately, but the pricing type within task orders is not disclosed in the solicitation text, which prevents a definitive assessment. The moderate severity rating is driven by two concerns. First, the sole-source nature of the AbilityOne procurement eliminates competitive discipline, making it more important that the contract type incentivizes efficiency. If task orders are T&M or labor-hour rather than FFP-per-unit, the vendor faces no financial incentive to close contracts quickly or process documents efficiently. Second, for services as discrete and countable as these, T&M would be a poor fit — yet it is a common default in administrative support IDIQs, especially in sole-source contexts. However, because the solicitation does not explicitly state the task order pricing type, there is a real possibility that FFP per-unit pricing is intended, which would be appropriate. The severity is therefore 2 rather than 3: the concern is real and structurally grounded, but the solicitation is ambiguous rather than clearly wrong. A well-structured IDIQ with FFP task orders priced per closeout action would be a reasonable fit for these requirements.
Evidence Quotes
This solicitation is for a mandatory source procurement pursuant to the Javits-Wagner-O'Day Act (41 U.S.C. 8501-8506)...Proposals from organizations other than the AbilityOne participating nonprofit agency will not be evaluated.
This is a sole-source, no-competition procurement. With only one eligible vendor, there is no competitive pressure to keep prices honest or to drive efficient performance. This context makes the choice of contract type more consequential: without competition, the pricing structure must do more work to incentivize efficiency.
The contractor will perform non-inherently governmental functions in support of contract completion and closeout, including auto-closeout actions, digital imaging (scans) of contracts and acquisition-related documentation, and document destruction.
The core tasks — auto-closeout, scanning, document destruction — are largely routine, repetitive, and well-understood administrative services. This suggests the scope and unit costs are estimable, which would favor a fixed-price or unit-price approach rather than T&M or cost-reimbursement.
The Department of War (DoW) requires efficient and effective contract management support for contract closeout services...deobligation of Unliquidated Obligations (ULOs) and Undelivered Orders (UDOs)
While individual tasks are routine, the volume of ULOs and UDOs to be processed is likely variable and dependent on the agency's internal workload, which is not fully predictable. This variability is a legitimate justification for an IDIQ structure, but the pricing type within task orders matters. If task orders are T&M, the government cannot control costs on what should be estimable, per-unit work.
AbilityOne Department of War Contract Management Services IDIQ
The vehicle is an IDIQ, which is appropriate for variable-volume service delivery. However, the solicitation provides no information about the pricing type to be used on task orders (FFP per closeout action, T&M, or labor-hour). For a service where outputs are discrete and countable (number of contracts closed, documents scanned), FFP per unit or FFP task orders would align incentives better than T&M. The absence of stated contract type prevents confirmation of alignment.
