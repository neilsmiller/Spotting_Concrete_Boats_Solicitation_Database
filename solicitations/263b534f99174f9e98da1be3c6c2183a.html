Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Insufficient Data 0 quotes ▾

⚠ Insufficient content to evaluate this sin — the solicitation lacked a substantive Statement of Work or attachments.

Reasoning
This solicitation consists of a brief commercial item acquisition (SF 1449 / SF 30 amendment) for AVEVA software license upgrades and one year of Customer FIRST support — a straightforward commercial product purchase, not a services contract. The attachments contain: (1) an SF 30 amendment extending the response date and updating evaluation criteria to require an OEM participation letter from third-party resellers; (2) an equipment list enumerating 21 specific software serial numbers; and (3) an item description memorandum specifying part numbers and quantities. There is no Statement of Work, Performance Work Statement, Quality Assurance Surveillance Plan, deliverables schedule, reporting requirements, or any other accountability structure that could be evaluated for compliance-over-outcomes patterns. The solicitation is a one-time commercial product purchase — licenses and support — with evaluation criteria limited to technical acceptability (can the vendor supply the specified part numbers) and price. There is simply no content from which to assess whether QA processes measure process compliance versus actual performance. The sin is not present or absent; it is inapplicable to this transaction type.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Severe 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation represents a textbook case of insider rewards at maximum severity. The designation as a sole-source acquisition to Q-Mation, LLC eliminates competition by definition. The amendment — which affirmatively added the requirement that any third-party reseller obtain a participation letter from Q-Mation — goes further, installing the incumbent as a gatekeeper who must authorize any competitor's ability to quote. This creates a structural impossibility: a qualified vendor cannot compete without the express cooperation of the vendor they are competing against. The serial-number-specific nature of the license upgrades compounds the exclusion by requiring detailed knowledge of an existing license estate that only the incumbent would manage. There are no countervailing evaluation criteria that would allow a non-incumbent to compete on capability, technical approach, or price — the solicitation's structure forecloses competition entirely. While sole-source acquisitions are sometimes legally justified under FAR 6.302 (e.g., only one responsible source), the relevant question here is whether the solicitation's structure rewards incumbency in ways that go beyond what the legitimate sole-source justification requires. The gatekeeping participation-letter requirement is not a natural consequence of a sole-source determination — it is an additional layer that further cements incumbent control. Severity 3 is the only appropriate rating.
Evidence Quotes
This requirement is a sole source acquisition to Q-Mation, LLC.
The solicitation is explicitly designated as a sole source acquisition to a named vendor — Q-Mation, LLC. This is not a competitive procurement at all. The 'insider rewards' dynamic is at its most extreme: no other vendor can win regardless of capability, experience, or price.

Even if a competing reseller could theoretically submit a quote, they are required to obtain a participation letter from Q-Mation itself — the sole-source awardee. This means Q-Mation controls access to competition, giving the incumbent absolute gatekeeping authority over any potential rival. A qualified competing reseller cannot proceed without the incumbent's blessing.

This is institutionalized knowledge that only the incumbent managing these licenses would possess.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 1 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a narrow commercial item acquisition for specific AVEVA software license upgrades and one year of Customer FIRST support. The requirement is defined entirely by specific part numbers (DEVSTD-04-U-231, INTCH-06-U-231, CF-STANDRD-N-WW), quantities, and serial numbers tied to existing installed systems at Tinker AFB. There is essentially no 'how' being prescribed here — the solicitation defines exactly WHAT is needed (specific product upgrades for specific existing licenses) and nothing more. The nature of this procurement — proprietary commercial software license renewals/upgrades for an existing SCADA/HMI system — inherently limits the product description to specific part numbers. This is not sprawl; it is the appropriate way to procure commercial software where the 'outcome' is receiving valid license entitlements for specific products. The evaluation criteria focus on technical acceptability (can the vendor supply the exact products) and price, which is appropriate for this type of acquisition. The only mildly prescriptive element is the requirement for a written statement rather than a simple concurrence, and the OEM letter from Q-Mation for third-party resellers — both of which are reasonable administrative controls for a sole-source commercial item buy, not requirement sprawl. The solicitation does not dictate implementation methods, staffing, workflows, or processes. Severity 1 is appropriate.
Evidence Quotes
Third-party resellers are required to submit a Third-Party Participation letter from QMation as part of their quote.
This is a process requirement for how third-party resellers must document their participation, but it exists in the context of a sole-source acquisition for specific commercial software licenses with fixed part numbers and serial numbers. It constrains documentation submission, not delivery approach. In context, this is a reasonable administrative control to verify authorized reseller status for proprietary software — not sprawl.

Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a straightforward commercial item procurement: specific AVEVA software license upgrades and a one-year standard support subscription, identified by part number, quantity, and serial number. The government has chosen a firm-fixed-price contract, which is exactly correct for this requirement. FAR Part 12 governs commercial item acquisitions, and FFP is the standard and preferred contract type under that authority. The scope is fully defined, the quantities are fixed, the deliverables are discrete (software licenses delivered to a specific location), and there is no labor uncertainty. There is no mismatch between the contract type and the requirements. Severity 1 — no square peg issue present.
