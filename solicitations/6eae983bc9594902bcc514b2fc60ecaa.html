Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?

⚠ Insufficient content to evaluate this sin — the solicitation lacked a substantive Statement of Work or attachments.

Reasoning
This is a 3-page pre-solicitation Notice of Intent (NOI) for a sole-source award. The document contains only a purchase description listing the types of IRB review activities required (initial and continuing review, amendments, reportable events, closeout, etc.) and salient characteristics focused on regulatory compliance capabilities. There is no Statement of Work, Performance Work Statement, Quality Assurance Surveillance Plan, deliverables list, reporting requirements, meeting cadences, or any other accountability structure that would allow meaningful assessment of whether the solicitation measures process compliance versus outcomes. The document is essentially a notice that NHLBI intends to award to Advarra, Inc. for continuity of IRB services — the substantive contract terms, performance standards, and QA mechanisms that would be needed to evaluate this sin are entirely absent. Accordingly, there is insufficient content to assess Sin #2.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Insufficient Data 0 quotes ▾

⚠ Insufficient content to evaluate this sin — the solicitation lacked a substantive Statement of Work or attachments.

Reasoning
This is a pre-solicitation Notice of Intent (NOI) to award a sole source contract, not a competitive solicitation with evaluation criteria, experience requirements, or personnel qualification standards. The document is three pages and contains no past performance thresholds, no evaluation criteria sections, no key personnel qualifications, and no scoring rubrics — the structural elements where insider rewards patterns are typically embedded. There is insufficient content to assess Sin #4 in the conventional sense. That said, the document is itself a sole source justification targeting Advarra, Inc. directly. The rationale given — continuity of oversight, existing reliance agreements, and study history — reflects a legitimate operational dependency rather than an artificial experience barrier constructed to exclude competitors. The government is not claiming Advarra is the only qualified IRB in existence; it is claiming that transitioning an active, regulated human subjects research study mid-stream to a different IRB would disrupt oversight and endanger subject safety. This is a substantively different situation from a solicitation that constructs narrow experience requirements to funnel award to an incumbent. IRB continuity for active IDE/IND studies is a genuine regulatory constraint: FDA and OHRP frameworks create real switching costs when a study is ongoing. A new IRB would need to re-review all active protocols, re-establish reliance agreements with all participating sites, and potentially introduce gaps in oversight — a non-trivial risk in federally regulated clinical research. The document does invite capability statements from other vendors, which is procedurally appropriate for a sole source notice under FAR 13.106-1(b)(1). There are no stacked experience thresholds, federal-specific mandates, or scoring criteria to evaluate here. Accordingly, sufficient_content is set to false, severity is 1, and the analysis cannot proceed further without a competitive solicitation containing evaluation and qualification criteria.

Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation does not exhibit the Rule Pools sin. The entire regulatory citation landscape is minimal and well-targeted: 45 CFR 46 and 21 CFR 56 are the two foundational federal frameworks that govern institutional review boards for human subjects research and FDA-regulated clinical studies respectively — precisely the work being solicited. The acquisition procedural authorities (FAR Parts 12 and 13) are appropriate and concise. There is no 'Applicable Documents' bulk list, no boilerplate accumulation, no irrelevant policies, and no contradictions. A vendor reading this solicitation can immediately understand the compliance landscape. The document is a three-page pre-solicitation notice for a straightforward sole-source continuation of IRB services, and its regulatory citations match that scope. If anything, the brevity and focus of the regulatory language is a strength of this document. Severity 1 is appropriate.
Evidence Quotes
Compliance with all applicable federal regulations, including 45 CFR 46 and 21 CFR 56
The solicitation cites only two specific regulations — 45 CFR 46 (the Common Rule for human subjects protection) and 21 CFR 56 (FDA's IRB regulations) — both of which are directly and unambiguously applicable to the exact service being procured. This is the opposite of a rule pool: it is a focused, purposeful citation of the two regulatory frameworks that define what an IRB must do and how it must operate for FDA-regulated clinical studies.
This acquisition is conducted under the procedures as prescribed in FAR Part 12—Acquisition of Commercial Products and Commercial Services and FAR Part 13—Simplified Acquisition Procedures.
The solicitation references only two FAR parts governing the acquisition procedure itself, both appropriate for the contract type and value. There is no accumulation of additional agency-specific policy layers on top of the standard FAR framework.
