Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is for a straightforward commercial telecommunications service: installation and ongoing maintenance of a 1Gbps internet connection at a New Jersey location. The pricing structure of monthly recurring charges and non-recurring charges is the standard commercial model for ISP services and is entirely consistent with a fixed-price contract vehicle. The requirement is well-defined (specific bandwidth threshold), the performance metric is objective and measurable, and the market is mature with multiple commercial providers. There is no scope uncertainty, no unpredictable labor quantity, and no evolving technical requirements that would make T&M or cost-reimbursement more appropriate. The LPTA evaluation method further reinforces that this is a commodity purchase where price competition on a defined specification is the right approach. This is a near-ideal use case for fixed-price contracting. No square peg concern is present.
Evidence Quotes
All quoted prices shall be identified as monthly recurring charges and/or non-recurring charges.
This pricing structure — monthly recurring charges (MRC) and non-recurring charges (NRC) — is the standard commercial pricing model for telecommunications services. It maps directly to a fixed-price vehicle: the government pays a predictable monthly rate for a defined service level, with one-time charges for installation or equipment. This is the textbook appropriate use of fixed-price contracting.

Fixed-price contracting is clearly appropriate here — there is no ambiguity in scope, no uncertainty in labor quantity, and no evolving requirements that would warrant T&M or cost-reimbursement.
