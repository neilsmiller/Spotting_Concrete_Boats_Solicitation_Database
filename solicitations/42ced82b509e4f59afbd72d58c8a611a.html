Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Minimal 0 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a pre-solicitation notice for a sole-source award to INSOURCE SOFTWARE SOLUTIONS, INC. for renewal of AVEVA Wonderware Customer FIRST Premium Level Software Maintenance. The justification for sole source is that INSOURCE SOFTWARE SOLUTIONS is identified as the sole provider of this specific software maintenance renewal. The Insider Rewards sin concerns experience requirements, evaluation criteria, or knowledge expectations that exclude non-incumbent vendors. In this case, the solicitation contains no experience requirements, evaluation criteria, or personnel qualification thresholds whatsoever — it is a simple maintenance renewal for a proprietary software product. There are no dollar thresholds, federal experience mandates, lookback windows, or stacked criteria of any kind. The sole-source rationale here is not incumbency capture in the Insider Rewards sense — it reflects the nature of software maintenance agreements, where the authorized reseller or vendor program (AVEVA Wonderware Customer FIRST) is the only entity that can provide the official maintenance renewal for a specific license agreement (Agreement #39049). This is analogous to requiring an OEM for parts covered under a warranty program. The limitation on competition stems from the proprietary nature of the product, not from experience requirements designed to favor an incumbent system integrator. The two-day response window for capability statements is very short and could theoretically discourage challengers, but given the proprietary software maintenance context, there is genuinely no realistic alternative vendor who could provide AVEVA Wonderware Customer FIRST Premium maintenance — this is not a case of artificially constructed requirements excluding capable competitors. There are simply no competing maintenance providers for this specific software product. Overall, this solicitation does not exhibit the Insider Rewards pattern. Severity is assessed at 1.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 0 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a presolicitation notice for a commercial software maintenance renewal — specifically, the renewal of AVEVA Wonderware Customer FIRST Premium Level Software Maintenance for a defined site at NASA Marshall Space Flight Center. The SOW is extremely brief (essentially a part number, a description, a place of performance, and a period of performance) and does not prescribe any processes, staffing structures, workflows, or step-by-step methods. The 'requirement' is the renewal of an existing software maintenance agreement for a named software product at a named facility — that is the outcome, and it is fully captured by the part number and agreement reference. There is no language telling the vendor how to perform the maintenance, what procedures to follow, what roles to staff, or what tools to use. The solicitation does not dictate the solution space at all. The sole-source justification is that INSOURCE SOFTWARE SOLUTIONS, INC. is identified as the sole provider of this specific vendor's software maintenance — this is a constraint of the commercial product ecosystem, not requirement sprawl imposed by the government drafter. In short, there is effectively nothing here to sprawl: the document is a minimal, appropriate commercial item acquisition notice for a software renewal. No requirement sprawl is present, and severity is rated 1.
Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?

Reasoning
This solicitation is a narrow pre-solicitation notice for a straightforward commercial software maintenance renewal — specifically, a one-year renewal of AVEVA Wonderware Customer FIRST Premium Level Software Maintenance for a single site at NASA Marshall Space Flight Center. The SOW is exceptionally minimal: it identifies the part number, describes the scope (maintenance renewal for software used in a test stand control room), specifies place of performance, and sets the period of performance. No regulatory compliance section, no 'Applicable Documents' list, no referenced standards, and no compliance frameworks are cited beyond the boilerplate reference to FAR Part 12 (appropriate for commercial item acquisitions) and NASA FAR Supplement Clause 1852.215-84 (a standard ombudsman clause). The only regulatory citations present are genuinely applicable and contextually appropriate: FAR 12.102(a) justifies the sole-source authority and commercial item treatment, and the ombudsman clause is a standard NASA requirement. There is no evidence of bulk regulatory listing, copy-forward accumulation, irrelevant policy citations, or undifferentiated compliance burden. For a software maintenance renewal of this scope and simplicity, this level of regulatory citation is appropriate and well-curated. No rule pool problem exists here.
Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 3 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is for a commercial software maintenance renewal — AVEVA Wonderware Customer FIRST Premium Level — for a single site over a fixed one-year period. The requirement is as well-defined as any procurement can be: a named vendor, a named product tier, a named agreement number, a single delivery location, and a fixed performance period. The cost of such renewals is typically set by the software publisher's price list and is not subject to labor variability or scope uncertainty. A firm-fixed-price contract is the natural and appropriate vehicle for this type of commercial item acquisition, and FAR Part 12 procedures (cited in the notice) are designed precisely for this context. There is no evidence of a contract type mismatch. The solicitation does not explicitly state the pricing type, but the commercial-item framing, the catalog-style part number, and the fixed-term structure all point unambiguously to FFP. No square peg concern exists here.
