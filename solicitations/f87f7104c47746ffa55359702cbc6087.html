Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The contract type (Firm-Fixed Price) is well-matched to the requirement. Telephone switchboard operator services at a single VA medical center is a classic example of a stable, recurring labor service where the scope is knowable in advance: the facility has defined operating hours, a predictable call volume, and a known number of operator positions to staff. These characteristics allow vendors to estimate costs with reasonable accuracy and bid competitively, which is precisely when FFP is appropriate under FAR guidance. There is no indication of unpredictable workload fluctuation, evolving requirements, or undefined deliverables that would argue for T&M or cost-reimbursement. The NAICS code (561421 — Telephone Answering Services) also reflects a commercial service market where FFP is the norm. The solicitation is limited in detail (this is a pre-solicitation notice without the full RFQ or PWS attached), so a complete assessment of CLIN structure, performance periods, and staffing requirements is not possible. However, based on the available information, there is no credible basis for concluding that FFP is the wrong contract type here. Severity is 1 — minimal concern.
