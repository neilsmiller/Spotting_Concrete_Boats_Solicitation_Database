Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The solicitation uses Cost Plus-Fixed-Fee (CPFF) for records administration and management services in support of BUPERS/NPC Force Management. CPFF is appropriate when circumstances do not allow the agency to define requirements sufficiently for a fixed-price contract, or when uncertainties in contract performance prevent accurate cost estimation (FAR 16.301-2). Records administration and management, however, is a mature, well-understood service area. The labor categories involved — records specialists, analysts, administrative personnel — are identifiable in advance, and the general nature of the work (managing, organizing, and administering records for a large HR enterprise) is predictable. This profile more naturally fits a T&M contract (if the volume of work is uncertain but the type of work is known) or potentially FFP (if specific deliverables and workloads can be defined). CPFF transfers cost risk entirely to the government and provides the vendor no incentive to control costs or work efficiently, since the fixed fee is earned regardless. That said, the severity here is moderate (2) rather than severe (3) for several reasons: (1) the solicitation provides minimal SOW detail, so it is possible that the underlying performance work statement describes genuine technical uncertainty — for example, if MyNavyHR Enterprise records management involves complex, evolving system integration or undefined volumes of records across Navy commands, CPFF could be defensible; (2) Seaport-NxG accommodates CPFF task orders and the vehicle's terms provide some oversight structure; and (3) records management for a large enterprise like MyNavyHR could involve unpredictable scope changes driven by policy, organizational restructuring, or system migrations, which would support cost-reimbursement. The mismatch is real but the solicitation's brevity prevents a definitive determination that CPFF is clearly wrong. A T&M structure with defined LCATs would likely have been a better fit — providing labor-hour predictability while preserving flexibility on quantity — but the CPFF choice is not egregiously misaligned given the enterprise-scale, support-services context.

Unlike basic research or highly technically uncertain programs — the canonical use case for cost-reimbursement — records management services typically involve predictable labor categories (clerks, analysts, records specialists) performing defined, routine tasks. This raises a question about whether CPFF is the right vehicle.
NAICS Code: 541611
NAICS 541611 covers Administrative Management and General Management Consulting Services. Services in this category can range from highly advisory/uncertain engagements to routine administrative support. The records management context here leans toward the latter — predictable, recurring labor — which would favor T&M (if labor quantities are uncertain) or even FFP (if scope is well-defined), rather than full cost-reimbursement.
This requirement is 100% Women Owned Small Business (WOSB) set aside amongst Seaport-NxG contract holders.
The Seaport-NxG vehicle is a multiple-award IDIQ primarily structured around T&M and cost-reimbursement task orders. While CPFF is permissible under Seaport-NxG, T&M is more commonly used for discrete, labor-intensive administrative support tasks where labor categories are known but quantities are uncertain — which better fits records management services than CPFF.
