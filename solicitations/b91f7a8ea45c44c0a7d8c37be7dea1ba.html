Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a straightforward DLA supply solicitation for four units of a NSN-identified digital computer system (NSN 7010-01-705-5751) from a qualified/restricted source (Northrop Grumman). The solicitation structure is purely transactional: quote a price, deliver the hardware by the required delivery date, meet packaging and marking standards, and submit a Counterfeit Prevention Plan within 30 days of contract award. There are no performance work statements requiring ongoing services, no QA surveillance plans, no reporting cadences, no prescribed status report formats, no required meetings, and no plan-adherence tracking mechanisms. The accountability structure is entirely product-acceptance oriented — the item either conforms to the NSN specification and passes origin inspection, or it does not. The SOW attached is focused exclusively on packaging and marking requirements (MIL-STD-2073-1, MIL-STD-129, etc.), not on ongoing performance reporting. The Counterfeit Prevention Plan CDRL is a single one-time deliverable appropriate to the supply chain risk context. This solicitation does not exhibit compliance-over-outcomes pathology because there is no ongoing service relationship to monitor — it is a buy of hardware. Sin #2 is essentially inapplicable to this acquisition type. Severity is 1 (minimal).

Insider Rewards

Reasoning
This solicitation is a DLA Aviation Request for Quotation for 4 units of NSN 7010-01-705-5751, a digital computer system (JTE), from a qualified/restricted source: Northrop Grumman (CAGE 1L4J7). The solicitation is a straightforward supply procurement with firm-fixed pricing evaluated solely on price. The Section M evaluation criteria state explicitly that 'All evaluation factors other than cost or price, when combined, are Significantly Less Important than Cost or Price' — and the only listed evaluation factor is price. There are no experience requirements, past performance thresholds, federal-specific experience mandates, dollar-value minimums for prior contracts, or lookback windows of any kind. The solicitation contains no elements that would function as insider rewards in the Niskanen Center framework. The one feature that limits competition — restriction to Northrop Grumman as the only qualified source — is a qualified products/approved source restriction, not an experience-based insider reward. This reflects a legitimate sole-source or restricted competition determination based on the technical/manufacturing qualification of the item (a specific NSN part), which is a distinct procurement mechanism from experience requirements that benefit incumbents. DLA regularly restricts hardware procurements to approved manufacturers for safety, interoperability, and supply chain integrity reasons that have nothing to do with rewarding a prior service contractor. The SOW addresses only preservation, packaging, and marking requirements — logistical and technical standards with no experience criteria attached. Note: This solicitation was subsequently canceled and replaced by SPRHA526R0382, so its practical impact is moot, but as written it presents no insider rewards problem. Severity is 1.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a straightforward DLA supply procurement for 4 units of a specific NSN item (7010-01-705-5751, a Northrop Grumman digital computer system) from a qualified/restricted source. It is a firm-fixed-price purchase of a specific manufactured product, not a services contract where requirement sprawl characteristically appears. The solicitation's 'Statement of Work' is entirely devoted to packaging, marking, and shipping requirements — prescribing MIL-STD-2073-1, MIL-STD-129, MIL-STD-647, MIL-STD-147, and related standards. These are not sprawl. They are mandatory DoD logistics infrastructure requirements that exist to ensure supply chain integrity, warehouse compatibility, and safe transport of military materiel. Every contractor selling into the DoD supply chain must meet these standards regardless of the specific contract, and they represent 'what must be achieved' (conforming packaging and marking) rather than 'how the vendor should run their business.' The core acquisition requirement is simply: deliver 4 each of part number 1320-5630003-01 (Cage 1L4J7) by 21 May 2027, packaged and marked to military standards. There is no services scope, no staffing structure, no workflow being prescribed, no incumbent process being replicated. The single CDRL deliverable is a Counterfeit Prevention Plan — a reasonable supply chain assurance document for a restricted-source electronic item. The solicitation is also already canceled and replaced by SPRHA526R0382, which is noted in the amendment. Even as written, this is a competently scoped product procurement. Requirement sprawl at any meaningful severity requires a solicitation that constrains vendor solution space across a services or complex delivery scope — that is not present here. Severity 1 is appropriate.

Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This is a DLA supply solicitation for 4 units of a single, qualified-source digital computer system (NSN 7010-01-705-5751, restricted to Northrop Grumman). The core acquisition is administratively straightforward. The solicitation itself (Sections B through M) follows standard DLA boilerplate with FAR/DFARS clauses incorporated by reference — this is normal and not a rule pool concern. However, the SOW attachment, which governs packaging and marking, presents a moderate rule pool problem. It lists approximately 17 distinct standards and regulatory documents in a master-template style, several of which (ISPM-15 for wood pallets in international trade, ICAO/IATA/IMDG hazardous materials codes, MIL-STD-648 for specialized container design) have no evident connection to shipping 4 computer units domestically from a single domestic source to DLA Distribution Barstow. The hazardous materials compliance section is the clearest example: it requires adherence to four international dangerous goods regimes without establishing that the item is actually hazardous. This is characteristic of copy-forward accumulation — a packaging SOW template applied without review for applicability to the specific item. The severity is moderate (2) rather than severe (3) because: (1) the main solicitation body's clause citations are standard FAR/DFARS incorporations by reference, which is normal practice; (2) the rule pool is confined to the packaging SOW rather than pervading the entire solicitation; (3) many of the packaging standards cited (MIL-STD-2073-1, MIL-STD-129, MIL-STD-130) are genuinely applicable to DoD materiel procurement; and (4) the acquisition is restricted to a single qualified source, meaning the pool of vendors affected is narrow. A potential bidder familiar with DLA packaging requirements would recognize the template nature of the SOW and know which standards practically apply. This is elevated boilerplate accumulation, not a severe undifferentiated compliance avalanche.

Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a simple supply procurement for 4 units of a specific, named digital computer system (NSN 7010-01-705-5751) from a single qualified source (Northrop Grumman). The contract type is Firm Fixed Price, and CLIN 0001 is the only priced line item. CLIN 0002 (Counterfeit Prevention Plan) is Not Separately Priced and is a documentation deliverable tied to the hardware purchase. FFP is the standard and appropriate contract type for a discrete hardware buy where the item, quantity, manufacturer, and part number are all fully specified. There is no labor-hour ambiguity, no undefined scope, and no evolving requirements — the government is simply purchasing a known item for delivery to a specified location by a specified date. The SOW is entirely focused on packaging, marking, and shipping standards, which are deterministic requirements that do not introduce scope uncertainty. No mismatch between contract type and requirements is present. This is a severity 1 finding.
