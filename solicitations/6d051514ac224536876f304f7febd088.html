Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a straightforward commercial software maintenance and support contract for a specific proprietary medical imaging platform (MIM Software) at a single VA facility. The accountability structure is appropriately minimal for the nature of the work. The primary QA mechanism is the service report submitted after each maintenance or repair event — a standard deliverable for maintenance contracts that ties documentation to actual service events rather than to a recurring reporting calendar. There are no weekly status reports, no monthly progress reports, no prescribed multi-section report formats, no overlapping reporting cadences, and no plan-adherence tracking requirements. The solicitation does include defined response time standards — 'virtual technical support will respond within one (1) hour' and 'onsite response...will be within four (4) hours' — which are lightweight but genuine performance metrics (SLA-style commitments). There are no quality assurance surveillance plan provisions, no waterfall-style schedule tracking, and no meeting frequency mandates beyond normal check-in and badging requirements. The security and IT compliance sections are standard VA boilerplate that appears across virtually all VA contracts; they serve regulatory and data protection purposes rather than functioning as QA mechanisms for software maintenance performance. The absence of explicit outcome metrics (e.g., software uptime, system availability, clinical workflow impact) is a minor gap, but given that this is a commercial software maintenance subscription — where the product's performance is largely defined by the OEM's standard service terms — the lack of elaborate outcome measurement is not anomalous. The solicitation does not substitute compliance documentation for outcome accountability; it simply relies on the implicit outcome of 'software functions as intended after service.' Overall, this solicitation shows no meaningful compliance-over-outcomes pattern. Severity 1 is appropriate.
Evidence Quotes
A service report submitted after each act of maintenance or repair via hardcopy
This is the primary documentation requirement in the solicitation. It is a deliverable-tied report — produced when something actually happens (maintenance or repair) — rather than a recurring status report tracking process adherence. It is closer to an acceptance/completion record than compliance theater.
Document of necessary repairs accomplished (service report) will be forwarded when the service is completed, and equipment is returned to use. This shall be completed within 14 days from completion of service.
The 14-day deadline for service reports and the condition 'equipment is returned to use' is a mild outcome indicator — the report is tied to equipment being restored to operational status, not merely to the performance of a procedure. This is not a strong outcome metric, but it is not purely process-tracking either.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation exhibits a moderate-to-elevated insider rewards pattern driven primarily by the nature of the requirement itself rather than explicit past-performance thresholds or lookback windows. The OEM personnel requirement and the manufacturer authorization letter requirement together function as a dual gating mechanism: a non-MIM Software vendor cannot compete without MIM Software's blessing, and personnel must meet the manufacturer's own standards. These requirements are partially justified — this is a sole-source-adjacent maintenance and support contract for proprietary software already installed at the facility, and it is genuinely difficult to service MIM Software's proprietary imaging tools without OEM access to patches, updates, and technical documentation. However, the solicitation does not pursue a formal brand-name justification or J&A, instead using competitive procedures while simultaneously imposing requirements that only MIM Software or its approved partners can meet. The absence of any experience thresholds, dollar floors, or federal-specific experience mandates keeps this from severity 3 — there are no 'five prior federal contracts exceeding $X' type requirements. The field is not entirely closed; authorized resellers can and do compete on SAM-priced contracts. But the manufacturer retains effective veto power over who enters the competition, which is the functional hallmark of insider capture. Severity 2 is appropriate: the playing field is materially tilted, but the mechanism here is proprietary software dependency rather than explicit stacked incumbency criteria.
Evidence Quotes
Contractor Experience Requirements—Key Personnel: Contractor personnel shall be OEM. All tasks shall be accomplished in accordance with the manufacturer's standards.
Requiring personnel to be OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) representatives means only MIM Software's own technicians or their explicitly authorized representatives can fulfill the contract. This is not a general capability requirement — it is a direct restriction to a single vendor's workforce or their designated channel partners.
To submit a responsive quote, please submit the following: ... Authorized letter of provider from MIM Software if your company is not MIM Software.
This requirement mandates that any non-MIM Software offeror obtain an authorization letter from MIM Software itself before competing. MIM Software controls who is 'authorized' and can therefore control who competes against it. A qualified third-party service provider cannot independently establish their eligibility — they are gated by the manufacturer's approval.
This evaluation will be based on price. ... This is a Low Price solicitation where a single Offeror will be selected who is deemed responsive and responsible in accordance with the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) 9.104-1, whose offer conforms to the RFQ requirements, offers the lowest price...
While a pure lowest-price evaluation theoretically opens competition, the combination of the OEM personnel requirement and the manufacturer authorization letter requirement means the only realistic offerors are MIM Software itself or MIM-authorized resellers. Price competition is structurally limited to the set of vendors MIM Software chooses to authorize.
The Price/Cost Schedule references proprietary item codes throughout: MIM-SQ-AD-SAM, MIM-SP-CC-SAM, MIM-SM-CC-SAM, SAM-MSMU-YL01*MIM-EN-CC, SAM-MSMU-YL01*MIM-SSSTSU, SAM-MSMU-YL01*MIM-NP-AD, 4DM-PRE-CC-SAM, MIM-DX-CC-SAM
Every line item is defined by MIM Software-specific proprietary item codes. A vendor cannot substitute equivalent functionality or offer a competing product — the solicitation defines the requirement entirely in terms of one manufacturer's catalog. This forecloses any alternative supplier competition and anchors the market exclusively to MIM Software and its authorized channel.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation exhibits moderate requirement sprawl — severity 2. The core acquisition is a straightforward commercial software maintenance and support contract for a named proprietary product (MIM Software) with well-understood vendor services. For this type of acquisition, the appropriate approach would define SLAs, uptime/availability outcomes, and security compliance requirements, then let the OEM or authorized reseller propose their standard service delivery model. Instead, the SOW includes several passages that prescribe specific processes, communication channels, staffing characteristics, and administrative procedures. The most notable are: the mandate for telephone support as a channel (rather than specifying availability outcomes), the hardcopy service report requirement, the OEM personnel mandate (which goes beyond a qualification requirement), and the NFO parts shipping prescription. These collectively constrain the vendor's service delivery model in ways that are not driven by legal mandates, safety requirements, or interoperability constraints. However, the sprawl is not pervasive. The majority of the SOW's length is occupied by standard VA information security boilerplate (sections 2-9 on VA information custodial language, FISMA/HIPAA compliance, security incident investigation, liquidated damages, and training) — language that is legally mandated, not process sprawl. The SLA requirements (1-hour virtual response, 4-hour onsite response) are legitimate outcome-based specifications. The overall document is relatively compact for a multi-year software maintenance contract. The prescriptive elements that do exist appear to reflect copied operational procedures from a prior service arrangement (e.g., the specific room number for check-in, the hardcopy report requirement) rather than a pervasive drafting culture of over-specification. A vendor would feel meaningfully constrained in a few specific areas but would not face the kind of wholesale process dictation that warrants a severity 3 finding.
Evidence Quotes
Unlimited telephone technical support available M-F from 8:00am- 6:00pm PST. A mechanism for providing support outside of these hours if the situation requires it. This shall be determined by a VA Biomedical Engineer, Biomedical Equipment Support Specialist, or designee. When a call for service is placed, virtual technical support will respond within one (1) hours. Onsite response, once determined it is needed, will be within four (4) hours of the request notification.
These requirements mix legitimate SLAs (response times) with prescriptive channel and staffing mandates. Specifying 'unlimited telephone technical support' and requiring a specific VA staff role (Biomedical Engineer/BESS) to authorize after-hours support dictates HOW support is structured rather than simply defining availability and response outcomes. A vendor cannot substitute a ticketing system, chat-based triage, or automated escalation path — the channel (telephone) and the authorization chain are both locked in.
A service report submitted after each act of maintenance or repair via hardcopy
Requiring hardcopy service reports prescribes a specific delivery method when the outcome need is simply documentation of completed work. This precludes electronic service record systems, vendor portals, or digital signatures that would be functionally equivalent or superior. This is a process/format mandate with no apparent legal or safety justification.
Contractor personnel shall be OEM. All tasks shall be accomplished in accordance with the manufacturer's standards.
Requiring all contractor personnel to be OEM (original equipment manufacturer) staff dictates WHO performs the work rather than what qualifications or outcomes are needed. This entirely forecloses authorized resellers, third-party maintainers, or qualified independent service organizations from proposing alternative staffing models — even if they could meet identical performance standards. While OEM access to parts and technical knowledge is a legitimate concern, mandating OEM personnel status as the only acceptable approach is a prescriptive process requirement.
For any onsite Support, vendor must check in and out with a Biomedical Equipment Support Specialist or designee located in B1D106-100-P.
Specifying the exact room number (B1D106-100-P) and requiring check-in/check-out with a specific role prescribes a granular administrative procedure. While a general escort/access control requirement for a healthcare facility is a legitimate security constraint, embedding the specific physical location and mandatory role at this level of detail reflects operational procedure being written into contract requirements — limiting any flexibility in how site access coordination is managed.
Parts shall be shipped via overnight delivery and/or next flight out (NFO) as required if the situation is emergent.
This prescribes a specific logistics method (overnight/NFO shipping) rather than defining an outcome such as 'parts must be on-site within X hours for emergent situations.' A vendor with a regional parts depot or pre-positioned inventory could meet the same response outcome through a faster or more cost-effective mechanism, but the specification forecloses that by mandating a particular shipping method.
Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation presents a moderate rule-pool problem driven primarily by a boilerplate VA information security addendum that runs approximately nine pages and was clearly designed for IT system development or hosting contracts, not for OEM software maintenance and repair agreements. The core work here is straightforward: MIM Software provides periodic upgrades, telephone support, and on-site repairs for nuclear medicine imaging software already installed and operating on VA-owned equipment at a single hospital. The contractor does not host VA data, does not develop systems, and does not operate VA networks. Yet the SOW imposes the full VA IT security compliance stack — FISMA, HIPAA, FISMA C&A, Privacy Act SOR operations, contingency planning, annual self-assessments, POA&M processes, and liquidated damages for data breaches — without any analysis of which provisions actually apply to this limited maintenance scope. The most egregious example is the Internet Explorer 7/Windows XP certification requirement, which is a decade-obsolete carryover that no one has removed. The solicitation does not provide a dedicated 'Applicable Documents' list in the traditional sense (which mitigates the severity somewhat — there is no 88-item bulk dump), but the effect is achieved through undifferentiated full-text inclusion of a broadly scoped compliance framework. A vendor new to VA contracting cannot tell which of the nine security sections genuinely govern their maintenance activities versus which were written for a software development or data-hosting context. Mitigating factors: the solicitation is relatively short overall, the FAR clauses are incorporated by reference rather than reproduced in bulk, and the genuinely applicable security requirements (PHI access, background checks, incident notification) are legitimately present given that service engineers will occasionally encounter patient health information. The problem is over-inclusion and lack of curation, not the wholesale irrelevance of every cited requirement. Severity 2 is appropriate: the compliance burden is elevated and some inclusions are clearly stale or misapplied, but the solicitation does not reach the extreme of citing dozens of unrelated regulations with no connection to the work.
Evidence Quotes
Contractors, contractor personnel, subcontractors, and subcontractor personnel shall be subject to the same Federal laws, regulations, standards, and VA Directives and Handbooks as VA and VA personnel regarding information and information system security.
This is a sweeping catch-all clause that obligates vendors to comply with the full universe of VA laws, regulations, directives, and handbooks without specifying which ones apply to this maintenance-and-support contract for a nuclear medicine imaging software package. It provides no curated list and no guidance on scope.
For information systems that are hosted, operated, maintained, or used on behalf of VA at non-VA facilities, contractors/subcontractors are fully responsible and accountable for ensuring compliance with all HIPAA, Privacy Act, FISMA, NIST, FIPS, and VA security and privacy directives and handbooks. This includes conducting compliant risk assessments, routine vulnerability scanning, system patching and change management procedures, and the completion of an acceptable contingency plan for each system.
This section applies a full federal information-system hosting and operations compliance framework — including contingency plans, vulnerability scanning regimens, and C&A — to what is essentially a software maintenance and upgrade contract for an on-premises medical imaging tool at a single VA facility. The contract explicitly states 'No Government-owned property will be transferred into the Contractor's control' and that the contractor is granted access to government equipment to perform upgrades. Full FISMA/FedRAMP-level hosting obligations are plausibly inapplicable to this scope, yet are cited without any explanation of how they apply.
Outsourcing (contractor facility, contractor equipment or contractor staff) of systems or network operations, telecommunications services, or other managed services requires certification and accreditation (authorization) (C&A) of the contractor's systems in accordance with VA Handbook 6500.3, Certification and Accreditation and/or the VA OCS Certification Program Office.
C&A/authorization requirements under VA Handbook 6500.3 are cited without any analysis of whether this contract — for OEM software upgrades and repairs on existing government-owned MIM imaging equipment at a single hospital — actually triggers these obligations. For a vendor providing periodic software updates and on-call repair, the relevance of a full system certification and accreditation process is not explained.
The contractor/subcontractor shall certify to the COTR that applications are fully functional and operate correctly as intended on systems using the VA Federal Desktop Core Configuration (FDCC), and the common security configuration guidelines provided by NIST or the VA. This includes Internet Explorer 7 configured to operate on Windows XP and Vista (in Protected Mode on Vista) and future versions, as required.
This provision references Internet Explorer 7 on Windows XP and Vista — operating systems and a browser that Microsoft end-of-lifed in 2014 and 2016 respectively. Including an obsolete configuration standard without review or update is a textbook example of copy-forward accumulation, where compliance language is never pruned for continued relevance. It creates an absurd compliance burden (certifying compatibility with a decade-dead OS/browser combination) for a 2026 contract.
FAR Number: 852.239-70 | Title: SECURITY REQUIREMENTS FOR INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY RESOURCES | Date: FEB 2023 [incorporated by reference alongside 52.203-17, 52.204-13, 852.203-70]
VAAR 852.239-70 (Security Requirements for IT Resources) is incorporated by reference without any explanation of how its requirements interact with the detailed security provisions already embedded in the SOW across nine numbered sections covering information custodial language, system design, hosting, incident investigation, and liquidated damages. The result is an undifferentiated stack of overlapping security mandates — some in full text, some by reference — with no guidance on which governs when conflicts arise or which carry the most operational weight for this specific maintenance contract.
Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is for commercial software maintenance and support services — specifically annual upgrades, technical support, and repair coverage for a suite of MIM imaging software products used in nuclear medicine at Portland VAMC. The contract type is explicitly stated as Firm Fixed Price. FFP is the appropriate contract type here for several reasons: (1) The requirement is commercial software maintenance, a standard commercial service with well-established vendor pricing. MIM Software publishes catalog pricing (item codes are provided in each CLIN), so cost is predictable. (2) The scope is fixed: the SOW enumerates specific named software modules (Encore, SurePlan MRT, SurePlan Y90, Assistant, Neuro, Spectra Quant, Invia 4DM Premium, Symphony DX) and defines the service level in concrete terms — telephone support hours, onsite response times, software updates, and service reports. There is no open-ended research, evolving requirements, or uncertain labor quantity that would make T&M preferable. (3) The performance period is fixed at 12 months per base and option year, with clear start and end dates for each CLIN. (4) Annual software maintenance contracts for commercial medical imaging software are a textbook FFP use case: the vendor knows its cost structure, the government knows what it is buying, and competition (or catalog price verification) keeps pricing honest. No mismatch exists between the contract type and the requirements. This is a well-matched FFP acquisition.
Evidence Quotes
Type of Contract: Firm Fixed Price single-year full-service maintenance and repair
The SOW explicitly states the contract type as Firm Fixed Price. This is the correct pricing vehicle for commercial software maintenance and support services where the scope is well-defined and pricing is catalog-based.

Each CLIN corresponds to a specific, named commercial software product with a fixed annual subscription period. The deliverable — annual software maintenance and support for an enumerated set of MIM products — is clearly defined, and vendor pricing is catalog-driven, making cost estimation highly predictable.
