Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation exhibits moderate compliance-over-outcomes characteristics, earning a severity of 2. The most significant problem is the QA plan: it references an AQL but never defines one, leaving the accountability structure hollow. The remedy mechanism (written response + re-performance) is entirely process-focused. The task list is composed almost entirely of installation, scripting, and documentation activities with no performance targets — no model accuracy requirements, no student thesis outcomes, no research productivity measures beyond a raw volume count of figures and data. Mitigating factors prevent a severity 3 rating. First, the reporting burden is actually quite light — the solicitation does not impose weekly status reports, multiple overlapping reporting cadences, or prescribed report formats. There is no evidence of the meeting-heavy or paperwork-heavy compliance theater typical of the most severe cases. Second, the work is genuinely technical and research-oriented, and some of the deliverables (User's Manuals, analysis packages, coupled model outputs) have implicit quality standards embedded in their nature — a non-functional STRIKE35 installation would be self-evidently a failure even without an explicit AQL. Third, the scale of this contract (a small sole-source research support effort) means the absence of elaborate QA machinery is partly appropriate. The core problem is that the QA table — the primary vehicle for defining performance standards — appears to be substantively empty. Whatever AQL standards may exist are not visible in the solicitation text. This creates a situation where the government's primary accountability tool is checking whether tasks were performed and documentation was produced, with no defined standard for what 'good' looks like in terms of model performance, research support quality, or scientific output. This is a meaningful but not severe compliance-over-outcomes problem.

Submitting 100 figures to a journal is a compliance checkbox, not an outcome measure. The remedy for poor performance is re-performance of tasks — a process-compliance remedy. There is no mechanism to assess whether the underlying mission objective (supporting NPS thesis research and curriculum development) was achieved. The accountability structure is entirely built around task completion rather than research outcomes.

The task list is structured as a series of installation, development, and documentation activities. Success is implicitly defined as completing each task — installing software, writing scripts, producing user manuals — rather than whether the installed models actually function correctly, support student research, or produce scientifically valid outputs. The deliverables structure reinforces activity completion as the measure of performance.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Severe 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation represents a severe case of insider rewards, compounding to the point where the agency has formally invoked a sole source justification — which is itself the logical endpoint of the insider rewards cycle. The notice of intent names a specific incumbent (Global Resolutions, Inc.) and explicitly states that only this vendor possesses the required 'unique background knowledge, data access, and immediate familiarity' with the installed systems. The PWS then layers additional requirements — no ramp-up period, MOODS processing experience, STRIKE35 knowledge, and specific educational credentials — that individually might be defensible but collectively function as a description of the incumbent's resume rather than a capability standard. The five-day response window for capability statements further underscores that this process is performative: the bar for displacing the incumbent is set not at 'can you do the work' but at 'can you prove you have already been doing this exact work at NPS without any learning curve.' That standard cannot be met by any party other than the current contractor. The NAICS misclassification (Temporary Help Services for specialized oceanographic modeling) compounds the problem by misdirecting the solicitation away from the technical vendor community that might otherwise respond. This is a textbook example of the insider rewards cycle reaching its terminal stage: the government's institutional knowledge has fully migrated to the incumbent, the agency can no longer articulate requirements in vendor-neutral terms, and the procurement process has become a formality around a predetermined outcome.
Evidence Quotes
Global Resolutions, Inc. (GRI) is the only known source possessing the unique background knowledge, data access, and immediate familiarity with the STRIKE35/Delft3D models already installed at NPS required to fulfill this requirement without causing unacceptable delays to the academic calendar and ongoing naval operations research.
This is the most explicit possible form of insider rewards: the agency has formally declared that familiarity with the specific models already installed on the specific NPS computers is a prerequisite for performance. This isn't a capability requirement — it's a description of the incumbent's accumulated institutional knowledge. Any other vendor, regardless of general competence with STRIKE35 or Delft3D, is disqualified by definition because they lack 'immediate familiarity' with the specific installation at NPS.
Sources interested in responding to this notice are required to submit a capability statement that includes technical data and cost information, in sufficient detail and with convincing evidence that clearly demonstrates the capability to provide the required computational services without a transition or ramp-up period that would disrupt ongoing NPS research.
The 'no transition or ramp-up period' standard is a textbook insider reward criterion. Only a vendor who has already been doing this exact work can demonstrate zero ramp-up. This standard is structurally impossible for any non-incumbent to meet, regardless of their qualifications. It converts institutional familiarity — which only the incumbent possesses — into an explicit evaluation threshold.
Knowledge of underwater bomb trajectory prediction model, i.e., STRIKE35.
While knowledge of STRIKE35 is a legitimate technical requirement, combined with the 'already installed at NPS' context and the no-ramp-up standard, this effectively requires familiarity with a specific legacy model that only the current contractor has been working with in this specific environment. There is no open-market pool of STRIKE35 practitioners; this is a narrow, specialized model associated with a small number of practitioners, further concentrating the eligible vendor pool.
Five years of experience in applying statistical, dynamical, and numerical techniques to find solutions to a wide variety of oceanographic problems; in processing the Navy's Master Observational Oceanographic Data Set (MOODS); in using a wide range of PC and LYNUX based systems with MATLAB, FORTRAN and C languages sufficient to improve ocean models.
The requirement for specifically processing the 'Navy's Master Observational Oceanographic Data Set (MOODS)' is an institutional knowledge requirement. MOODS is a DoD-specific dataset; experience with it implies prior work on Navy research contracts. Combined with the STRIKE35 familiarity requirement and the no-ramp-up standard, this stacks a third insider-favoring criterion that narrows the eligible pool further toward anyone who has previously performed this or nearly identical work.
The applicable NAICS code is 561320: Temporary Help Services.
The use of NAICS 561320 (Temporary Help Services) for what is clearly a highly specialized scientific and computational research support contract is itself a signal of incumbent capture. This misclassification obscures the competitive landscape by categorizing the requirement under a staffing industry code rather than a research, engineering, or IT services code. A mismatched NAICS code suppresses responses from qualified technical vendors who would not be monitoring the staffing services category — further insulating the incumbent.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation exhibits moderate requirement sprawl. The PWS task list is largely structured as a step-by-step procedural decomposition — each numbered task describes a specific action (install X in room Y, update with Matlab scripts, develop input link from Z) rather than a desired capability or end state. A vendor reading this document has very little room to propose an alternative technical approach: the tools (Matlab, FORTRAN, Delft3D, STRIKE35), the implementation steps, and even the physical location of software installation are all specified. The solicitation reads more like a work instruction for an incumbent than a statement of need for a competitive market. However, several mitigating factors prevent a severity-3 rating. First, the prescriptiveness here is partially justified by legitimate technical interoperability constraints: STRIKE35 and Delft3D are specific government-relevant models already in use at NPS, and the requirement to install and integrate them with existing NPS infrastructure is a genuine constraint rather than arbitrary process mandating. The government cannot be outcome-neutral about which numerical model is used when the research program is built around those specific models. Second, the scope is technically narrow — this is specialized scientific computing support for a specific research program, not a broad services contract where process prescription would be more damaging. Third, the solicitation is also being issued as a sole source to an incumbent, which explains (though does not fully excuse) why the task list reads as a description of what the incumbent currently does rather than a performance-based outcome statement. The overall pattern is consistent with copy-paste from an incumbent's current work scope, where the 'how' of the last vendor has become the requirement for the next contract. A better-drafted PWS would specify the research capability outcomes (e.g., 'NPS researchers and thesis students shall have access to an integrated computational environment capable of predicting UXO burial depth in estuary environments') and let the contractor propose how to achieve them, with STRIKE35/Delft3D integration specified as a constraint rather than a step-by-step implementation recipe. As written, vendors can only comply with the prescribed approach, not propose improvements — but given the narrow, specialized, incumbent-driven nature of the work, this is moderately problematic rather than severely so.

Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation does not exhibit the Rule Pools sin. Rather than presenting a bulk 'Applicable Documents' list with dozens of undifferentiated regulatory citations, the PWS relies on a very small number of specific references: one NPS cybersecurity instruction (NPSINST-5239.1E), a Privacy Act citation, references to DoDD 3216.02 and DFARS 252.235-7004 in the context of human subjects research (narrowly scoped and conditionally applicable), and standard FAR/DFARS cross-references (Parts 7.5 and 37.1). Each citation that appears is either clearly contextual — explaining why it applies — or is standard federal contracting boilerplate. There is no 'Applicable Documents' section with a long enumerated list. There is no evidence of copy-forward accumulation of older or irrelevant policies. There are no citations of unrelated frameworks (e.g., energy efficiency standards, badge policies disconnected from the work). The regulatory footprint of this solicitation is notably lean for a Navy contract of this type, likely because the PWS is narrowly focused on technical computational support at a single NPS department with well-defined deliverables. The compliance burden a prospective vendor would face is transparent and limited. Severity is assessed at 1 — minimal.

Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation awards an FFP contract for a multi-year research and software development effort at NPS, where the contractor will support thesis students, update complex oceanographic and trajectory simulation models, and develop new coupled modeling capabilities. FFP is best suited to well-defined, stable scope where cost and schedule can be estimated with reasonable accuracy. Here, several factors cut against FFP: (1) the work is fundamentally research support, which is iterative and difficult to scope precisely; (2) the five-year period of performance means requirements will almost certainly evolve; (3) the sole-source justification emphasizes expertise and familiarity rather than a specific deliverable, which is the hallmark of a labor-hour or T&M contract; and (4) many individual tasks (e.g., developing a novel UXO penetration model, coupling multiple simulation systems) involve genuine technical uncertainty in level of effort. That said, the severity is assessed as moderate rather than severe for several reasons: the PWS does enumerate specific deliverables and tasks in reasonable detail, meaning the government has made an effort to define scope; FFP creates appropriate incentives for the contractor to work efficiently; and the relatively small scale of NPS research contracts may make the administrative overhead of T&M surveillance impractical. Contracting experts would note the mismatch, but the FFP choice is not indefensible — it is a second-best option that creates some risk of costly modifications if scope expands, rather than an obviously wrong choice.

The actual level of effort required to, for example, 'develop a projectile penetration model' or 'couple Delft3D with STRIKE35/UXO penetration model' is difficult to estimate in advance, which is a characteristic that tends to favor T&M or cost-reimbursement over FFP.

A five-year period of performance for research support and software development work that will evolve over time (including thesis student support and model updates) heightens the uncertainty concern. Over a five-year horizon, requirements are likely to change, making a rigid FFP structure potentially problematic and increasing the likelihood of costly contract modifications.

The work is explicitly tied to ongoing academic research and thesis student support at NPS. Research-support work by nature is iterative and evolving — the tasks will be shaped by what students and PIs discover as the research progresses — which is a poor fit for a FFP structure that assumes scope can be fully defined upfront.
Global Resolutions, Inc. (GRI) is the only known source possessing the unique background knowledge, data access, and immediate familiarity with the STRIKE35/Delft3D models already installed at NPS required to fulfill this requirement without causing unacceptable delays to the academic calendar and ongoing naval operations research.
The sole-source justification emphasizes the incumbent's unique expertise and knowledge, not delivery of a fixed product. When the government is essentially buying continued expert access and support rather than a defined deliverable, T&M or labor-hour contracts are typically the better fit. A FFP structure here may either overpay (if the contractor pads the fixed price to cover uncertainty) or underpay (if scope expands and modifications are needed).
