Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation uses firm-fixed-price (FFP), which is appropriate for the requirements described. The procurement covers two well-defined components: (1) a one-time installation of broadband infrastructure, and (2) recurring monthly Wi-Fi service. Both elements are standard commercial services offered by Charter/Spectrum on a predictable pricing basis. The scope is stable, the deliverable is measurable (functioning broadband connectivity), and costs are readily estimable because these are commercial catalog services. FFP correctly places performance risk on the vendor and gives the government cost certainty for what is essentially a utility-like service. T&M would be inappropriate because this is not a labor-intensive project with unpredictable quantities of work; cost-reimbursement would be inappropriate because there is no technical uncertainty. The FFP structure aligns well with the nature of the requirement. The primary concern with this solicitation is the sole-source justification and the very short 7-day response window, not the contract pricing type. No Square Peg issue is present here.

The Department of the Air Force, 4th Contracting Squadron (4 CONS) at Seymour Johnson Air Force Base (SJAFB), North Carolina, intends to award a sole source firm-fixed-price contract to Charter Communications Operating, LLC (doing business as Spectrum).
The solicitation explicitly states the contract type: firm-fixed-price (FFP). This is the relevant data point for the Square Peg analysis.
The objective of this procurement is to acquire commercial broadband internet installation and recurring monthly Wi-Fi services for Unaccompanied Housing facilities at SJAFB. The contractor shall provide all necessary labor, materials, equipment, and infrastructure required to install, configure, activate, and maintain high-speed Wi-Fi broadband services with tiered options for the designated facilities.
The requirements are for commercial broadband services — a well-defined, commercially available service with predictable recurring costs. Internet service provision by an established commercial ISP is a textbook candidate for FFP: the scope is stable (installation plus monthly service), the deliverable is clear (working broadband at specified speeds), and the pricing structure mirrors how commercial ISPs sell services to any customer.
