Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Minimal 3 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation does not exhibit meaningful compliance-over-outcomes pathology. The document is primarily a pre-award qualification and contracting vehicle — an Ordering Agreement for individual mediators — and is notably sparse on post-award accountability mechanisms of any kind. There are no weekly, monthly, or quarterly reporting requirements. There is no required QA surveillance plan. There are no prescribed status report formats. There are no meeting cadence requirements. The accountability structure consists of: (1) a standard professional services inspection clause giving the CO rejection authority, (2) a per-mediation flat fee payment structure, and (3) the implied outcome of successful mediation as the deliverable. The evaluation criteria are self-reported experience levels — a pre-award selection tool, not a post-award compliance mechanism. The solicitation's minimalism in this dimension actually reflects the nature of the service: individual mediators conducting discrete sessions, where the deliverable is a completed mediation and any resulting settlement agreement. The absence of elaborate reporting infrastructure is appropriate, not concerning. If anything, the accountability gap here is the inverse problem — there are no outcome metrics at all (settlement rates, party satisfaction, timeliness of case completion) — but that is an absence of accountability generally, not a displacement of outcomes by compliance theater. The sin as defined requires the presence of compliance-focused mechanisms crowding out outcome focus; neither element is strongly present here. Severity 1 is appropriate.

The absence of defined performance metrics or settlement rate targets here is notable but not itself a compliance-theater mechanism.

Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Minimal 3 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is structured as an open call for individual mediators — not firms — to join a roster for USPS EEO mediation work. The evaluation criteria are self-reported capability ratings on a five-point scale (no experience → expert) across mediation methodology, software tools, and EEO domain knowledge. There are no dollar-threshold requirements for prior contracts, no minimum number of previously completed contracts, no federal-specific experience mandates that would disqualify someone with equivalent private-sector or state-sector experience, and no short lookback windows tied to contract size. The experience questions (Questions 7–10) ask about transformative mediation, facilitative mediation, workplace discrimination disputes, and dispute facilitation in general — all of which a qualified mediator could have developed outside USPS contracts. The EEO domain knowledge question (Question 5) is the most USPS-specific element, but federal EEO statutes and case law are public, taught in law schools, and known to many employment mediators who have never held a USPS contract. The USPS EEO Mediator Skills Class (Section D) represents a mild incumbency advantage: current USPS mediators have already passed it, while new offerors must invest time and unreimbursed money to complete it. But the class is 8 hours long via Zoom — a low barrier that does not meaningfully exclude a capable outsider. This is qualitatively different from, say, requiring three prior federal EEO mediation contracts of $50,000 or more within the past two years. Overall, the solicitation is capability-focused and open to any individual with appropriate mediation skills and EEO knowledge. The playing field is not materially tilted toward incumbents. Severity 1 is appropriate.
Evidence Quotes
NOTE: THIS SOLICITATION IS FOR INDIVIDUALS ONLY. SUBMISSIONS FROM COMPANIES OR CORPORATIONS WITH EMPLOYEES DESIGNATED TO PERFORM THIS TYPE OF WORK WILL NOT BE CONSIDERED. SELECTED OFFERORS MUST PERFORM ALL THE WORK ASSOCIATED WITH THE ASSIGNED TASK ORDER.
This restriction to individual contractors rather than firms is a structural choice, but it does not function as an insider reward. It narrows the pool by entity type, not by prior incumbency. Any qualified individual mediator — whether or not they have worked this specific USPS contract before — can compete. The restriction reflects the nature of personal services (mediation is inherently individual), not a preference for prior holders.
7. Offerors have the ability to conduct mediations using the Transformative Model... Have you had training in Transformative Mediation? If so, please provide the dates of training(s). Please identify any certification(s) that you may have.
Transformative mediation is a specialized but publicly available methodology with training programs widely offered by non-USPS organizations (e.g., the Institute for the Study of Conflict Transformation). Asking for training and certification is a capability test, not an incumbency test. A mediator who has never worked for USPS can hold transformative mediation credentials from outside sources and compete on equal footing.
D. EEO Mediator Skills Class. The final step is the USPS EEO Mediator Skills Class. It is customary to award a contract if the offeror successfully completes the USPS EEO Mediator Skills class. Currently, the USPS EEO Skills class is an eight (8) hour long class conducted via Zoom.
This is the closest element to an insider reward in the solicitation. Current USPS contract mediators would already have completed this class, while new applicants must do so at their own expense and risk (no reimbursement if they fail). However, the class is open to all qualified offerors — it is an 8-hour Zoom session, not a multi-year credentialing process or a reference to prior USPS contract work. The barrier to a new entrant is modest (time, cost of fingerprints/drug test) and appears designed to ensure minimum USPS-process familiarity, not to exclude competition. It does create a mild incumbency advantage but does not rise to the level of a structured insider reward.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 1 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation shows minimal requirement sprawl. The core work — conducting EEO mediations using transformative and facilitative models — is defined as an outcome-oriented service. The solicitation does not dictate how a mediation session must be structured step-by-step, does not prescribe negotiation scripts, and does not mandate specific staffing arrangements or workflow procedures. It appropriately identifies two mediation modalities (transformative and facilitative) that the USPS program is built around, but those are programmatic constraints rooted in the REDRESS program's established methodology, not arbitrary process impositions. The evaluation questionnaire, while detailed, is a self-assessment instrument for screening individual contractors — not a process manual for performing the work. The equipment list is the most prescriptive element, but it is defensible: independent contractors working from home on sensitive federal EEO matters need minimum security, compute, and connectivity baselines to protect case confidentiality. The specifications are proportionate and most are framed as minimums rather than exact mandates. The 'such as' framing for platform examples reduces lock-in further. This is a relatively simple professional services engagement — individual mediators conducting discrete sessions at a flat rate — and the solicitation's level of specificity is appropriate to that scope. There is no evidence of copy-paste from incumbent operations generating sprawl, no multi-page workflow procedures, and no staffing structure mandates. The predominant character of the solicitation is qualification screening and standard contractual terms, not prescriptive process dictation.

Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation uses an Ordering Agreement with a pre-set flat fee of $800 per mediation session. For this requirement, that is the right contract pricing approach. Each mediation is a discrete, time-bounded event with a well-understood scope: one mediator, one session, parties in attendance, facilitation or transformative process applied, settlement agreement facilitated if possible. The deliverable is clear and the duration is predictable (a single session). A T&M or cost-reimbursement structure would be inappropriate here — mediators are not performing open-ended research or maintaining complex systems. A per-event fixed price correctly allocates risk to the mediator (who must complete the session for the fee) while giving USPS cost predictability. The Ordering Agreement vehicle appropriately handles the demand uncertainty (USPS cannot know in advance how many EEO complaints will arise), while the fixed unit price handles the per-session pricing efficiently. The REDRESS program has operated for many years, giving USPS ample data to set a reasonable unit price. No mismatch between contract type and requirements is present here.
Evidence Quotes
Services | New Fee Structure Mediation | $ 800 New NEEOISO Fee Structure effective October 1, 2025
The compensation structure establishes a flat $800 per-mediation fee. This is effectively a firm-fixed-price per-unit (per-event) structure, which is entirely appropriate for this type of service: each mediation session is a discrete, bounded deliverable with a well-understood scope and duration.
CLAUSE B-3 CONTRACT TYPE (MARCH 2006) This is an Ordering Agreement. An Ordering Agreement is not itself a contract. Rather, a contract will be established when an order is issued under the Ordering Agreement. There is no obligation by the Postal Service to place future orders with the Supplier under this Ordering Agreement.
The vehicle is an Ordering Agreement (analogous to an IDIQ), under which individual task orders are placed per mediation session. This structure is well-suited to a demand-driven service where the government cannot predict the exact number of sessions needed in advance, but can set a fixed unit price for each discrete event.
The Supplier will be compensated based upon the stated pricing structure developed by EEO Office, therefore the Supplier will not be submitting Price Proposals.
The USPS has pre-established the price ($800/mediation), eliminating the price competition risk and confirming a fixed-unit-price model. For a mature, recurring program like REDRESS — which has operated for decades — the government has sufficient historical data to set a reasonable fixed rate per session.
NOTE: THIS SOLICITATION IS FOR INDIVIDUALS ONLY. SUBMISSIONS FROM COMPANIES OR CORPORATIONS WITH EMPLOYEES DESIGNATED TO PERFORM THIS TYPE OF WORK WILL NOT BE CONSIDERED. SELECTED OFFERORS MUST PERFORM ALL THE WORK ASSOCIATED WITH THE ASSIGNED TASK ORDER.
Each mediator is an individual performing a single, discrete session per task order. This reinforces the appropriateness of a per-event fixed price: the unit of work is clear, the deliverable (a completed mediation session) is well-defined, and there is no ambiguity about scope creep within a single session.
