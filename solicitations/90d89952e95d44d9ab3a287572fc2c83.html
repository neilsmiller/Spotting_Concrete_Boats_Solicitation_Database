Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The solicitation uses a Fixed Price ID/IQ contract, with task orders issued per survey event. This is a well-matched contract type for the requirement. The surveys follow a rigidly standardized CMS methodology (Title 42 CFR Part 483, SOM Appendix PP), meaning the work content per survey is highly predictable and well-defined. The number of facilities is known (134 CLCs plus 5 SCI/D centers), and each survey follows the same prescribed process. Under an ID/IQ structure, the government issues task orders as surveys are needed, giving flexibility on timing and volume while keeping pricing fixed — a standard and appropriate approach for recurring, standardized professional services. The fact that surveys may be conducted onsite or virtually depending on public health conditions introduces some variability, but this is a relatively minor operational distinction that does not fundamentally alter the scope or cost of a survey. A fixed-price approach is defensible here because: (1) the deliverable is clearly defined (a CMS-compliant survey); (2) the methodology is externally mandated and well-established; (3) the government knows the universe of facilities; and (4) competition among qualified firms can keep pricing honest. There is no indication that T&M or cost-reimbursement would be more appropriate — the work is not exploratory, the requirements are not ambiguous, and labor hours per survey should be estimable by any experienced survey firm. This is a severity 1 — the contract type is well-matched to the requirements.
