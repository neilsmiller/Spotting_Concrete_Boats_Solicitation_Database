Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation uses FFP for what is fundamentally litigation support work in a complex, multi-decade international arbitration proceeding (the Iran-U.S. Claims Tribunal). The core tension is straightforward: FFP is designed for work where scope, cost, and schedule are predictable, while litigation support is inherently reactive and demand-driven — workload depends on what claims are filed, what the opposing party does, and what the tribunal schedules. These are external drivers the contractor cannot control and that the government cannot reliably forecast. That said, this is severity 2 rather than 3 for several reasons. First, this is an established, long-running proceeding with a known incumbent (N0018921CZ105), meaning both parties have significant historical data about workload patterns and cost. If the Iran-U.S. Claims Tribunal activity has been relatively stable and predictable in recent years — which is plausible given that the original claims date back to the 1979-1981 period and the docket has matured — a structured FFP arrangement might be defensible. Second, the solicitation is a presolicitation notice with no attached SOW/PWS, so the actual statement of work, labor categories, and deliverable structure are unknown. It is possible that the SOW structures the work into predictable deliverables (e.g., specific filings, defined research outputs) that could support FFP at the task level. Third, level-of-effort FFP contracts (FAR 16.207) can be used for professional services where effort rather than output is the primary measure, which may be what the government intends here. The mismatch is real but not severe given the historical precedent and the maturity of the underlying proceeding. A T&M or labor-hour contract would more naturally fit reactive litigation support, but the government's choice is not indefensible.

The solicitation explicitly states a firm-fixed-price (FFP) contract type. FFP is most appropriate when scope, cost, and schedule can be estimated with reasonable certainty. The nature of litigation support — particularly international arbitration defense — raises questions about whether FFP is well-suited here.
administrative, professional, and technical support of the defense of Iranian FMS claims against the United States Government at the Iran - U.S. Claims Tribunal, The Hague, Netherlands
Litigation support for international arbitration proceedings is inherently unpredictable in scope. The volume and complexity of claims, the need for responsive filings, hearing schedules, and document review can fluctuate dramatically based on opposing party actions and tribunal decisions — factors entirely outside the government's or contractor's control. This variability is characteristic of work better suited to T&M or labor-hour contracts.
The objective of this requirement is to provide the Navy's Office of the General Counsel and Department of State the most thorough and exhaustive litigation support possible.
The stated objective of 'most thorough and exhaustive' support signals that the government is purchasing expert effort and responsiveness rather than a defined deliverable. This is a classic T&M use case — buying expertise on demand rather than a predictable output — which is in tension with FFP pricing.
The period of performance is expected to be from 01 October 2026 through 30 September 2027, with four (4) 12-month option periods and one (1) 6-month option period pursuant to FAR 52.217-8.
A potential 5.5-year performance period for ongoing international litigation defense suggests the government anticipates long-term, evolving needs. Litigation workloads are notoriously difficult to predict over such long horizons. Locking in FFP pricing over this duration exposes the government to either overpaying (if activity is low) or under-scoping (if activity is high), requiring costly modifications.
