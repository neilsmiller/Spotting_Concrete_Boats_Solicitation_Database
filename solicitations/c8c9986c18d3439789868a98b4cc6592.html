Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is for a commercial telecommunications leased-line service — a 10Gb Ethernet circuit between Ohio and Oklahoma. The requirement is a reaward (i.e., a continuation of an existing service), meaning the government has historical pricing data and the scope is fully defined: fixed bandwidth, fixed endpoints, standard installation, and ongoing maintenance. The pricing structure — monthly recurring charges and non-recurring charges — is the standard commercial model for such services and maps directly to a firm-fixed-price contract structure. There is no ambiguity in scope, no variable labor component, and no unpredictable workload. The NAICS code 517111 (Wired Telecommunications Carriers) and the product/service code DG11 further confirm this is a routine commercial telecom acquisition. Fixed-price pricing is unambiguously appropriate here: the government knows exactly what it wants, vendors can price it competitively, and there is no reason to use T&M or cost-reimbursement for a commodity telecom circuit. The LPTA evaluation method is also consistent with this type of straightforward, well-defined commercial service. No square peg concern exists.
