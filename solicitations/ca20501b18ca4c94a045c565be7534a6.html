Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Minimal 0 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a simplified acquisition / commercial item purchase (FAR Part 12, simplified acquisition procedures) for RTI DDS software licenses — a straightforward commercial software procurement on a firm-fixed-price basis covering a one-year license period (6/21/2026–6/20/2027). The attachments consist of a deliverables list and an SF 1449. There is no Statement of Work, Performance Work Statement, Quality Assurance Surveillance Plan, or service delivery requirements of any kind. The acquisition is for commercial off-the-shelf software licenses, not a services contract. As a result, the entire apparatus that gives rise to compliance-over-outcomes problems — recurring status reports, meeting cadences, QA surveillance plans, process documentation requirements — is simply absent and structurally irrelevant. Award is on Lowest Price Technically Acceptable basis with the only technical requirement being that the vendor is an authorized RTI reseller. There are no performance targets to evaluate because there is no ongoing service being performed; the government is buying licenses. No evidence of compliance theater was found, and none is expected for this acquisition type. Severity is 1 (minimal) because the solicitation structure does not create the conditions for this sin to manifest.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Severe 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation represents a severe case of insider rewards — arguably the most extreme form, where competition has been eliminated rather than merely constrained. The sole source designation under FAR 6.103-1(b) means no other vendor can win regardless of their qualifications, pricing, or technical capability. The justification offered — that RTI software is already installed and 'compatible in all aspects (form, fit and function) with the systems presently installed' — is a direct acknowledgment that the agency has built operational dependency on a single proprietary vendor. This is vendor capture operationalized: the agency cannot articulate requirements in vendor-neutral terms because their systems are defined by RTI's proprietary technology. The solicitation further restricts even the reseller competition to pre-authorized RTI partners, meaning RTI itself controls who may bid on government contracts for its own software. The IP retention clause ensures this dependency compounds over time: any government-funded improvements to RTI software become RTI's property, foreclosing future competitive transitions. Taken together, these elements do not merely tilt the playing field — they eliminate it. While FAR does provide mechanisms for legitimate sole source acquisitions (including proprietary software renewals), the pattern here is consistent with the self-reinforcing cycle described in the Insider Rewards framework: prior sole source → deeper system integration → next sole source is even more justified. The severity is 3 not because the solicitation is poorly written, but because its structure represents the endpoint of the insider rewards progression.
Evidence Quotes
Basis for award: __ Brand Name - FAR 6.103-1(d) X Sole Source - FAR 6.103-1(b)
The solicitation is structured as a sole source acquisition, meaning competition has been eliminated entirely. Only one vendor — RTI or its authorized resellers — can fulfill this requirement. This is the most extreme form of insider rewards: not just tilting the playing field but removing it altogether.
The Naval Surface Warfare Center Dahlgren Division (NSWCDD) intends to procure products/service manufactured by Real-Time Innovations Inc. ... RTI provides a unique, data distribution software tool that meets the Government's requirements for systems and is in use across multiple platforms. Data distribution licenses are compatible in all aspects (form, fit and function) with the systems presently installed.
The justification for sole source is that RTI software is already installed and in use. This is a textbook incumbent lock-in: the agency's systems have been built around a specific proprietary vendor, making competitive replacement practically impossible. The phrase 'compatible in all aspects (form, fit and function) with the systems presently installed' explicitly rewards the incumbent's installed base rather than evaluating capability.
NOTE: Vendor must be a Real-Time Innovations, Inc. authorized reseller to quote!
Even within the limited pool of potential offerors (authorized resellers), the requirement gatekeeps participation to those who have a pre-existing commercial relationship with RTI. A qualified IT reseller without a prior RTI authorization agreement cannot compete, regardless of their capability or pricing.
The offeror shall provide documentation from the Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) confirming the offeror is an authorized reseller or distributor of the brand-name item(s) quoted. Quotations submitted without such documentation may not be considered for award.
This requirement formalizes the reseller authorization barrier. Combined with the sole source justification, it creates a two-layer insider filter: first, only RTI software is acceptable; second, only pre-authorized RTI partners may quote. No new entrant can qualify without RTI's explicit permission.
RTI retains ownership of all ideas, processes, inventions, improvements, technology, software, designs, patents, copyrights, trademarks and services marks (collectively 'Intellectual Property') that constitute an improvement to, extension of, or modification of RTI software, products, or services and that is generated as a result of this order.
The IP retention clause reinforces perpetual vendor lock-in. Any work performed under this contract that improves RTI software becomes RTI's property, meaning the government cannot use those improvements to transition to a competitor. This structural feature ensures that each successive contract renewal deepens the agency's dependency on RTI.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 0 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a sole-source procurement for a commercial software license renewal — specifically RTI Data Distribution Service (DDS) software from Real-Time Innovations, Inc. for a one-year license period (6/21/2026 – 6/20/2027). The document is essentially a commercial item purchase using simplified acquisition procedures under FAR Part 12. The solicitation does not exhibit requirement sprawl in any meaningful sense. There are no bullet lists prescribing how work shall be performed, no staffing structures, no workflow procedures, and no step-by-step process mandates. The 'requirements' are simply: procure a named commercial software license from an authorized reseller. The only prescriptive element is the specification of the exact product (RTI DDS software), but this is entirely appropriate and expected in a sole-source acquisition. The justification provided — that RTI software is already deployed across multiple platforms and that data distribution licenses must be compatible 'in all aspects (form, fit and function) with the systems presently installed' — reflects a legitimate interoperability constraint, not sprawl. Specifying the exact product in a sole-source renewal is the definition of a technical interoperability constraint. The remaining terms (authorized reseller documentation, SAM registration, firm-fixed-price, LPTA evaluation) are standard commercial acquisition procedures, not prescriptive process requirements. The solicitation does not have sufficient SOW or PWS content to generate sprawl concerns — the attached deliverables list is referenced but appears to be a simple license schedule. There is simply no 'how work shall be done' language present because this is a product purchase, not a services contract. Severity is 1 — minimal.
Rule Pools

Minimal 0 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation — a sole-source RFQ for RTI Data Distribution Service (DDS) software licenses — is a streamlined commercial item acquisition under FAR Part 12 using simplified acquisition procedures. The regulatory citation burden is minimal and appropriate. The solicitation references FAR Part 12, FAR Part 5, the applicable FAR/DFARS clauses incorporated by reference via the SF 1449, Buy American/Trade Agreements thresholds, and DFARS 252.225-7013 (Duty-Free Entry). These are all standard, clearly relevant clauses for a commercial software license purchase — the Buy American/Trade Agreements language is required for commercial acquisitions, and the Duty-Free Entry clause is conditionally noted as applicable given potential import duties. There is no 'Applicable Documents' list, no bulk regulatory citation section, no referenced standards beyond what is standard for a simplified commercial acquisition, and no regulations that appear irrelevant to the scope. The deliverables attachment simply defines the license period and line items. This is a focused, minimalist procurement document. A vendor reviewing this solicitation faces no meaningful compliance interpretation burden — the regulatory landscape is clear and proportionate to the work being solicited (procurement of proprietary software licenses from an authorized reseller). No rule pool pattern is present.
