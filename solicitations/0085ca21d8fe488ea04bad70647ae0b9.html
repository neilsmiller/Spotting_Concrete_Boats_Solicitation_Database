Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 3 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation procures cellular phone services — a standard commercial telecommunications offering — for the U.S. Embassy in Port-au-Prince. The contract type is firm-fixed-price, which is the correct choice here. The requirements are well-defined: monthly recurring service for a set of lines, plus an as-needed daily rental option for temporary devices at special events. Both components are commercially available, routinely priced, and predictable in cost structure. FFP is explicitly recommended by FAR for commercial services and routine requirements where risk can be estimated with reasonable certainty. The temporary additional services introduce minor quantity variability, but this is managed through an as-needed ordering mechanism with a fixed daily rate — a standard and appropriate approach. There is no mismatch between the contract type and the nature of the work. No modification risk, cost overrun dynamic, or misaligned incentive structure is evident. This is a straightforward, low-complexity procurement appropriately structured as FFP.
