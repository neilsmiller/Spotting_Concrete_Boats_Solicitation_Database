Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The solicitation uses a firm-fixed-price ID/IQ contract for residential housing and wraparound supportive services for homeless veterans. On balance, this is a moderate rather than severe square peg. The core housing component — room, board, and transportation — is a well-understood, commercial-style service that lends itself to per-diem fixed pricing. The VA routinely uses this model for HCHV (Health Care for Homeless Veterans) contract residential services, and per-diem rates are a standard mechanism in this program space. This is a legitimate and defensible choice for the housing portion. However, the solicitation also bundles in therapeutic rehabilitation, recovery services, and intensive individualized case management. These services vary significantly in labor intensity depending on the acuity of each veteran served. A flat fixed price does not easily accommodate this variability unless the VA has established tiered rates or minimum/maximum quantities — neither of which is described in this pre-solicitation notice. Without rate tiers or level-of-care adjustments, a rigid fixed-price structure creates incentives for contractors to minimize services for high-need veterans (to protect margin) or to underbid and then underperform. The five-year ordering period also increases exposure to cost volatility in labor and housing markets, which is a known risk for FFP in service contracts. That said, this is a well-established VA program type, the services are broadly familiar to the market, and ID/IQ structure provides some flexibility in ordering volumes. The severity is 2 — moderate — because the FFP choice is defensible for the housing core but creates meaningful tension with the more variable, individualized service components that accompany it.
Evidence Quotes
The Alexandria VAMC intends to award a fixed priced Indefinite Delivery/Indefinite Quantity Contract (ID/IQ) with a five (5) year ordering period with a period of performance from 1June 2026 through 31 May 2031.
The government is using a firm-fixed-price ID/IQ structure for this requirement. This is the contract type that must be assessed for fit.
The purpose of this contract is to provide a safe, low-barrier emergency shelter and coordinated supportive services for veterans experiencing homelessness, with the primary goal of rapidly resolving housing instability and securing permanent housing.
The core service is residential housing (room and board) with supportive case management. Housing bed-night services are essentially a per-diem or per-bed commercial service — a well-understood, commoditized offering that can be priced on a per-veteran-per-night or per-bed basis. This type of service is generally amenable to fixed pricing.
requires contractors to provide residential housing services to include room, board, transportation, therapeutic rehabilitation and recovery, and other required services
The bundling of room, board, transportation, therapeutic rehabilitation, and recovery services creates scope ambiguity. The intensity and duration of therapeutic rehabilitation and recovery services will vary significantly by individual veteran's needs, which can make fixed pricing difficult and potentially lead to either over- or under-pricing by offerors.
The program will deliver comprehensive case management as the central service component, focused on assessing each veteran's housing barriers, developing individualized housing plans, and actively coordinating with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) and community partners to obtain and sustain permanent housing placements.
Individualized housing plans and case management intensity vary substantially by veteran. The volume and complexity of case management work is unpredictable. A fixed-price structure may underpay contractors who serve veterans with high needs, incentivizing them to minimize individualized services, or may overpay for veterans with simpler needs.
It is understood that the type of patients to be provided with services under this contract will normally require services above the level of simple room and board.
The government acknowledges that service levels exceed simple room and board, yet the contract is still structured as fixed-price without apparent per-acuity tiering or level-of-care adjustments. This creates a mismatch: fixed pricing works best when the level of effort per unit is predictable, but the solicitation signals significant variation in required services.
