Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Severe 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation presents a clear and severe square peg problem. The government is attempting to use a Firm Fixed-Price contract for SETA advisory and assistance services supporting the Assistant Secretary of the Air Force for Space Acquisition and Integration — a mission area characterized by rapidly evolving program portfolios, emerging space threats, and shifting policy priorities. FFP contracting requires that 'the risk involved is minimal or can be predicted with an acceptable degree of certainty' (FAR 16.202-2). That condition is not met here. SETA work is fundamentally labor-based advisory support. The workload — how many tasks, what analyses, which technical areas, at what tempo — is driven by SAF/SQ's operational demands, which will shift significantly over five years as the Space Force matures, programs move through acquisition phases, and national security priorities evolve. The volume and type of expert labor required cannot be predicted with the accuracy FFP demands. There are no defined deliverables with predictable costs; there is a pool of expertise made available to a senior office. The appropriate contract type for this work is labor-hour or T&M, with fixed hourly rates by labor category. This would allow the government to pay for actual expert hours consumed while maintaining cost visibility and ensuring rates are locked. Alternatively, if the government has a good historical baseline of labor hours consumed, a Firm-Fixed-Price, Level-of-Effort (FFP-LOE) term contract under FAR 16.207 could be defensible — but the solicitation makes no mention of level-of-effort terms, historical utilization data, or any mechanism that would make FFP tractable for advisory services. The four-day response window further compounds the problem: FFP contracts rely on competitive price discipline to function, but a four-day window makes genuine competition nearly impossible, especially for complex SETA work requiring proposed staffing plans and labor category pricing. This suggests the award vehicle is not structured to generate the market competition that would make FFP appropriate. Most experienced SETA contracting officers would flag this as an unusual choice. FAR 16.601 authorizes T&M only when 'it is not possible at the time of placing the contract to estimate accurately the extent or duration of the work or to anticipate costs with any reasonable degree of accuracy' — exactly the conditions present here. The government's decision to use FFP instead risks either locking in an over-estimated price (overpaying for unused capacity) or forcing expensive contract modifications when task demands exceed the fixed price. This is a severity 3 finding.

NAICS Code: 541715 — Research and Development in the Physical, Engineering, and Life Sciences
NAICS 541715 covers R&D services, which by definition involve technically uncertain, evolving work. The government's own classification of this work signals scope uncertainty — exactly the condition under which FAR prohibits firm-fixed-price contracting unless risk can be estimated with acceptable certainty.
Classification Code: R425
R425 is the federal supply classification for 'Engineering and Technical Services,' specifically advisory and assistance services. This classification is associated with labor-based, expert-driven work that is responsive to agency needs, not deliverable-driven work with a predictable cost and schedule. FFP is rarely appropriate for R425 work.
