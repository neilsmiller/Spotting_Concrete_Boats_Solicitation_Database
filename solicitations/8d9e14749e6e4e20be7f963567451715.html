Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation uses a firm-fixed-price structure for what appear to be variable, labor-intensive advisory and technical support services tied to fleet readiness operations. The scope includes supporting 'up to 10 deployed ships,' implying workload that fluctuates with operational tempo — a characteristic that typically favors T&M or labor-hour contracts rather than FFP. Engineering, technical support, readiness support, and training services are generally expert-labor-driven with uncertain quantities, making it difficult to estimate costs with the accuracy FFP requires. The government partially acknowledges this unpredictability by using cost-reimbursable CLINs for travel, but does not extend the same logic to the underlying labor effort. A T&M or labor-hour contract with defined labor categories and fixed hourly rates would give the government better cost control and more accurately reflect the variable nature of the work. However, the severity is assessed at 2 (moderate) rather than 3 (severe) for several reasons: (1) SeaPort NxG task orders for similar readiness support services are frequently awarded as FFP when the government has sufficient historical data on labor mix and hours from prior task orders under the same IDIQ vehicle; (2) the solicitation does not provide enough detail about CLINs, labor categories, or period of performance to confirm there is no definitized scope that could support FFP; and (3) the hybrid structure (FFP labor + CR travel) is a recognized mitigation approach. The mismatch is real but may be defensible depending on the level of historical data available to the government from previous SeaPort task orders for this requirement.
Evidence Quotes
intends to award a firm fixed price with cost-reimbursable CLINs for travel service task order from SeaPort NxG for engineering, technical support, readiness support, training support and financial services
The solicitation uses a hybrid structure: firm-fixed-price (FFP) for the primary effort and cost-reimbursable (CR) CLINs for travel. The FFP component covers engineering, technical support, readiness, training, and financial services. These are professional/advisory service categories that typically involve variable labor levels depending on fleet operational tempo, deployment schedules, and ship readiness conditions — factors that are not easy to predict in advance and may make FFP a suboptimal fit.
providing training, management and engineering support services... directly supports the material readiness of 4 surface ships and up to 10 deployed ships
The phrase 'up to 10 deployed ships' signals variability in workload. The number of deployed ships supported at any given time can vary significantly, meaning the labor demand is not predictable with certainty. Fixing a price for services whose scope fluctuates with operational deployments introduces risk that the government either overpays (if fewer ships deploy) or the contractor underperforms (if more ships deploy than priced for).
engineering, technical support, readiness support, training support and financial services
These service categories are characteristic of labor-hour or T&M contracts, not FFP. Engineering and technical support involve variable, expert-driven effort that is difficult to scope precisely. Training support frequency depends on crew rotations and ship schedules. FFP works best when deliverables are well-defined and quantities are stable; none of these categories fit that description well given the operational context described.
cost-reimbursable CLINs for travel
The government's own decision to separate travel into cost-reimbursable CLINs implicitly acknowledges that some costs cannot be predicted in advance. This is a reasonable mitigation for the travel portion. However, it also highlights the tension: if travel is unpredictable enough to warrant CR treatment, the labor effort tied to that travel (deployed ship support, training events, readiness assessments) may be equally variable — yet those labor costs remain under FFP.
