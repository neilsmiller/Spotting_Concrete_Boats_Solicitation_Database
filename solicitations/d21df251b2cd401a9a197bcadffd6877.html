Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation uses a firm-fixed-price contract for a software development and transition effort that includes development, modernization, sustainment, maintenance, and scaling of a prototype decision support tool to full operational capability. FFP is defensible here — but only partially. The mitigating factor is that the prototype has already been successfully delivered under a prior OT agreement, so Peraton Labs has deep familiarity with the system and both parties have a documented baseline. This reduces (but does not eliminate) scope uncertainty. However, prototype-to-production transitions in software routinely surface new requirements, integration challenges, and infrastructure dependencies that are difficult to anticipate and price accurately in advance. The work description bundles inherently variable activities — scaling, modernization, and development — alongside more predictable ones like initial sustainment and maintenance. Pricing all of these together under FFP forces the vendor to either pad the bid to cover uncertainty or risk losses when surprises emerge. A hybrid approach (FFP for defined deliverables like specific capability milestones, with T&M or labor-hour CLINs for sustainment and exploratory scaling work) would better align incentives and risk. That said, this is not a severe mismatch: the existence of a working prototype and a documented performance baseline meaningfully constrains scope uncertainty, and the government can plausibly argue that the prior OT work gives Peraton Labs enough information to estimate costs accurately. The severity is assessed as moderate (2): the FFP choice is defensible given the prototype baseline, but the bundling of development and scaling work — which carry residual uncertainty — into a single FFP contract creates meaningful risk of costly modifications if the production hardening process surfaces unexpected requirements.

The government explicitly states it will use FFP pricing. The key question is whether this is appropriate given the nature of the work: transitioning a prototype to full operational capability, including 'data and analytics services, digital tool development, modernization, initial sustainment and maintenance, and scaling.' Prototype-to-production transitions frequently encounter scope uncertainties that are difficult to price accurately under FFP.
initial development to reach Full Operational Capability of the Strategic Laydown and Dispersal (SLD) Decision Support Tool (DST), to include data and analytics services, digital tool development, modernization, initial sustainment and maintenance, and scaling of the SLD DST as it transitions into full production
The scope bundles multiple distinct work types — development, modernization, sustainment, maintenance, and scaling — into a single FFP contract. Development and scaling activities carry inherent scope uncertainty (unknowns surface during integration and production hardening), whereas sustainment and maintenance are more routine. Bundling uncertain development work with routine maintenance under a single FFP creates pricing risk: the vendor must pad the fixed price to cover development unknowns, or underbid and absorb losses.
The resultant contract will include a base period of twelve (12) months plus one six (6) month option period.
An 18-month total performance window for a prototype-to-FOC transition covering development, modernization, sustainment, and scaling is relatively compressed. Under FFP, if scope expands or technical challenges arise during production hardening — common in software transitions — the government must issue contract modifications, which are costly and disruptive. A T&M or cost-plus-fixed-fee structure would give the government more flexibility to manage emerging requirements without formal modifications.
Following the successful delivery of the SLD DST prototype as documented in Determination of Successful Prototype (IWRP-22-LANT-2594, Deliverable 5.6), OPNAV N52 is seeking support to transition the prototype created under IWRP-22-LANT-2594 to an operational production capability
A successful prototype has been delivered, which does provide a defined baseline and reduces some scope uncertainty relative to a greenfield effort. This is the primary mitigating factor supporting FFP: Peraton Labs knows the codebase, the government has documented prototype deliverables, and both parties have a shared understanding of the system. This makes FFP more defensible than it would be for a novel development effort.
