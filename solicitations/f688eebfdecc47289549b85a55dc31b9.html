Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation presents a mixed picture. On the outcome side, Section 1.1 establishes meaningful SLAs: 99.9% production availability, tiered issue resolution timeframes (Severity 1 within 2 hours), and specific user support response targets (90% of tickets within 24 hours, live chat connections within 2 hours, fraud investigations within 14 days for 90% of cases). These are genuine outcome-oriented accountability mechanisms that represent real performance standards, not compliance theater. The solicitation is meaningfully better than many in this respect. However, the deliverable structure layered on top of these SLAs tilts toward compliance and activity tracking. There are six recurring deliverables per period: a bi-weekly snapshot report, a monthly subscription usage report, a monthly SLA monitoring plan, a monthly SLA report, a monthly user support report, and a monthly MFA status report. That is five monthly reports plus a bi-weekly report — a notable administrative cadence for a commercial SaaS subscription renewal. Critically, most of these deliverables measure activity volumes, consumption counts, and process documentation rather than Veteran outcomes. None of the six deliverables requires reporting on user satisfaction, identity proofing abandonment rates, the percentage of Veterans who successfully established credentials on first attempt, or service quality trends. The SLA Report gets closest to outcomes, but it is paired with a redundant SLA Monitoring Plan that documents the methodology rather than the results. The government's primary visibility into whether Veterans are being well-served comes from SLA metrics, which are legitimate. But the substantial parallel reporting structure — six deliverables tracking consumption, volumes, and process adherence — adds compliance overhead without adding insight into whether the ID.me solution is actually serving Veterans well. This is a moderate manifestation of the sin: outcome mechanisms exist and are real, but they are accompanied by a reporting apparatus that primarily confirms process adherence and activity levels. Severity 2 is appropriate.
Evidence Quotes
Deliverable: Bi-weekly Snapshot Report. Due 7 days After Receipt of Order (ARO) and bi-weekly thereafter. Electronic submission to: VA PM, COR, CO.
The bi-weekly snapshot report is described only in terms of submission schedule and recipients. The Product Description elaborates that it covers 'subscription issuances and renewals consumed by each integration' — activity counts, not performance outcomes. There is no acceptance criterion defining what a 'good' report looks like or what action the government will take if subscription consumption is anomalous.
Deliverable: Monthly Subscription Usage Report. Due 30 days ARO and due monthly thereafter. Electronic submission to: VA PM, COR, CO.
This report covers 'issuance of new users, renewal users, key attributes that pertain to tracking used, and unused subscriptions across each billing period to allow for forecasting and user management.' It is a consumption ledger — useful for billing, but oriented entirely toward tracking what happened rather than evaluating whether the service is meeting Veteran needs. No performance standard or outcome threshold is attached to it.
Deliverable: SLA Monitoring Plan (due monthly). Deliverable: SLA Report (due monthly).
Two separate monthly deliverables are required for SLA oversight — a monitoring plan and a report. The SLA Monitoring Plan describes the methodology ID.me will use to measure itself, while the SLA Report documents results. Requiring both monthly is somewhat redundant; the plan does not change month to month. More importantly, these deliverables confirm process compliance (is the monitoring methodology being followed?) as much as outcomes (is the service actually performing?).
Deliverable: User Support Report (due monthly)... the User Support Report shall include information on volume of open investigations, volume of closed investigations, volumes of each outcome (e.g., how many reinstated vs. labeled fraud), breakdown of reasons for account suspensions, and statistics on time required to complete the investigation.
The User Support Report is heavily oriented toward activity volumes and case counts — inputs and throughput — rather than Veteran experience outcomes. There is no requirement to report user satisfaction scores, successful identity proofing completion rates, or the percentage of Veterans who gave up during the process. Counting tickets closed is a process metric, not an outcome metric.
Deliverable: MFA Monthly Status Report... shall include itemizing the usages per credential... a report on the current usage of already provided MFA events for the already procured LOA3/IAL2 credential at the new issuance rates as well as the lower renewal rate for each activity.
The MFA Monthly Status Report tracks consumption counts against the contracted pool of 125 million MFA events. This is a billing and utilization tracking document, not an outcome assessment. No metric addresses whether Veterans are successfully authenticating on first attempt, what the failure rate is, or whether the authentication experience is improving over time.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Severe 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation represents the most severe form of insider rewards: it is structured as a brand-name sole-source procurement that explicitly names the incumbent vendor (ID.me) and its specific proprietary part numbers throughout the price schedule, then uses an 'Exception to Fair Opportunity' justification rather than open competition. The sin is not merely that experience requirements tilt the playing field — the solicitation is written in a way that makes it legally impossible for any vendor offering an alternative solution to win. While the solicitation is technically issued as a 100% small business set-aside (allowing multiple authorized ID.me resellers to compete on price), this represents reseller competition, not capability competition. No alternative IAM/CSP provider can participate regardless of their technical capability, past performance, or price competitiveness. The Justification for Exception to Fair Opportunity acknowledges this explicitly: VA's entire external authentication infrastructure was built on ID.me's proprietary code, VA has limited data rights to its own configurations, and switching costs are estimated at $32 million plus years of redundant labor. This is a textbook vendor capture outcome. The agency's institutional knowledge about its own authentication systems now resides in ID.me's proprietary architecture, not in VA's own workforce. The self-reinforcing cycle is evident: VA built systems on ID.me's proprietary stack → VA cannot describe requirements in vendor-neutral terms → every renewal solicitation must be brand-name → switching costs grow → competition becomes even less viable. The severity is unambiguously 3. A qualified vendor with comparable IAM/CSP capabilities, NIST 800-63 compliance, and federal experience cannot meaningfully compete because the specification is not a capability description — it is a product specification for a single vendor's commercial offering. The only mitigation is that competition exists among authorized resellers for pricing, which prevents this from being a pure sole-source, but that is reseller price competition, not capability competition, and does not reduce the severity of the underlying insider rewards problem.

The solicitation is structured as a brand-name requirement for a specific commercial product — ID.me — from the outset. This isn't an experience requirement per se, but it is the most extreme form of insider reward: the solicitation specifies the incumbent's exact product by name and part number, making it structurally impossible for any competitor offering an alternative solution to win on merit.

This requirement limits competition to authorized ID.me resellers. Only vendors who have a pre-existing commercial relationship with ID.me (the OEM) can participate. Any vendor offering a competing IAM/CSP solution is automatically excluded regardless of technical capability, price, or past performance. This is incumbency protection at the OEM authorization level, not just the contract level.

This is the textbook consequence of vendor capture: VA's institutional knowledge and technical architecture now live inside the incumbent's proprietary systems. The requirement for interoperability with VA's existing stack is not vendor-neutral — it is a technical specification that only the incumbent can satisfy.

This passage from the Justification reveals profound vendor lock-in: VA has limited data rights to its own configurations, and those configurations only work with ID.me's proprietary code. This is not a capability threshold that a qualified newcomer could meet — it is a proprietary technical dependency that makes any alternative vendor structurally incapable of competing. The Justification uses transition cost and complexity as a rationale for sole-source justification, but this framing itself illustrates how deeply the insider rewards cycle has progressed. VA has now accumulated so much switching cost that competition is functionally impossible. This is the endpoint of the insider rewards dynamic: the agency cannot articulate vendor-neutral requirements because the incumbent's systems have become inseparable from VA's own operations, and any future solicitation will inherit this same structural exclusion.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a brand-name software subscription renewal for ID.me, and that context fundamentally shapes the assessment. The vast majority of requirements describe WHAT the government needs (identity proofing at NIST 800-63 assurance levels, 99.9% uptime, specific SLA response timeframes by severity tier, defined MFA event volumes) rather than HOW ID.me must build or operate its platform. The prescriptive elements that do exist — enumerated support channels, specific report cadences, defined issue severity classifications with response time windows — are best understood as commercial product feature documentation and outcome-based SLAs, not process mandates imposed on a services vendor. The SLA table specifying response and resolution times by severity level (e.g., Severity 1: response within 30 minutes, resolution within 2 hours during operating hours) is precisely the kind of outcome-oriented requirement that defines what must be achieved without prescribing how. The security and compliance sections (FedRAMP maintenance, NIST 800-63, Section 508, IPv6, FIPS 140-2, TIC) are regulatory and interoperability constraints, not sprawl. The addendum security language (Addendum B) is boilerplate VA information security language applied uniformly across VA IT contracts and reflects legal obligations. There is no staffing structure prescribed, no workflow enumerated step-by-step, no process that removes vendor discretion over implementation. The brand-name justification document makes clear why VA cannot use outcome-neutral requirements: the product is proprietary and interoperability with existing VA infrastructure is the core constraint. This is a competently scoped software subscription renewal with legitimate specificity about product capabilities and measurable service levels. Severity 1 is appropriate.
Evidence Quotes
The Contractor shall provide a Bi-weekly Snapshot Report including subscription issuances and renewals consumed by each integration. In addition, the Contractor shall provide a Monthly Subscription Usage Report that details the issuance of new users, renewal users, key attributes that pertain to tracking used, and unused subscriptions across each billing period to allow for forecasting and user management.
This is a reporting deliverable requirement, but it is outcome-oriented — it specifies what information must be conveyed (subscription consumption data for forecasting), not how the contractor must generate or compile it. This is not sprawl; it is a reasonable deliverable specification.
User Support channels shall include inbound phone support via direct routing from VA call centers, outbound phone support from ID.me, outbound video chat from ID.me, publicly available live chat, publicly available chatbot, publicly available inbound ticket submission, inbound escalation email and ticket submission from VA staff (va.support@id.me and customersupport@id.me) on behalf of Veterans, and outbound email from ID.me.
This is the most prescriptive passage in the solicitation — it enumerates specific support channels rather than stating a support accessibility outcome. However, this is a brand-name acquisition for an existing SaaS product. ID.me's existing commercial offering already includes these channels, so the solicitation is documenting the product's existing capabilities rather than prescribing a novel delivery method. The channels listed are features of the product being procured, not instructions on how a vendor must staff or operate a new support function.
Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation does not exhibit the most severe form of rule pooling — there is no 88-item 'Applicable Documents' list and no obvious contradictions between a cited policy and the solicitation's core purpose (identity verification software). The regulatory citations are mostly at least plausibly related to federal IT security. However, the solicitation demonstrates moderate rule pooling through a different mechanism: a 14-page Addendum B security block that is almost certainly copied forward from a VA IT contract template designed for custom system development, and applied without curation to a commercial SaaS subscription renewal where a reseller — not the software developer — is the expected awardee. The core problem is undifferentiated applicability. Requirements for software development attestation (EO 14028, OMB M-22-18), vulnerability scanning, ATO development, hardware tamper-evident packaging, and IPv6 deployment engineering are presented at the same level as legitimately applicable requirements like FedRAMP maintenance, NIST 800-63 compliance, and Section 508. A new offeror — specifically a small business reseller of ID.me, which is what the market research identified as the likely vendor pool — would face a substantial burden interpreting which of these requirements they can actually satisfy, which are handled by ID.me under its existing FedRAMP authorization, and which are simply inapplicable to a reseller relationship. The solicitation provides no guidance on this. The severity is 2 rather than 3 for two reasons: First, most of the cited frameworks have at least some plausible connection to a federal identity management platform handling PII and PHI for millions of veterans — this is genuinely sensitive work with a legitimately high security baseline. Second, the core product requirements (Section B.5) are well-specified with clear, curated regulatory citations explaining why each standard applies (NIST 800-63-2, NIST 800-63-3, WCAG 2.1, OMB M-19-17, EOs 14249/14247/14271 all explained in context). The rule pool problem is concentrated in the Addendum B boilerplate block, not throughout the entire document, which is a mitigating factor.

The 14-page Addendum B security block is applied wholesale to this SaaS subscription renewal without any curation or scoping guidance distinguishing which sections apply to ID.me (the OEM, who is not the contracting party), to a reseller (who has no operational control over the system), or to VA itself. Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
FFP is appropriate here because this is a commercial SaaS product renewal with well-defined deliverables, established vendor part numbers, and a standard subscription pricing structure. The requirements are stable and well-understood — VA has been using ID.me and knows its usage volumes (8 million accounts, ~125 million MFA events per year). The quantities are bounded by 'up to' ceilings with optional tasks for overflow, which is a standard mechanism for managing volume uncertainty under FFP. The tiered pricing model described in Section 1.5 locks in unit prices at award, making total cost predictable within the defined quantity bands. The deliverables (SLA reports, usage reports, snapshot reports) are NSP line items that do not affect the pricing structure. There is no open-ended labor component, no R&D uncertainty, and no scope ambiguity that would warrant T&M or cost-reimbursement. The Justification for Exception to Fair Opportunity confirms this is a known, deployed commercial system being renewed — exactly the kind of requirement where FFP is the right choice. The optional tasks (FAR 52.217-7) provide flexibility for volume growth without destabilizing the pricing structure. No mismatch between contract type and requirements is evident.
