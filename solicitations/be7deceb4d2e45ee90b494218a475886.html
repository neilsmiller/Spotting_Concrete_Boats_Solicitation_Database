Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Moderate 3 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation presents a moderate square peg concern. The use of FFP for a UESC is not inherently wrong — UESCs are a well-established federal contracting vehicle authorized under 10 U.S.C. § 2913 and DOE guidelines, and some degree of fixed-price structure is common in UESCs because the utility company guarantees a minimum level of savings. However, the FFP label here warrants scrutiny. A true UESC is a performance-based instrument: savings guarantees are the mechanism by which the government is 'paid back.' This is structurally more like a performance-based or savings-guarantee contract than a classic FFP for a defined deliverable. If the FFP structure here means the government pays a fixed total price regardless of actual energy savings achieved, that misaligns risk: the contractor bears no ongoing incentive to ensure savings materialize over 25 years. Conversely, if the FFP structure wraps a savings-guarantee mechanism (as is standard in UESCs), then the FFP label may be technically accurate but potentially misleading about the underlying performance risk sharing. The 25-year duration amplifies the concern — it is extremely difficult to predict energy baselines, facility usage, and technology performance over such a long horizon, making the 'firm' in FFP aspirational rather than practically achievable. The severity is assessed as 2 rather than 3 because: (1) UESCs have an established statutory and regulatory framework that contemplates long-duration agreements with utility companies, and the FFP structure is a recognized approach within that framework; (2) this is a presolicitation notice with no attached SOW/PWS, so it is possible the full RFP will include savings-guarantee provisions and M&V protocols that adequately manage the mismatch; and (3) UESC vehicles are specifically designed to shift implementation risk to the utility company, which provides some mitigation for the pricing mismatch. The concern is real but not severe given the specialized nature of the UESC vehicle.
Evidence Quotes
award for Fort Benning, Georgia, a Firm Fixed Price (FFP), Utility Energy Savings Contract (UESC) with a period of performance that shall not exceed 25 years
The solicitation explicitly uses FFP pricing for a UESC spanning up to 25 years. UESCs are a specialized vehicle where a utility company finances and implements energy conservation measures (ECMs) and is repaid from the resulting energy savings. The savings are, by nature, estimated projections — not guaranteed fixed outcomes. Actual energy savings depend on occupancy changes, operational patterns, equipment performance, and weather, all of which fluctuate over a 25-year horizon. Locking this into a pure FFP structure raises questions about how risk is allocated when savings projections diverge from actuals.
Utility Energy Savings Contract (UESC) with a period of performance that shall not exceed 25 years
A 25-year period of performance is an exceptionally long duration. Over such a timeframe, facility usage at Fort Benning could change substantially — units could be realigned, buildings demolished or added, mission changes could alter energy demand — making the fixed-price assumption that scope and cost can be predicted at contract award highly questionable. Long-duration FFP contracts on complex performance-dependent programs are inherently difficult to price accurately upfront, and this creates risk of expensive modifications as conditions change.
energy management services
Energy management services under a UESC involve a utility company assessing current energy use, designing and installing ECMs (e.g., lighting, HVAC, controls, renewable energy), and then measuring and verifying (M&V) savings over the contract life. The M&V component — ongoing measurement of actual versus baseline energy consumption — is inherently variable and performance-dependent, which is in tension with FFP pricing. FFP implies a stable, pre-defined deliverable; M&V over 25 years is an ongoing, adaptive process tied to variable real-world outcomes.
