Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Minimal 0 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a simple sole-source commercial item procurement for a one-year software maintenance renewal (McCabe IQ Maintenance, M-IQ-SERVER) under simplified acquisition procedures. The requirements list contains a single line item: one lot of software maintenance for the period 07/01/2026–06/30/2027. There are no QA surveillance plans, no reporting requirements, no status report cadences, no prescribed meeting frequencies, and no deliverables tracking mechanisms of any kind described in the solicitation text or the attached requirements list. This is consistent with the nature of the acquisition — a commercial software maintenance subscription typically delivers value through continued access to updates, patches, and support, not through a performance-monitored service delivery model. There is no evidence of compliance-over-outcomes patterns because there is essentially no accountability structure at all beyond the contract terms. The absence of outcome metrics is unremarkable given the procurement type; a software maintenance renewal does not require the elaborate QA apparatus that this sin targets. The solicitation is minimal and appropriate for what it is buying. Severity is 1.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Severe 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation represents a severity 3 — the most severe form of insider rewards — but it is important to note that the mechanism here is not the typical stacking of experience requirements with dollar thresholds and lookback windows. Instead, the insider rewards problem manifests in its most extreme form: a formal sole source justification that eliminates competition entirely. The government has allowed a proprietary software dependency (McCabe IQ, a specific part-numbered product from a single vendor) to develop to the point where it now explicitly states no other vendor can serve the requirement. The justification for sole source — that switching would cost money and take nine months — is itself evidence of advanced vendor capture: the agency's institutional knowledge and operational capability are now inseparable from a single vendor's proprietary platform. A qualified software vendor with equivalent or superior capabilities cannot compete because the requirement is defined around the incumbent's specific product, not around the government's underlying mission need (code review and software testing). The combination of proprietary platform lock-in, sole source designation, OEM-controlled reseller authorization requirements, and the explicit acknowledgment that no alternative is feasible collectively represent the most complete possible expression of insider rewards: competition has not merely been tilted — it has been eliminated.
Evidence Quotes
The Naval Surface Warfare Center Dahlgren Division (NSWCDD) intends to award on a sole source basis for the following products manufactured by McCabe IQ Maintenance, Part Number: M-IQ-SERVER, manufactured by McCabe Software, Inc.
The solicitation is structured as a sole source award to a single named vendor — McCabe Software, Inc. This is the ultimate form of insider rewards: not merely tilting the playing field toward incumbents, but explicitly eliminating competition entirely. No other vendor can compete regardless of capability.
Awarding to another vendor would require changing to a new design and production code review platform. This would result in duplication of cost to the Government and take an additional nine (9) months in delays to critical programs.
The government's own justification for sole source acknowledges that switching vendors would be disruptive. This is a textbook description of vendor capture — the agency has become so dependent on the incumbent's proprietary platform that it cannot articulate requirements in vendor-neutral terms or transition to a competing solution without significant cost and delay.
The required software is exclusive to McCabe Software, Inc.
The government explicitly acknowledges that only one vendor can fulfill this requirement as specified. This reflects a procurement structure where proprietary lock-in has been allowed to develop to the point where competition is structurally impossible — the most severe form of insider rewards.
The software must be compatible in all aspects (form, fit and function) with existing software presently installed.
Requiring compatibility with already-installed proprietary software ensures that only the incumbent vendor (or authorized resellers of that vendor's product) can respond. This is a compatibility requirement that functions as a de facto sole source justification, foreclosing any possibility of competitive alternatives.
Award will be made on a Lowest Price Technically Acceptable basis... Quotes will only be accepted from authorized re-sellers who have provided a Letter of Authorization.
Even among potential resellers, the requirement for a Letter of Authorization from McCabe Software, Inc. means McCabe controls who can compete. This gives the OEM incumbent complete gatekeeping authority over the competitive pool, further entrenching the insider dynamic.
Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 0 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a sole-source RFQ for a single commercial software maintenance renewal — one line item (Part Number M-IQ-SERVER, McCabe IQ Maintenance, 1 LOT, July 2026–June 2027). The 'requirements list' attachment is a simple spreadsheet containing only the part number, description, quantity, unit of measure, and license period. There are no task descriptions, no workflow mandates, no staffing prescriptions, and no step-by-step process requirements. The solicitation body explains the sole-source rationale (proprietary software, form/fit/function compatibility, nine-month delay cost if switching) and standard commercial acquisition terms. Because this is a software maintenance renewal — not a services contract — the question of 'how' versus 'what' is largely inapplicable: the vendor (McCabe Software, Inc.) delivers maintenance for its own proprietary product, and the government is simply renewing a license. There is nothing in the solicitation that dictates how the vendor should perform its maintenance activities. The only specificity present is the part number and license period, which are legitimate identification constraints for a commercial product purchase, not prescriptive process requirements. Requirement sprawl is not a meaningful concern here, and no evidence of it exists in the solicitation text.
Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Minimal 0 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a straightforward sole-source combined synopsis/solicitation for a single commercial software maintenance renewal (McCabe IQ Maintenance, Part Number M-IQ-SERVER, one lot, covering July 2026 through June 2027). The document is 24 pages total across two attachments, one of which is simply a one-row requirements list and the other the standard SF1449 form. The solicitation contains no 'Applicable Documents' section, no bulk regulatory citation list, and no undifferentiated compliance framework dump. Regulatory references are minimal and clearly relevant: FAR Part 12 (commercial items acquisition), FAR Part 5 (publicizing requirements), FAR 6.103-1(b) (sole source justification), FAR 19.104-1 (small business set-aside consideration), DFARS 252.225-7013 (Duty-Free Entry, noted as applicable where relevant), and the Trade Agreements Act/Buy American requirement — all standard, appropriate citations for a simplified acquisition of this type. There is no indication of copy-forward accumulation, no tangential or contradictory regulations, and no compliance burden that a vendor could not readily understand. The solicitation is narrow in scope (one SKU, one year, one vendor) and its regulatory footprint matches that scope appropriately. This is a textbook simplified acquisition with no rule pool characteristics. Severity is assessed at 1 — minimal.
Square Peg

Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is for a renewal of a commercial software maintenance subscription (McCabe IQ Maintenance, Part Number M-IQ-SERVER) covering a fixed one-year period (07/01/2026 – 06/30/2027). The contract type is firm-fixed-price. This is a textbook appropriate use of FFP: the deliverable is a defined commercial product/service, the quantity is fixed (1 LOT), the license period is precisely bounded, and the vendor (McCabe Software, Inc.) offers published pricing for a known product. There is no ambiguity about scope, no variable labor component, and no reason to expect cost or schedule uncertainty. FFP is the standard and correct vehicle for commercial software license and maintenance renewals. There is no mismatch between the contract type and the requirements. Severity is 1 — Minimal.
