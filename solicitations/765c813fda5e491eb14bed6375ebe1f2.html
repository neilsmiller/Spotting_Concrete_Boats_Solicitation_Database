Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation uses personal services contracts (PSCs) with a negotiated hourly rate structure, which is functionally equivalent to a labor-hour contract. This pricing approach is well-suited to the requirements. The work involves providing flexible, expert technical assistance to emerging market governments on infrastructure finance and P3 development — tasks that are inherently advisory, scope-variable, and demand-driven by host country needs. Resident assignments are for roughly one year with likely renewal, and intermittent assignments vary in duration, both of which make fixed-price impractical: there is no well-defined deliverable, the pace and direction of work is set in part by the host government, and the quantity of labor is genuinely unpredictable. A fixed-price contract would be inappropriate here because outputs cannot be specified with enough precision to price competitively. A cost-reimbursement structure would be unusual for individual PSC advisors and would add unnecessary administrative complexity without meaningful benefit. The labor-hour/PSC approach is the standard vehicle Treasury OTA uses for this type of capacity-building advisory work and is well-justified by the nature of the requirements. There is no meaningful square peg concern here.
