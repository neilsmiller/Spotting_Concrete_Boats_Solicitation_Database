Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation presents a strong fit between the FFP contract type and the nature of the requirement. The work involves providing a fixed number of qualified stationary engineers (six) to operate and maintain specific, named equipment — four Cleaver Brooks boilers and three Carrier chillers — on a defined rotating shift schedule covering all hours of the day, week, and year. The scope is stable and well-defined: the equipment is enumerated, the staffing headcount is fixed, the shift structure is described, and the location is a single campus. These characteristics — predictable labor quantity, known equipment, recurring operations — are precisely the conditions under which FAR recommends FFP contracts. The government knows how many people it needs, what skills they require, and what shifts must be covered. Vendors can accurately price this work based on prevailing wage rates (likely governed by the Service Contract Act given NAICS 561210), benefits, overhead, and profit. There is no significant scope uncertainty that would drive expensive modifications. A T&M contract would be inappropriate here because the labor quantity is not unpredictable — the government is essentially buying a fixed staffing complement, not purchasing expertise on an as-needed basis. A cost-reimbursement contract would be inappropriate because this is not a complex research or development effort with uncertain costs. FFP is the correct tool for this requirement, and the solicitation's choice reflects sound procurement judgment.
