Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Severe 4 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation represents the most severe manifestation of Insider Rewards (Sin #4). The agency has not merely tilted the playing field toward an incumbent — it has formally declared sole-source intent and then structured the nominal competition provisions (required by FAR 5.207 and similar regulations) in ways that ensure no challenger can succeed. The compatibility requirement is particularly egregious: it requires challengers to certify integration with HSQ's proprietary software and hardware, which means the evaluation standard is literally defined by the incumbent's product. A qualified SCADA vendor with a superior, modern platform would be deemed nonconforming simply for offering their own solution. This is vendor capture rendered explicit and codified in solicitation language. The agency has lost the ability to articulate requirements in vendor-neutral terms — they have defined their requirement as 'what HSQ provides,' not as 'a SCADA system that achieves these functional outcomes.' The 7-day response window, withheld solicitation, and sole-source justification all stack to confirm this is not a genuine competition. Severity is 3 — the most severe — because the insider reward is not a structural tilt but a categorical exclusion of all non-incumbents.
Evidence Quotes
Whiteman AFB intends to award a sole source contract to HSQ Technology (CAGE 3S480) as authorized by RFO 6.103-1, 'Only one responsible source and no other supplies or services will satisfy agency requirements.'
The agency has formally declared that only one vendor — HSQ Technology — can satisfy its requirements. This is the most extreme form of insider reward: the incumbent is not merely advantaged, they are the only eligible source. No other vendor, regardless of capability, can compete on equal footing.
An interested party must certify with the submission of its offer that its proposed services and products integrate seamlessly with HSQ's software and hardware.
This requirement embeds the incumbent's proprietary systems as the evaluation standard. Any competitor must certify compatibility with HSQ's own software and hardware — a requirement that only HSQ itself can satisfy with certainty, and that competitors can only meet if they have reverse-engineered or are otherwise subordinate to HSQ's platform. It is not a capability test; it is a loyalty oath to the incumbent's product stack.
An offer whose services and products lack compatibility with Whiteman AFB's existing SCADA system will be deemed nonconforming; thus, ineligible for award.
The agency has defined 'nonconforming' in terms of compatibility with the incumbent's proprietary system, not in terms of functional capability or outcome delivery. This converts a technical evaluation criterion into a hard gate that eliminates any vendor proposing a different — potentially superior — SCADA platform. Institutional lock-in is written directly into the eligibility rules.
The solicitation will not be made available through the GPE. In order to obtain the solicitation, a responsible source must email the primary point of contact identified in this notice No Later Than 08 May 2026, 11:00AM Central Time.
Withholding the solicitation from public posting and requiring direct contact within a 7-day window further suppresses competition. Combined with the compatibility requirement, this structure ensures that potential challengers face informational and procedural barriers on top of technical ones, reinforcing incumbent advantage at every layer.
