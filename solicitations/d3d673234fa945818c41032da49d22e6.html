Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation is a simple product purchase — a 'Cyber OPFOR Kit' consisting of a specific list of commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) hardware, software tools, and training subscriptions for what appears to be a cybersecurity opposing force training capability. The concept of 'requirement sprawl' as defined — dictating HOW work should be done rather than WHAT outcome is needed — applies primarily to services contracts where process prescription crowds out vendor innovation. This is a supply/product procurement with no service delivery component described. There is no statement of work, no task ordering, no staffing prescription, and no workflow methodology being mandated. The solicitation is essentially a bill of materials. Specifying exact products in a kit procurement is qualitatively different from sprawl in a services context: the 'what' and the 'how' collapse into the same thing when you are buying specific physical items. The most defensible concern is whether brand-specific specifications for items like the laptop preclude technically equivalent alternatives, but this is a mild and common feature of equipment procurement, not the organizational dysfunction that produces true requirement sprawl. Overall, this solicitation is too narrow in scope and too product-focused to exhibit meaningful requirement sprawl. Severity is rated 1 (minimal).
