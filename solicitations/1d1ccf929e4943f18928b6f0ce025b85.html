Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Severe 3 quotes ▾
Reasoning
This solicitation represents the most severe form of insider rewards (Severity 3): it is an explicit sole-source procurement directed at a single named vendor for a proprietary software product. There is no competition whatsoever. The requirement is written around a trademarked, proprietary product — RSMeans Costworks — that only R.S. Means Company LLC can supply. No qualified alternative vendor, regardless of how capable or cost-effective, could fulfill the requirement as written because the requirement is defined by the incumbent's product name rather than by the underlying capability (cost estimating for electrical, plumbing, road, and mechanical projects). The statutory notice to potential alternative vendors is directed entirely at future procurements, not the current one. While sole-source awards for proprietary software renewals are common and often legally justified under FAR 6.302-1 (only one responsible source), this solicitation exemplifies the structural dynamic that defines insider rewards at its most complete: the incumbent vendor holds exclusive rights to the product, the agency has built a workflow dependency on that product, and competition has been entirely foreclosed. The agency's dependence on a proprietary tool — rather than on a vendor-neutral capability — is itself a consequence of prior procurement decisions that did not adequately protect the government's ability to re-compete. Whether this sole-source is legally defensible does not change the analytic finding: a qualified vendor new to this contract has zero realistic path to compete.
