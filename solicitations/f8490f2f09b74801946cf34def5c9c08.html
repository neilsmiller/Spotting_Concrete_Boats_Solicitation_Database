Sin Analysis

Compliance Over Outcomes
Does the contract measure process compliance rather than actual outcomes?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The Alliant 3 GWAC solicitation shows moderate compliance-over-outcomes characteristics, though not severe. The evaluation architecture in the Self-Scoring Worksheet (J.P-16) is heavily oriented toward verifiable credentials and process certifications — accounting system audits, CMMI levels, ISO certifications, facility clearances — each assigned large point values. These measure whether an offeror has invested in process infrastructure, not whether that infrastructure translates to successful IT delivery. The C-SCRM plan template reinforces this pattern by structuring security accountability around document maintenance cycles rather than risk reduction outcomes. The subcontracting plan evaluation similarly asks whether FAR elements are present rather than whether goals are meaningful. However, the solicitation stops short of severity 3 for several reasons. First, this is an IDIQ vehicle solicitation, not a task order SOW — the primary purpose is to establish a qualified pool, and credential-based screening is more defensible at the IDIQ level than at the task order level. Second, the available text does not show the kind of overlapping multi-cadence reporting burden (weekly + monthly + quarterly reports with prescribed section formats) that characterizes severe compliance theater — the reporting requirements visible in the provided attachments are relatively contained. Third, past performance ratings are included as a scored element (up to 2,500 points per project), which is at least nominally outcome-oriented. The absence of the full RFP body text (particularly any QA Surveillance Plan, Section G performance management provisions beyond what appears in the amendment, or task order SOW) limits confidence in a higher severity finding. Based on the available evidence, severity 2 is appropriate: compliance mechanisms are noticeable and structurally dominant in the evaluation scoring, but some outcome-oriented elements exist and the reporting burden observable in the provided text is not yet the primary accountability mechanism.

This is credential-based compliance scoring, not outcome measurement.
Insider Rewards
Do requirements or criteria favor incumbent or well-connected contractors?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
Alliant 3 is a GWAC on-ramp, not a single-agency incumbent capture situation, and the solicitation does include meaningful mitigations: it accepts up to seven experience projects across a broad NAICS code table, allows commercial projects for Emerging Technology experience, permits Contractor Teaming Arrangements to aggregate experience, and awards are intended for 76 firms out of 133 proposals received — indicating the field is genuinely competitive. The solicitation explicitly allows subcontractor experience and CTA member experience to count, which meaningfully broadens access. However, several stacked scoring elements structurally favor vendors who have already held large federal contracts, particularly Alliant 2 or similar GWACs: (1) bonus points for project size at $35M+ and $100M+ thresholds; (2) up to 4,000 bonus points for cost-reimbursement contract experience, which is predominantly a federal phenomenon; (3) up to 7,700 points for DCMA/DCAA-audited business systems that only emerge from sustained prior federal contracting; (4) up to 3,500 points for existing facility clearances; and (5) up to 7,000 points for the Organizational Risk Assessment rewarding prior co-performance in the proposed team arrangement. Individually, each element has a rationale. Cumulatively, a vendor with an established federal IT presence — particularly one currently on Alliant 2 — can realistically score 15,000–20,000 more points than an equally capable vendor whose experience is primarily commercial or who has not yet assembled a team with a shared performance history. This tilts the playing field meaningfully toward incumbents without fully closing competition, which is consistent with a severity 2 finding: moderate insider reward with meaningful but incomplete opportunity for non-incumbents to compete.

For CTAs, this rewards teams that have already worked together on prior federal contracts — directly advantaging existing teaming arrangements that have already been performing on Alliant 2 or similar vehicles. Requirement Sprawl
Does the scope dictate HOW work is done rather than WHAT must be achieved?
Minimal 2 quotes ▾
Reasoning
Alliant 3 is a Governmentwide Acquisition Contract (GWAC) vehicle — a multiple-award IDIQ contract at the MAC vehicle level, not a task-order-level requirement. This fundamentally shapes the appropriate analysis. The solicitation is not specifying how IT services will be delivered on any particular task; it is qualifying contractors for a vehicle off which individual task orders will later be competed. In this context, the 'requirements' are almost entirely about demonstrating past qualifications, organizational systems, and certifications — not about prescribing delivery methods for future work. The scoring criteria (self-scoring worksheet J.P-16) evaluate relevant experience projects, past performance ratings, accounting system adequacy, industry certifications, facility clearances, and organizational risk. These are qualification-based criteria, not operational process mandates. The C-SCRM Plan template (J.P-13) requires contractors to document cybersecurity supply chain risk management practices using NIST frameworks — a legitimate federal security baseline, not arbitrary process prescription. The subcontracting plan template (J.P-9) reflects statutory requirements under FAR 52.219-9, not agency-invented process constraints. The 13 amendments across the solicitation period, while voluminous, primarily address proposal submission mechanics, scoring clarifications, and administrative changes rather than expanding operational process mandates. The relevant experience templates (J.P-2, J.P-3) define what must be documented about past projects without prescribing how future work will be performed. This solicitation does not exhibit meaningful requirement sprawl at the vehicle-qualification stage. The appropriate place to assess sprawl for Alliant 3 would be the individual task order solicitations issued under the contract, not the vehicle establishment itself. Severity 1 is appropriate.

Rule Pools
Does the contract bury contractors in undifferentiated regulatory citations?
Moderate 5 quotes ▾
Reasoning
The Alliant 3 GWAC solicitation exhibits moderate rule pool characteristics, warranting a severity 2 rating. The evidence does not rise to severity 3 because the solicitation lacks the hallmark features of the worst cases: there is no single dedicated 'Applicable Documents' list of 88+ uncurated regulations, and the core solicitation structure — a self-scoring GWAC qualification vehicle — is actually quite transparent about what is being evaluated and why. The regulatory citations are mostly plausibly relevant to the subject matter. However, several genuine rule pool indicators are present. First, the C-SCRM Plan Template (J.P-13) presents a comprehensive NIST control list and an open-ended multi-category regulatory appendix with 'include, but are not limited to' language — a structure that structurally encourages accumulation rather than curation. For a GWAC that will serve agencies with wildly different IT environments, this undifferentiated control list creates interpretation burden without agency-provided prioritization. Second, and most tellingly, the solicitation originally included climate risk management and GHG reporting requirements for what is fundamentally an IT services vehicle — requirements that persisted through six amendments and were only removed after executive orders mandated their removal. This is textbook copy-forward accumulation: regulatory requirements added without curating applicability, sustained until external pressure removed them. Third, the subcontracting plan template's dense citation of multiple overlapping FAR clauses, GSAM provisions, and SBA regulations without synthesis is consistent with passive regulatory management. Mitigating factors include: the solicitation's self-scoring structure is unusually transparent; the core evaluation criteria (relevant experience, past performance, systems/certifications) are well-explained with specific thresholds; standard FAR clauses are incorporated by reference rather than restated; and the multiple amendments show genuine responsiveness to industry feedback. The rule pool problem is real but contained — most pronounced in the C-SCRM and sustainability compliance areas rather than pervasive throughout the solicitation.

Square Peg
Is the pricing/contract type mismatched to the nature of the work?
Minimal 3 quotes ▾
Reasoning
Alliant 3 is a governmentwide IDIQ GWAC vehicle, not a single-award contract with a fixed pricing structure. The solicitation establishes the master vehicle and awardee pool; individual task orders placed by federal agencies will carry their own contract types (FFP, T&M, cost-reimbursement, etc.) depending on the specific requirements of each order. This is the correct and well-established approach for broad IT services GWACs — the vehicle itself should not and does not mandate a single pricing type. The presence of a labor rate attestation template is consistent with supporting T&M task orders, while the structure also accommodates FFP orders for well-defined deliverables. There is no square peg here: the mismatch this sin describes occurs when a specific requirement is paired with an incompatible pricing type, but Alliant 3 deliberately and appropriately leaves pricing flexibility to the ordering level. The relevant experience scoring includes cost-reimbursement experience as a separate point category (L.5.2.3.5), indicating the vehicle anticipates all major contract types. No severity concern is warranted. The document set provided does not include the full RFP Section B (contract line items) which would show if any specific pricing type is mandated at the GWAC level in a way that would be problematic, but nothing in the available text suggests such a mandate exists. Severity 1 is appropriate.
